# Charter — what we are building and why

Owner's words, recorded 2026-09-16. Everything else in this repo serves this.

## The goal

Translate every previously untranslated work, oldest first, moving forward
through the Middle Ages and beyond. That is the first run: the full corpus,
in chronological order, no favorites skipping the line.

## Accuracy and rights

Every translation is locked to the original language — Greek, Latin, Aramaic,
whatever the work was written in. English translations may be consulted as
references and help, but the rendering must be new, made from the source
text, so the result is non-copyright and free for anyone to use. Never mass
copy someone else's translation. If a passage cannot be honestly rendered
from the locked source, say so in the open (lacuna notes, honest caveats)
rather than inventing it.

## Navigation

Every translation carries its references with it, and they must work in both
places the reader meets the text: the website and Logos Personal Books.
A Bible link asserts the author invokes the passage
(see `docs/LOGOS_MARKUP.md`); references a reader cannot follow are clutter.

## The website

Beautiful, easy to navigate, and consistent: every translation, every
workflow, every page looks the same and works the same. Nothing fragmented.
English is the primary title for everything; the original-language title
rides along as a subtitle so readers who know Greek or Latin can still
search and find. The site is built for a non-technical reader first, while
keeping full scholarly credibility underneath: locked sources, edition
statements, honest scope notes, and verifiable claims on every page.

## Seeing thought move through time

Beyond reading single works, the site must show how each author views each
major topic, and let readers compare authors against each other. A reader
should be able to type in a topic and see the evolution of thought: who
believed what, when they lived, and how the positions relate. Timelines are
the natural spine; distributions and comparisons (bars, shares, flows) are
welcome where they clarify. Think it through from the reader's question
backward; do not decorate first.

## Standing tensions, resolved

- Scope is everything, but the standard never drops to meet a deadline.
  The gates (tip-ready, verify, PBB guards, review) are how scope and
  standard coexist.
- A passage with no Scripture honestly has no Bible links
  (tip exemption); the book waits rather than padding.
- Free compute is supervised compute (`docs/SUPERVISED_COMPUTE.md`).
