## Analysis (where each error came from, and what to change)


**Bottom line:** 14 of the 23 major errors (61%) were not missed by the checkers. The pipeline made them itself (repair, read-fix, polish), or the second checker voted down a correct flag. You can remove most of them with process changes that also cut Cloudflare spend. The other 9 majors (8 distinct) are hard grammar misreadings that the drafter and both checkers missed.

This was a read-only audit. I changed no repo files; the scripts I used are in the scratchpad (`attr.py`, `errs.tsv`). I traced each error through `outputs/work-pipeline/<slug>/sections/<sec>.json` (repairs, edits, `checks.last_rejected`, refereed findings, hold files) and `run.log`. Every error is in both the staged and the live English, so nothing was edited after the receipt.

How the code produces these:
- **Single-checker findings are trusted too easily.** 16 of the 17 errors in row A came from findings raised by one model and "upheld" by the other. The judge in `check_section` (`ADJ_SYS`) sees only the claim. A missing ruling also counts as upheld (`work_pipeline.py:792`).
- **The repair model's own doubts are ignored.** In Clement 20 the repair model wrote that ἀσαφῶς means "obscurely", then applied "clearly" anyway. Across the corpus, 669 of 7,803 repairs with edits carry this kind of doubt in their "fixed" notes, and the edits are still applied (`repair_attempt`).
- **Read-fix and polish keep any edit whose fresh check finds no more problems than before** (`n_new > n_old`, lines 1252 and 1297). The fresh check is a new random sample, so the comparison is mostly noise:
  - 2,249 of 3,553 accepted edits started from already-passed text that drew at least one confirmed problem when checked again.
  - 1,655 accepted edits left confirmed problems open.
  - So "every section passed" is true for one sample only. It cannot be reproduced.
- **gpt-oss-120b was involved in 13 of the 24 errors in rows A and D**, either raising the false finding or rejecting the true one. It is still the last checker of record for 184 sections in 31 certified works.
- **Minor findings are dropped** before they are agreed, judged or stored (`check_section`, lines 762–766). That hides the five lower-case divine titles and most of the garbled-English errors.
- **Readers' term-drift and Scripture notes go straight to repair**, though readers cannot see the source.

### 1. Are we at a defensible quality level?
Not for the word "certified". The current rate is 1.21 majors per 1,000 words (95% range about 0.77–1.81), or about one meaning-changing error every 800 words. That is fine for a disclosed "AI-assisted reading translation". It is not fine for a page that implies verified accuracy.

Suggested target: **at most 0.5 majors per 1,000 words, measured as the 95% upper bound, and no unforced negation or agency reversals.**
- Removing rows A–D alone would leave about 9 majors in 19k words, roughly 0.47 per 1,000.
- A stretch goal of 0.25 per 1,000 needs a stronger final check on the hard-grammar cases in row E.

How to measure it continuously:
- Each week, draw a random sample of about 10k words (20k for a tighter range) from newly certified works and have it audited by the same independent protocol used for this audit. Publish the rate and its Poisson upper bound for the rolling window.
- Add these 94 errors, with the correct readings, to `scripts/defect_bench.py` as a set of real errors. Score any model or prompt change against it for recall and false positives before switching.

### 2. Changes that catch the most for the least spend
1. **Check every edit against the version it replaces, using only the changed sentence.** This applies to check-repair, read-fix and polish. The judge sees the source sentence plus the old and new English in random order, picks the more faithful one or says they are equal, and the edit is kept only if the new one is at least as good. Use the third model family (Nemotron on NVIDIA). Also make `REPAIR_SYS` return a true/false verdict per problem, and drop edits for problems it calls not real.
   - Expected catch: rows A and C plus part of B, about 31 errors and 10 majors.
   - Cost: it replaces the full two-model re-check that read-fix and polish run now. That is 6,198 attempts, each with at least two section-length checks plus judgments, about as much checking as the whole section stage (5,963 check rounds). This should be a net saving.
2. **Replace "count did not rise" with matching the actual findings.** After a repair, ask "is finding X fixed?" about the edited span instead of drawing a new blind sample. Certify only when no finding agreed by both families is open, and remove the `n_new <= n_old` acceptance. Rounds 2–4 are currently 1.9 times the volume of round 1, so targeted checks shrink them too. This catches row B and stops sections shipping with confirmed problems open.
3. **Add free deterministic checks to `section_gate` / `work_lint.py`:**
   - If the source marks a lacuna or "…", the English must keep a […] marker, and no edit may remove it.
   - A cast name in the English that does not appear in that section's source is flagged.
   - The cited verse range must match the quoted words (using `kjv_lookup.py`).
   - Lower-case father/savior/king/child/lord/word near God, Christ or Lord is listed for one cheap model to decide.

   Expected catch: about 12 errors, including 2 majors (Joshua and Jacob).
4. **Check readers' notes against the source before they become edits.** Send term-drift and Scripture notes to one source-reading checker first. Term drift is skipped when the variants come from different source words. The author's own wording of a Scripture quotation is never "corrected" to a standard Bible. This removes most of row C and the repair calls those notes now trigger.
5. **Drop gpt-oss-120b from every role. Keep minor findings that both checkers agree on and repair them** through change 1. Add a check that compares Pass A with Pass B clause by clause, which would have caught Pseudo-Cyprian 6, where Pass A is right but Pass B lost the condition.
   - Cost: small.
   - Limits: the misreadings in Tertullian 6, Minucius 14 and Eustathius come from Pass A itself. They need the stronger final check from section 1, run only on the sample.

**Spend:** I cannot split the $350/day by stage, because `call()` does not tag stages. Add stage and token counts to `call()` and report them in `scripts/throughput.py` before making cuts. Also:
- Polish failed 893 of 2,202 times. Each attempt costs a full DeepSeek rewrite plus two checkers.
- Limit polish to the flagged sentences and to garbled or speaker notes.

### 3. Do the certified works need a re-check?
Yes, but in targeted steps, not a full re-run. Scope: about 110 receipts and about 129k staged English words. Of 586 staged sections, 458 contain applied repair edits, 131 were polished, and 184 were last checked with gpt-oss.

1. **Run the deterministic checks from change 3 over every certified work.** No model spend.
2. **Judge every stored edit after the fact.** `repairs[].edits` keeps the old and new text: 2,270 edits in certified works, averaging 142 characters. Run the change-1 comparison on each (about 2.3k short calls) and restore the old text where it wins. This would have fixed most of rows A and C here.
3. **Run a full re-check with Kimi + GLM under the new rules** only on sections that were last checked with gpt-oss, that were polished (polish keeps no old text), or that `run.log` shows "accepted (k -> k>0)".
4. **Measure again** on a fresh sample of other works to get a before-and-after number. Separately, correct the 94 known errors in these 13 works.

### tertullian-on-patience
- 7 [major/meaning] "sins against his neighbor and so against God" <- de proximo in deum peccat: 'de proximo' is an adverbial phrase meaning 'directly, at close range'. It does not mean 'against his neighbor'. Tertullian says that impatience over loss sins directly against God. The English adds a sin against the neighbor that is not in the text. | fix: sins directly against God (found_by both)
- 7 [major/agency_reference] "So the casting away of riches has supplied beforehand a distaste for wealth" <- ita detrimentorum patientiae fastidium opulentiae praeministrauit, demonstrans per abiectionem diuitiarum: The subject is the Lord, carried over from the previous sentence. 'per abiectionem diuitiarum' gives the means, so it cannot be the subject. The dative 'detrimentorum patientiae' (for patience under losses) is dropped. The real point, that by scorning wealth the Lord prepared us for patience under loss, is lost. | fix: So by his distaste for wealth the Lord prepared us beforehand for patience under losses, showing by his rejection of riches (found_by both)
- 8 [minor/addition] "we take up patience of that wrong as the highest thing" <- eiusque iniuriae patientiam summus [= subimus]: The garbled 'summus' stands for 'subimus' (we submit to, we undergo). It is not the adjective 'highest'. 'As the highest thing' is an idea the text does not contain. | fix: and we submit to bearing that wrong patiently (found_by both)
- 8 [minor/agency_reference] "If I hear some shameless or wicked word spoken against me with too little calm" <- si parum aequanimiter audiam dictum aliquod in me proteruum aut nequam: 'parum aequanimiter' modifies 'audiam'. It is the hearer who lacks calm. Placed after 'spoken against me', the phrase reads as describing how the insult was spoken. | fix: If I hear with too little calm some shameless or wicked word spoken against me (found_by A)
- 9 [minor/scripture_ref] "I long, the apostle says, to depart and be with Christ" <- cupio, inquit apostolus, recipi iam et esse cum domino: The English gives the standard wording of Philippians 1:23, not Tertullian's own citation, which reads 'to be received now and to be with the Lord'. The meaning is close, but the quoted words are not his. | fix: I long, the apostle says, to be received now and to be with the Lord (found_by both)
- 6 [minor/divine_names] "You see what a father patience acquires for us." <- uides, quem nobis patrem patientia adquirat.: 'patrem' means God the Father, picking up 'your Father in heaven' just before. House style capitalizes it. | fix: You see what a Father patience acquires for us. (found_by B)
- 6 [major/meaning] "For God, who had counted him as righteous, knew how heavy the command was, and that it was not pleasing to him to have it carried out; yet Abraham heard it patiently" <- ceterum deus quem iustitiae deputasset sciebat, tam graue praeceptum, quod nec domino perfici placebat, patienter et audiuit: Misparsed. The English says God knew how heavy the command was, which the source does not say. It also drops 'nec' (not even), and 'pleasing to him' is unclear about who is meant. | fix: (God knew the man He had counted righteous.) - 7 [minor/garbled_english] "We will perish with what is lost. Here we find where we have to lose." <- peribimus cum perdito qui hic inuenimus ubi habemus amittere.: 'qui ... inuenimus' is a relative clause on the subject of 'peribimus'. Splitting it off produces an English sentence that has no clear meaning. | fix: We shall perish along with what we lost, we who find here something we must lose. (found_by B)
