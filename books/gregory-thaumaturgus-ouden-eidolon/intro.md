Gregory Thaumaturgus (c. 213–c. 270) was a Christian bishop of Neocaesarea in Pontus. He studied under Origen in Caesarea and later devoted himself to Christianizing his largely pagan home region. This short treatise is one of his surviving theological writings, preserved in an incomplete state.

The work addresses the difference between honoring the God who truly exists and honoring a god who does not exist.

A reader will meet two kinds of honor. Honor directed toward the God who truly exists is itself an honor to the one who gives it. Honor directed toward a god who does not exist becomes, in reverse, dishonor to the one who gives it. The key terms are honor, dishonor, and a god who does not exist.
