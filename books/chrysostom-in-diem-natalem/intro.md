John Chrysostom (c. 349–407) preached this homily to the congregation in Antioch on the feast of Christ's Nativity. The feast had only recently been introduced at Antioch, and a large crowd had gathered. Chrysostom defends the December date of the celebration and prepares the people for worthy reception of Communion.

Chrysostom argues that the feast is rightly celebrated in the present month. He reasons from the rapid spread of the feast, from the Gospel census under Augustus, and from the timing of Zechariah's service in the Holy of Holies that Christ was conceived when Elizabeth was in her sixth month and born nine months later. He then answers pagan and Manichaean mockery of the Incarnation by showing that God is not defiled by dwelling in pure flesh, any more than the sun is defiled when its rays touch filth. Finally, he exhorts the congregation to approach the Eucharist with fear, purity, and orderly reverence.

A reader will meet Mary, Joseph, Elizabeth, Zechariah, John the Baptist, the angel Gabriel, Augustus, Quirinius, Herod, Paul, Moses, Aaron, Gamaliel, Nathanael, Philip, and Nicodemus. Chrysostom quotes Paul, Moses, the Evangelist, and Gabriel, and he paraphrases objections from pagan and Manichaean opponents and from an unnamed interlocutor. Key terms include the plan of salvation for God's saving arrangement in the Incarnation, the Holy of Holies for the innermost sanctuary, the veil for the curtain separating it, the Feast of Tabernacles for the Jewish festival when the high priest entered that sanctuary, the priestly division for the order of service to which Zechariah belonged, the Sun of Righteousness as a title for Christ, condescension for God's becoming incarnate, the temple of God for the believer or body as God's dwelling, the sacred mystery for the Eucharist, and the sacrifice for the Eucharistic offering.
