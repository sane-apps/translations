Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote these brief comments on verses from the Psalms. He was bishop of Alexandria from 412 until his death in 444. His full commentary on the Psalms no longer survives, but portions remain as fragments preserved in later collections of excerpts.

The fragments address two Psalm verses. On Psalm 1:5, Cyril explains how the resurrection of sinners differs from that of the righteous. On Psalm 2:7, he explains how the verse reveals both the eternal Sonship of Christ and his birth in the flesh from the Virgin.

A reader will meet Cyril himself as interpreter, along with Christ and the Father, whose words from Scripture are quoted. Cyril argues that sinners will rise again but will not share the assembly of the righteous. He insists that the Son is eternally begotten from the Father's essence, that 'today I have begotten you' refers to the incarnation, and that the incarnate Word remains the Father's own Son by nature. Key terms include 'assembly of the righteous,' 'plan of salvation,' 'flesh,' 'Mother of God,' 'eternal,' 'begotten,' 'Son by nature,' 'incarnation,' 'faith,' and 'Lord.' Psalm numbers follow the Septuagint. The source also quotes a blessing about inheriting the kingdom, likely from Matthew 25:34, but does not name the book.
