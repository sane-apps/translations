Tertullian (c. 155–c. 220), a Christian teacher in Carthage, wrote this treatise to fellow Christians whom he addresses as brothers. He wrote because heretical teachers were using Scripture to draw believers away from the faith, and he wanted to show that such teachers should not be allowed to appeal to Scripture at all, since the Scriptures belong to the apostolic churches.

The occasion is the spread of heresies in the early church. Tertullian argues that heresies were predicted by Christ and the apostles, so their existence should not disturb believers; they serve to test and reveal the faithful. He insists that the command Seek and you will find was given to the Jews before Christ was known, and that once the rule of faith has been received, there is nothing further to seek. Heretics have no right to argue from Scripture because the Scriptures belong to the churches founded by the apostles, from which the true rule of faith has been handed down.

A reader will meet heretical teachers such as Marcion, Valentinus, and Apelles, whose words are quoted and argued against, as well as the apostles Peter, Paul, and John, and bishops such as Clement of Rome and Polycarp of Smyrna. Key terms include heresies, rule of faith, legal objection, Scriptures, tradition, apostolic churches, truth, falsehood, curiosity, philosophy, discipline, deposit, and the Holy Spirit. The work uses old Bible book names such as the first of Kingdoms for 1 Samuel and Jesus son of Nave for Joshua, and Psalm numbering follows the Septuagint.
