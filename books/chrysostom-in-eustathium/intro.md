John Chrysostom (c. 349–407) delivered this encomium to the congregation at Antioch. He spoke on a festal occasion when the Antiochene church annually honored the memory of Eustathius, the city's former bishop, whose body lay buried in Thrace. Chrysostom's purpose was to show that Eustathius deserved the honor of a martyr even though he died in exile rather than by violence.

The occasion was a public celebration in Antioch, where the church gathered to remember Eustathius. Chrysostom presents him as a vigilant shepherd who defended the church of Antioch against the Arian heresy, was expelled by his enemies, and continued to care for his flock even from exile. A reader will meet Abraham, Paul, Stephen, Moses, Aaron, Solomon, and Meletius, whose words and examples Chrysostom uses to support his argument.

Chrysostom argues that martyrdom is determined by intention and readiness, not only by the manner of death. He quotes Solomon from Ecclesiastes, Paul from his epistles and Acts, God from Genesis, Moses from Exodus, and the Lord speaking to Paul. 'Exile' is the specific suffering Eustathius endured for Christ. 'Wolves' refers to heretics who threaten the flock of the church. 'Flock' is the congregation entrusted to a bishop's care. 'Heresy' is the Arian heresy spreading from Egypt that Eustathius opposed. 'Paganism' is the earlier external enemy of the church, contrasted with the internal threat of heresy. 'Messenger of Satan' is Paul's phrase for the human opponents and persecutors who afflicted him, understood as agents of Satan. 'Thorn in the flesh' is Paul's phrase interpreted by Chrysostom as the persecutions and opponents that afflicted Paul's body but could not touch his soul. 'Virtuous life' is the disciplined, virtuous way of life proper to Christians, especially in enduring suffering. 'Luxury' is self-indulgence, which Chrysostom contrasts with the life of virtue and martyrdom. 'Crown' is the crown of martyrdom or victory awarded by God to those who endure for Christ.
