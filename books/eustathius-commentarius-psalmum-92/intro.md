Eustathius of Antioch (c. 320–337) wrote this commentary on Psalm 92. He was bishop of Antioch and a leading opponent of Arianism at the Council of Nicaea. The surviving fragments explain how the psalm's language about an anointed king applies to Christ, distinguishing what belongs to his divine nature from what belongs to his assumed human nature.

The commentary addresses the relationship between the one who anoints and the one anointed. Eustathius argues that the one who anoints is God by nature, begotten from God, while the anointed one has received acquired virtue and is adorned by the indwelling divinity. He then turns to Isaiah's description of the suffering servant to show that the dishonor and suffering belong to the man, not to the divine nature.

The indwelling God was not led to death like a lamb or slaughtered like a sheep, since he is invisible by nature. The man described by Isaiah, after his insults, appeared without form or beauty, but then through change was clothed again with splendor. The author quotes Isaiah 53:2-3 according to the Septuagint. Psalm numbering follows the Septuagint, so Psalm 92 corresponds to Psalm 93 in modern Hebrew numbering.
