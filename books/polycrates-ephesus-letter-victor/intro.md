Polycrates, bishop of Ephesus, wrote this letter to Victor, bishop of Rome, and the Roman church. He wrote on behalf of the bishops of Asia Minor to defend their practice of observing Pascha on the fourteenth day of the month. Victor had demanded that the Asian churches abandon this custom and conform to the Roman way of celebrating.

Polycrates argues that the Asian churches keep the day without tampering, following the gospel and the rule of faith. He appeals to the great figures buried in Asia who all kept the fourteenth day: Philip the apostle, John the disciple, Polycarp, Thraseas, Sagaris, Papirius, and Melito. He adds his own authority as the eighth bishop in his family and as one who has studied all holy scripture. He declares that he is not frightened by threats, since one must obey God rather than men.

A reader should know that the fourteenth day refers to the date of the Jewish Passover, when leaven was removed from the house. The priestly plate was the golden plate worn by the Jewish high priest, and Polycrates says John wore it, marking him as a priestly figure. The rule of faith means the standard teaching handed down in the churches. Eumeneia was a city in Asia Minor. Melito is called a eunuch, but the letter does not explain whether this means he was celibate or something else. Awaiting the visitation from heaven means awaiting the coming of the Lord in glory to raise the saints.
