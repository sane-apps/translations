Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) wrote this commentary for Christian readers seeking a deeper understanding of Matthew's Gospel. He was the most important theologian and biblical scholar of the early Greek church, and this work dates from late in his life, after AD 244.

This portion of the commentary treats selected later passages of Matthew, beginning with the title over the cross and the bribed soldiers' false report. Origen then moves to the two great commandments, the Gethsemane and arrest scenes, the woes against the scribes and Pharisees, the saying on eunuchs, the rich young man, the request of the sons of Zebedee, the withered fig tree, the parable of the vineyard, the tribute to Caesar, and the Sadducees' question about the resurrection. Throughout, he consistently prefers the spiritual and moral sense over the merely literal.

The term 'letter and spirit' refers to the contrast between the literal text of Scripture and its spiritual meaning.
