Severianus of Gabala (fl. c. 400) was a bishop in Syria who became a popular preacher in Constantinople and a rival of John Chrysostom. He wrote many biblical commentaries, but most survive only as excerpts in later Greek catenae, or chain commentaries. These are Severianus's fragments on Ephesians, not those of Origen of Alexandria. They come from that tradition and preserve his explanations of difficult points in Paul's letter.

Severianus reads Ephesians as Paul's timely instruction to believers who had already been prepared by John the Evangelist's preaching. Paul urges them not to be held fast by the law. Severianus argues that Paul carefully distinguishes the Father and the Son while honoring both, and that Christ's incarnation brought together and reconciled all things. The law's dividing wall between Jews and Greeks was abolished not because the commandments were inappropriate but because their temporary custom was no longer useful.

A reader will meet Paul as the apostle and author, John the Evangelist as the one whose earlier preaching prepared the Ephesians, and Christ as the incarnate Son through whom reconciliation is accomplished. Severianus also cites Ezekiel, David, Adam, and Eve. He quotes Paul's words from Ephesians directly, along with Old Testament passages and one saying of the Lord. Key terms include divine plan for God's saving work in Christ, law for the Mosaic law, dividing wall for the separation between Jews and Greeks, fullness for the church as Christ's body filled with God, and brought together for Christ's summing up of all things. Old Testament citations follow the Septuagint, so Psalm numbering may differ from modern English Bibles.
