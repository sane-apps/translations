John Chrysostom (c. 349–407) preached this homily to a Christian congregation. He announces a move from earlier doctrinal discussion to a fuller treatment of the resurrection, aiming to correct doctrine, order moral life, and answer complaints against God's providence. He develops this through sustained exposition of Paul's words in 2 Corinthians.

The occasion is a pastoral shift in teaching. Chrysostom wants his hearers to see that belief in the resurrection shapes both doctrine and moral life: it consoles the righteous who suffer and warns the wicked who prosper. He addresses objectors who raise doubts or accusations, and he quotes Paul extensively, especially from 2 Corinthians, along with other epistles.

A reader should know that the body is called a tent and the resurrection body a dwelling. Corruption means decay and mortality, while incorruption means the immortal state of the risen body. The Spirit is called a guarantee, a down payment of future goods. The outer self is the body subject to decay, and the inner self is the soul being renewed. Affliction means trouble endured for Christ, and confidence means boldness before God. Bible books are cited by their standard English names, with Psalm numbering following the Septuagint.
