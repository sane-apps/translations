Amphilochius of Iconium (c. 340–c. 395) wrote this homily for a Christian congregation. The sermon addresses the Gospel story of Zacchaeus to encourage sinners to run to repentance by showing Christ's mercy to tax collectors and sinners.

The homily presents Zacchaeus the chief tax collector as a model of repentance. Amphilochius argues that nothing raises the soul to joy like fear of God, turning from evil, and repentance, and that all the saved drew from the same mercy of Christ. A reader will meet Zacchaeus, Jesus, Rahab, Joshua son of Nun, Elisha, David, Peter, Paul, and Matthew. The preacher imagines Zacchaeus's interior thoughts, quotes Jesus calling him down from the tree, and quotes the murmuring critics who complain that Jesus lodges with a sinner.

Key terms include repentance, tax collector, chief tax collector, love for humanity, spring, salt, tree, paradise, the poor, sinner, and sycamore. The homily compares the barren spring of Jericho healed by Elisha's salt with the church healed by Christ through the apostles, and draws a parallel between Zacchaeus and Rahab, both saved by receiving Jesus.
