Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) wrote this commentary on Proverbs. He was an early Christian theologian and biblical scholar who served as head of the Catechetical School of Alexandria, succeeding Clement of Alexandria. The work is a verse-by-verse spiritual exposition of selected passages from Proverbs, explaining how its proverbs signify hidden realities about God, the soul, virtue, and knowledge.

Origen interprets Proverbs as a book of hidden sayings that teach spiritual knowledge, wisdom, and moral training. He argues that wisdom is spiritual knowledge of God, incorporeal realities, judgment, and providence, while discipline is the moderation of the soul's passions. The commentary contrasts the way of virtue and knowledge, which leads to life and friendship with God, with the way of wickedness and ignorance, which leads to destruction under the devil.

A reader will meet Solomon as the wise teacher whose sayings Origen expounds, along with Christ, Paul, David, Moses, John the Baptist, Peter, and other biblical figures. Origen reads the text allegorically, identifying figures such as the woman, mother, sister, bread, wine, and animals as symbols of knowledge, wisdom, the Church, virtues, or demonic powers. He repeatedly urges the soul to pursue purity, practice the commandments, and advance from practical virtue to contemplation of God. Key terms include wisdom, discipline, knowledge, virtue, wickedness, freedom from passion, mind, soul, heart, contemplation, practical virtue, fear of the Lord, the devil, demons, angels, Church, word, commandment, life, and death. Origen cites the Greek Old Testament, so Psalm numbering may differ from the Hebrew numbering used in most English Bibles.
