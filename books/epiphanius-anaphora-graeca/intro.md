This prayer is attributed to Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403), bishop of Salamis in Cyprus for nearly forty years and a well-known defender of orthodox teaching. It is a eucharistic prayer, or anaphora, giving thanks to God the Father and recalling the institution of the Lord's Supper. It was written for use in the liturgy, to be prayed over the bread and cup.

The prayer opens with thanksgiving for the sending of the eternal Son, the divine Word, who became fully human without ceasing to be God. It then recounts how Christ, before his voluntary death, took bread, gave thanks, blessed it, and gave it to his disciples as his body, and likewise the cup as his blood of the new covenant for the forgiveness of sins. The reader will meet God the Father, Christ, and the disciples, and will hear Christ's own words quoted directly.

A reader should know that the prayer asks for the bread and cup to be changed into the body and blood of Christ, saving for souls and bodies. The cup is described as mixed from two substances, the fruit of the vine and water, a common ancient liturgical practice. The prayer also speaks of sharing in the holy mysteries in newness of spirit, a phrase that echoes Paul's contrast between the newness of the spirit and the oldness of the letter in Romans 7:6.
