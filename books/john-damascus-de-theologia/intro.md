The work distinguishes the theology of the Trinity from the economy of the Incarnation, likely for instruction or clarification.

The treatise opens with the Trinity. One God is glorified and worshipped in three persons, with one divinity, nature, essence, power, authority, and will. Each person keeps a distinct property: the Father is unbegotten, the Son is begotten, and the Spirit proceeds. The Father is without beginning, the Son is co-beginningless though from the Father, and the Holy Spirit is co-eternal and co-beginningless, proceeding from the Father not by filial generation but by procession.

The second part concerns the incarnate Word. Two natures are confessed in one person, Jesus Christ, God together with man, with two wills and two activities. Each nature keeps its property without division or confusion. The divine nature raises the dead and performs miracles, while the human nature hungers, thirsts, grows weary, and endures sufferings. The Word was united to flesh without confusion or division, ascended with that flesh, sits at the Father's right hand, and will come in glory at the end of the ages to judge the living and the dead as God incarnate.
