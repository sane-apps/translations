## A. Person-reversal fixes (the 23 flagged findings)

### Finding 1 — §4.2.7
- **Was:** "I will strike at the other side of [his] definition."
- **Finding:** Latin *Pulsas* (2nd sg., "you strike") and *definitionis meae* ("of **my** definition") — Julian addressing Augustine.
- **Now:** "[You strike] — as you say — at the other side of [my] definition."
- **Why:** *Pulsas … meae* fixes both persons; *ut dicis* ("as you say") restored.

### Finding 2 — §4.3.14 (two flips)
- **Was:** "I pursue the points [in his argument] that seem clever to him." / "Eagerly blaming the substance, [he] praises its gifts."
- **Finding:** Latin *Mea … quae mihi videntur acuta persequeris* — *persequeris* 2nd sg. ("you pursue"), *Mea/mihi* 1st ("my/me"); *studio vituperando substantiam, dona collaudo* — *collaudo* 1st sg. ("I praise"). (The *tu qui* in Augustine's frame confirms "you" = Julian.)
- **Now:** "[You pursue] my points — as you say — that seem sharp to me." / "Eagerly blaming the substance, [I] praise its gifts."

### Finding 3 — §6.22.69 (two flips)
- **Was:** "Throughout my argument I have crushed your assertion about the evil of sexual desire and original sin." / "'The people's murmuring' is the only thing you set against us."
- **Finding:** Latin *Tota disputatione tua contrivisti … assertionem meam* — *tua* ("your"), *contrivisti* 2nd sg. ("you crushed"), *meam* ("my"); *populi murmur, quod solum vobis a nobis … opponi* — *vobis* ("to you" pl.), *a nobis* ("by us").
- **Now:** "Throughout your whole argument you have crushed — as you boast — my assertion about the evil of sexual desire and original sin." / "'The people's murmur' is the only thing set against you by us — as you say."

### Finding 4 — §6.19.60
- **Was:** "I cannot imagine in what dialectical treatise you found that all opposites can be reversed."
- **Finding:** Latin *Te cogitare non posse, in qua dialexi invenerim …* — *Te* ("you") cannot imagine; *invenerim* 1st sg. ("I found").
- **Now:** "[You] cannot imagine in what dialectical treatise I found that all opposites can be reversed."

### Finding 5 — §2.10.34
- **Was:** "[He would not] find anything … if standing with me among learned people…"
- **Finding:** Latin *…invenirem … consisterem tecum* — 1st sg. verbs ("I would find/stand"), *tecum* ("with you").
- **Now:** "[I would not] find anything to do or anywhere to turn, if pressed by the judges' authority, if standing with you among learned men…"

### Finding 6 — §2.10.35 (two flips)
- **Was:** "[If he were] pressed … [he would] have nothing … since he could find no answer to my arguments." / "[He] sets against me the helpless opinion of an ally and, as though panic-stricken, names his accomplices."
- **Finding:** Latin *Me, si sub potestate iudicum premerer … cum tuis argumentationibus …* — *Me* ("I"), *tuis* ("your"); *…me imbellem tibi opinionem consortis opponere … conscios nominare* — *me* ("I"), *tibi* ("against you").
- **Now:** "[If I had been] pressed by the judges' authority, [I would] have had nothing to do and nowhere to turn, since I could find no answer to your arguments." / "Heaven forbid that I should dare to think, let alone say, of these blind men, that [I] set against you the helpless opinion of an ally and, as though panic-stricken, name my accomplices." (The dropped *Absit ut audeam … vel cogitare, vel dicere* frame was restored with the flip fix, since it belongs to the same sentence.)

### Finding 7 — §2.10.36 (two flips)
- **Was:** "[He promises] to conduct this case against us, one man on behalf of everyone." / "It brings me greater glory before God to defend truth when it has been abandoned."
- **Finding:** Latin *ut me istam causam contra vos … agere pollicerer* — *me* ("I"), *contra vos* ("against you" pl.); *maioris tibi esse apud Deum gloriae …* — *tibi* ("to you").
- **Now:** "[I promised] to conduct this case against you, one man for everyone." / "It brings you greater glory before God to defend abandoned truth."

### Finding 8 — §2.8.30 (two flips; second found in sweep)
- **Was:** "[I do not] turn the filth of people's conduct into an accusation against nature…" / "[You] undermine the wall of modesty."
- **Finding:** Latin *nec conversationis sordes in naturae refundis invidiam* — *refundis* 2nd sg. ("you pour back"); *suffodiunt … murum pudoris* — *suffodiunt* 3rd pl. ("they undermine"; Julian's objection quoted by Augustine, *sicut mihi obicis*).
- **Now:** "[You do not] pour the filth of conduct back as an accusation against nature, nor comfort its obscenities by insulting the apostles and all the saints." / "[They] undermine the wall of modesty."

### Finding 9 — §3.2.7
- **Was:** "[He has] spoken against himself. To defend himself, he said that the new heretics call [him and his party] condemners of marriage…"
- **Finding:** Latin *Contra me ipsum locutum me fuisse: qui cum propter meam defensionem dixerim, ab haereticis novis dici nos damnatores esse nuptiarum…* — *me … meam … dixerim … nos* (all 1st).
- **Now:** "[I have] spoken against myself. To defend myself, I said that the new heretics call us condemners of marriage and of God's work. Then I took that back…"

### Finding 10 — §3.6.13
- **Was:** "I will not let him escape, but will question him closely and briefly: does he think an action is guilty in infants, or their nature?"
- **Finding:** Latin *non me patieris elabi, sed presse interrogabis et breviter: In parvulis actionem ream, an naturam putem* — *patieris/interrogabis* 2nd sg. ("you will"), *me* ("me"), *putem* 1st sg. ("I think").
- **Now:** "You will not let me escape, but will question me closely and briefly: whether I think an action is guilty in infants, or their nature?"

### Finding 11 — §3.10.20
- **Was:** "Perhaps [he will say] that we must establish from the testimony of Scripture…"
- **Finding:** Latin *Quod forsitan dicam …* — *dicam* 1st sg. ("I may say").
- **Now:** "Perhaps [I may say] that it must be shown from the testimony of Scripture, not from syllogisms, that children born from bodily union are counted as God's work."

### Finding 12 — §3.11.21
- **Was:** "Let him say, if he can, that something he sees God sometimes bestow as a gift belongs to the devil's work."
- **Finding:** Latin *ut asseram si possum, ad opus diaboli pertinere, quod Deum videam interdum conferre pro munere* — *asseram/possum/videam* 1st sg.
- **Now:** "Let me assert, if I can, that what I see God sometimes bestow as a gift belongs to the devil's work."

### Finding 13 — §3.21.47
- **Was:** "[He is] like the drug seller who promised an animal that would eat itself."
- **Finding:** Latin *sum pharmacopolae similis – ut dicis – …* — *sum* 1st sg. ("I am").
- **Now:** "[I am] like the drug seller — as you say — who promised an animal that would eat itself."

### Finding 14 — §3.23.52
- **Was:** "[He] charges all parents' sins to all their children."
- **Finding:** Latin *quia peccata omnium parentum omnibus filiis imputarem* — *imputarem* 1st sg. ("I would impute").
- **Now:** "[I would] impute all parents' sins to all their children."

### Finding 15 — §3.25.58
- **Was:** "[He declares] that the infants who have filled the world … are the devil's work…"
- **Finding:** Latin *eos parvulos … diaboli opus … definiam* — *definiam* 1st sg. ("I define").
- **Now:** "[I define] the infants who have filled the world, for whom Christ died, as the devil's work, born from a disease, and guilty from their beginning."

### Finding 16 — §3.26.65
- **Was:** "The whole purpose of [his] thinking is … He frightens those resisting … He falsely claims…"
- **Finding:** Latin *quod non sit alia mei sensus intentio … mentiri me tantas obscenae libidinis vires …* — *mei* ("of my"), *me* ("that I").
- **Now:** "The whole purpose of [my] thinking is to swear allegiance to vices … I frighten those resisting shameful conduct … I falsely claim that obscene desire has such strength…"

### Finding 17 — §4.1.2
- **Was:** "[He said] that, on the apostle's testimony, marital chastity is God's gift."
- **Finding:** Latin *Teste Apostolo – dixerim – donum Dei esse pudicitiam coniugalem* — *dixerim* 1st sg. ("I said").
- **Now:** "[I said] that, on the apostle's testimony, marital chastity is God's gift."

### Finding 18 — §4.3.15
- **Was:** "[I thought he called] the achievement of the human will heavenly gifts."
- **Finding:** Latin *effectum voluntatis humanae dona caelestia me nuncupasse* — *me nuncupasse* ("that I called").
- **Now:** "[that I called] the achievement of the human will heavenly gifts."

### Finding 19 — §4.9.55 (four flips)
- **Was:** "[His] position is a bedbug…" / "[I am] ashamed to crush him by defeating him." / "As though defeated, [he has] fled to filthy places, and [I] recoil from following and killing him." / "Because [I am] forced to speak about the sexual organs, [my] modesty … has excluded … the parts of [my] argument that would crush or destroy him."
- **Finding:** Latin *Sententiam meam cimicem – dicis – …* (*meam*, "my"); *verecunderis vincendo me atterere* (*verecunderis* 2nd sg. "you would be ashamed"; *me* "me"); *tamquam victus ad loca caenosa confugerim, exhorreas consequi atque perimere* (*confugerim* 1st "I fled"; *exhorreas* 2nd "you recoil"); *partes disputationis tuae, quibus atterendus vel perimendus sum, quoniam de pudendis loqui cogeris … pudor tuus …* (*tuae/tuus/cogeris* 2nd "your/you"; *sum* 1st "I [am to be crushed]").
- **Now:** "[My] position is a bedbug — as you say…" / "[You would be] ashamed to crush me by defeating me." / "As though defeated, [I have] fled to filthy places, and [you] recoil from following and killing me." / "Because [you are] forced to speak about the sexual organs, [your] modesty, like a temple keeper, has excluded from free expression the parts of [your] argument that would crush or destroy me."

### Finding 20 — §4.12.58
- **Was:** "[I quote] a few things to increase his shame: under the teaching of the sacred law he has failed to understand things that pagans could grasp through reason alone."
- **Finding:** Latin *Unde ob hoc pauca ponis – ut dicis – ut oneres pudorem meum, non ea me intellexisse sub magisterio sacrae legis…* — *ponis* 2nd sg. ("you quote"); *pudorem meum / me* ("my shame"/"me").
- **Now:** "[You quote] a few things — as you say — to increase my shame: under the teaching of the sacred law I have failed to understand things that pagans could grasp by reason's guidance alone."

### Finding 21 — §4.13.62
- **Was:** "[I ask] modest ears to forgive [me], and to share [my] distress rather than be indignant at [my] necessity."
- **Finding:** Latin *petis ut ignoscant verecundae aures, et congemiscant potius, quam indignentur necessitati tuae* — *petis* 2nd sg. ("you ask"); *tuae* ("your").
- **Now:** "[You ask] modest ears to forgive [you], and to share [your] distress rather than be indignant at [your] necessity."

### Finding 22 — §5.5.20
- **Was:** "I feel ashamed and am prevented by horror from speaking about such things, but compelled by necessity."
- **Finding:** Latin *verecundari te – dicis – et de rebus loqui talibus horrore prohiberi, sed necessitate compelli* — *te* ("you").
- **Now:** "[You] feel ashamed — as you say — and are prevented by horror from speaking about such things, but compelled by necessity."

### Finding 23 — §5.7.27
- **Was:** "If you admit desire's evil cannot be conquered, you will profess yourself an advocate of shamefulness. But if you say you called this evil natural, yet capable of being conquered—that is, avoided—"
- **Finding:** Latin *Si connivero – sicut dicis – invictum esse libidinis malum, profitebor me esse turpitudinis advocatum: si autem dixero, malum me quidem hoc naturale dixisse…* — *connivero/profitebor/dixero* 1st sg.; *me … dixisse* ("that I said").
- **Now:** "If I concede — as you say — that desire's evil cannot be conquered, I will profess myself an advocate of shamefulness. But if I said that I called this evil natural, yet one that could be conquered — that is, avoided —"

## B. Inserted fragments (the 8 flagged omissions) — with Latin incipits

### Omission 24 — §2.2.4 (long Christological fragment, ~100 words), inserted as new para [0]
- **Incipit:** *Per unum – inquit – Dominum Iesum salus ventura nationibus declaratur, qui solus non potuit iustus esse, cum generatio omnis erraret, nisi natus ex Virgine…*
- **Now:** "Through one Lord Jesus — he says — the salvation to come is declared to the nations: he alone could not be just, though the whole race went astray, unless, born of the Virgin, he was not in the least subject to the claim of the guilty race. "Behold," he says, "I was conceived in iniquities, and in sins my mother bore me" (Psalm 51:5) — so says he who was thought just above all others. Whom then shall I call just, if not him who is free of these chains, whom the chains of our common nature do not bind? All are under sin; from Adam death reigned over all. Let him come who alone is just in God's sight, of whom it is now said without exception, "He did not sin with his lips" (Job 2:10) — no, "He committed no sin" (1 Peter 2:22)." (Scripture refs: Ps 51:5; Job 2:10; 1 Pet 2:22 — the source's [n5][n6][n7] markers.)

### Omission 25 — §3.17.31 (infant-baptism statement), inserted as new para [2]

## C. Same-defect sweep — additional fixes beyond the 31 flagged

A programmatic sweep compared Latin person pronouns in every section's `candidate_quotations` against the English pronouns across all 251 entries, plus a check of every bracketed 3rd-person subject (`[He]`/`[They]`/etc.); each hit was adjudicated against the full `latin_context` for speaker. The review's validated indirect-speech normalizations (§§3.1.2–4, 3.21.42, 4.3.28, 5.16.65, 6.12.39, 6.13.40, 6.18.53, 6.23.70, and 2.10.37 para [3]) were confirmed correct and left alone.

1. **§2.5.11** — Latin *Me, nempe, dixisti nihil magis laborare, quam ut non intellegar* (Julian quoting Augustine's charge; *me* = Julian). Was "[He] works at nothing harder than making himself impossible to understand." → **Now:** "[I] work at nothing harder than making myself impossible to understand."
2. **§2.10.37 para [0]** — Latin *de plebeia faece sellulariorum … in vestram invidiam concitati sunt?* (Julian's own jest, *sicut Tulliane iocaris*; *vestram* = Augustine's party). Was "'stirred up against us.'" → **Now:** "'stirred up against you.'" (Para [3] "'We are denied an examination…'" correctly normalizes Augustine's *vobis* → Julian's "we"; untouched.)
3. **§3.17.33 para [0]** — Latin *Sed ab hoc digressu, quo te impetus … abstulerat, ad ea quae institueras … reverteris* is **Augustine's** sentence addressing Julian (*te* "you", *institueras* "you had begun"). Was "…carried [me], [I return] to what I had begun." → **Now:** "But from this digression, into which the force of grief carried [you], [return] to what you had begun."
4. **§3.17.33 para [2]** — Latin *ullis laqueis tuis peccati naturalis suffocatur assertio* (*tuis* "your"). Was "strangled in [these] snares." → **Now:** "strangled in [your] snares." (Review had rated this MINOR; fixed for consistency with para [0]'s restored 2nd person.)
5. **§3.23.53 para [1]** — Latin *sententiae meae pars falsissima est, qua dixi: …* (Julian's words quoted by Augustine; *meae* "my", *dixi* "I said" — the *qua dixi* was also dropped). Was "The most false part of [his] position is the statement: …" → **Now:** "The most false part of [my] position is the statement in which I said: …"
6. **§4.6.36 para [0]** — Latin *Ridenda contrarietate me dixisse …* (Augustine quoting Julian; *me dixisse* "that I said"). Was "[He has said], in a ridiculous contradiction…" → **Now:** "[I have said], in a ridiculous contradiction…"
7. **§4.9.54 para [0]** — Latin *…invenisse me unde persuaderem, Deum aeria constituisse coniugia* (Augustine: *Dicis …*; *me* = Julian). Was "…[he] found a way to persuade people…" → **Now:** "…[I] found a way to persuade people that God had established marriages made of air."
