John of Damascus wrote this brief doctrinal summary of the soul's parts, virtues, law, and faculties. It appears to have been compiled for instruction or reference rather than addressed to a particular person.

The treatise presents the soul as having three parts: the appetitive part, the spirited part, and the rational part. The law is described as threefold: natural, written, and spiritual in grace.

The soul also has five powers: intellect, discursive thought, opinion, imagination, and sense perception.
