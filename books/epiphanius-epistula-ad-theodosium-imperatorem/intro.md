Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403) was bishop of Salamis on Cyprus and a vigorous defender of what he considered orthodox Christian practice. He wrote this letter to the emperor Theodosius to oppose the painting of images of Christ and the saints in churches and on door curtains, and to urge their removal.

Epiphanius writes that the devil introduced idolatry into the world and is now deceiving believers back into it through painted images. He claims no ancient father or bishop painted Christ or the saints, and that those who do so dishonor Christ and invent false appearances from their own imagination.

A reader should know that Epiphanius uses the word image for a painted portrait, especially of Christ or the saints, and idolatry for the worship of images as gods. He argues that painters wrongly depict Christ with long hair by confusing the title Nazorean with Nazirites, who took a vow involving uncut hair and abstinence from wine, even though Christ drank wine. He also reasons that if Christ had distinctive long hair while the other disciples were shorn, the Pharisees and scribes would not have needed Judas to identify him with a kiss. He asks that curtains with such images be used for burying the poor, wall paintings be whitewashed, and images already painted in a shrine be removed if possible, with no new images painted.
