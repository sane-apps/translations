Apollinaris of Laodicea (c. 310–c. 390) was a bishop of Laodicea in Syria and a noted opponent of Arianism. He was elected bishop around 361 by the Nicene community of Laodicea. He was known for his logical mind and knowledge of Hebrew, and he lectured at Antioch around 374, where Jerome was among his auditors.

These fragments come from a commentary on the Gospel of John. They reflect Apollinaris's concern to defend the deity of Jesus and the unity of his person. His eagerness on this point later led him to deny a rational human soul in Christ's human nature, a view condemned at the First Council of Constantinople in 381.

The surviving material moves through passages of John, including the Logos and John's testimony, the light and the Spirit, circumcision and the Sabbath, the shepherd and the law, the compassion of the Word, the Spirit's work and the humanity of Christ, the side of Christ and the resurrection, and Peter's duty.
