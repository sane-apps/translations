John Chrysostom (c. 349–407) preached this homily to the congregation assembled in church on the day after Epiphany. The feast of Christ's baptism had ended, and many worshippers had already gone home. Chrysostom speaks to those who remained, continuing his previous exhortation and honoring the martyr Lucian, whose death he presents as a baptism in blood on this day.

The homily opens with a lament that the crowd has departed after yesterday's feast. Chrysostom urges those still present to value the church as a spiritual ark that protects against worldly passions and the instability of wealth. He then turns to Lucian, describing how the devil first tried to break him by prolonged starvation, and when that failed, added the further trial of placing food offered to idols before his eyes. Lucian endured both, sustained by hope, the example of Paul, and the fear of God.

At his trial Lucian answered every question only with 'I am a Christian,' showing that this one confession contains his homeland, citizenship, family, and occupation. The homily closes with an exhortation to train in peace for the contests of war, to confess the faith boldly, and to adorn that confession with a worthy life.
