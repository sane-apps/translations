Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) wrote this commentary for Christian readers and interpreters of 1 Corinthians. He was a theologian and biblical scholar born in Alexandria, Egypt, who later founded the Christian School of Caesarea. The work survives only in fragments, preserved in catenas, which are chains of quotations from earlier commentators compiled by later writers.

Origen expounds selected verses of 1 Corinthians to resolve exegetical, moral, and doctrinal questions for Christian readers. He often answers heretical or literalist misreadings. The fragments cover most of the epistle, from chapter one to chapter sixteen, and were edited and published by C. Jenkins in the Journal of Theological Studies in 1908.

A reader will meet the apostle Paul as a wise master-builder and model of spiritual maturity, along with Apollos, Cephas, and Timothy. Origen quotes Paul extensively and argues against heretics such as Marcion, who forbade marriage and divided the Godhead, and Basilides, whose clever arguments are cited as worldly wisdom. He also mentions the Ophians, a heretical sect who praise the serpent and require initiates to anathematize Jesus, and the Montanist prophetesses Priscilla and Maximilla, whose followers he rejects. Key terms include church as the assembly of the praiseworthy, wisdom and foolishness, spiritual person and unspiritual person, fleshly, mature, milk and solid food, spiritual gift, love, resurrection, firstfruits, temple of God, and steward. Old Testament quotations follow the Septuagint, so non-standard book names like 4 Kingdoms correspond to 2 Kings in modern Bibles. Aquila's Greek translation is an ancient Greek version of the Old Testament.
