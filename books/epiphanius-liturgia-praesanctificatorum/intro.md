This liturgical text is attributed to Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403), bishop of Salamis, the metropolis of Cyprus. He was consecrated bishop in 367 and served for nearly forty years. The work is a form of the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts, a Byzantine Rite service used on weekdays of Great Lent and the first three days of Holy Week.

The Presanctified Liturgy is performed on strict fast days when the joyful character of the full Divine Liturgy is considered unsuitable for the somber season of repentance. In this service, communion is received from Gifts that were sanctified in advance, and the liturgy has no anaphora, or Eucharistic Prayer. Its structure resembles other vesperal divine liturgies prescribed for strict fast days.

A reader will encounter prayers and rubrics for the priest and deacon, including prayers for catechumens, prayers for the faithful, and a prayer for those preparing for holy illumination, meaning baptism. The text also includes a prayer said after the holy gifts have been placed aside and a prayer behind the ambo. Readers should know that this service is connected to Vespers and includes an entrance, readings, and the communion of the Presanctified Gifts.
