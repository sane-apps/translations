## Finding 1 — §1: causal compliment re-parsed as conditional warning (MAJOR)

**Was:** "One church ought not to differ in knowledge from another church. If you lie, you are not a church."

**Finding:** ἐπεί is causal ("since"), and the clause affirms the addressees: "since you are not a church that lies." The English converted it into a conditional threat, turning a compliment on the bishops' truthfulness into a warning.

**Now:** "One church ought not to differ in knowledge from another church, since you are not a church that lies."

**Why (Greek):** «Οὐκ ὀφείλει ἐκκλησία ἐκκλησίας διαφορὰν ἔχειν ἐν γνώσει, ἐπεὶ οὐκ ἐστὲ ἐκκλησία ἡ ψευδομένη» — ἐπεί + indicative = causal; ἡ ψευδομένη (present participle, "the one that lies") with the negative οὐκ yields "not a church that lies."
