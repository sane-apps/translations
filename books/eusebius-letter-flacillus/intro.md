Eusebius of Caesarea (c. 260–c. 339) wrote this letter to Flacillus, whom he addresses as a brother. Eusebius was bishop of Caesarea in Palestine and one of the most influential Christian thinkers of his time.

The letter concerns a dispute over the Son of God. Now Eusebius presents three further books that refute the same points. He says his brief response was enough because Marcellus' errors were self-evident and already condemned.

A reader should know that Marcellus is the opponent whose treatise Eusebius answers. Key terms include Trinity, distinct person, theology, treatise, refutation, quotation, and exposure. No scripture is cited in this letter.
