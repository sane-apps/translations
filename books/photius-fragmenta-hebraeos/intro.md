Photius of Constantinople (c. 810–c. 893) was a scholar and churchman who served twice as Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople, from 858 to 867 and again from 877 to 886. Before his elevation to the patriarchate he taught philosophy at the University of Constantinople, and he is remembered in the Eastern Orthodox Church as Saint Photius the Great. His writings include biblical commentaries, letters, and the Bibliotheca, a survey of hundreds of books.

This work consists of fragments from a commentary on the Epistle to the Hebrews. The fragments survive not as a continuous treatise but as excerpts preserved in catenas, the chain commentaries that gathered remarks from earlier interpreters alongside the biblical text. The Greek text was printed in Migne's Patrologia Graeca, volume 101, columns 1253 to 1254.

A reader will meet Photius explaining passages from Hebrews, often in short notes that clarify difficult phrases or theological points. The fragments assume familiarity with the letter's argument about Christ's priesthood and the old covenant. The translation follows the surviving Greek excerpts, and readers should know that the biblical book is referred to by its traditional title, the Epistle to the Hebrews.
