Severianus of Gabala (fl. c. 400) was bishop of Gabala in Syria and a popular preacher in Constantinople from about 400 until 404. He became an enemy of John Chrysostom and helped condemn him at the Synod of the Oak.

This homily is a public sermon on the words of 1 Corinthians 15:28, where Paul says that the Son will be subjected to the Father. Severianus addresses the apparent difficulty of that subjection by arguing that it belongs to the Church's relation to Christ's sufferings, not to the divine nature.

A reader should know that Severianus uses 'economy' to mean the condescending manner in which Christ or the apostles speak for the sake of human weakness. The homily assumes familiarity with the Pauline epistles and the Gospels, and it treats the unity of the divine nature as a central theme.
