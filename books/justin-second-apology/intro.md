Justin Martyr (c. 100–c. 165) wrote this short defense in Rome, addressing it to the Roman Senate and people. He was a Christian philosopher and former Platonist, originally from Flavia Neapolis in Palestine. He wrote after recent executions of Christians in Rome under the urban prefect Urbicus, and he asks the Roman authorities to publish his defense so that others may learn the truth about Christians.

The immediate occasion was a legal case. A Christian woman separated from her unchaste husband by giving him a Roman bill of divorce. The husband retaliated by accusing her of being a Christian, and through a centurion friend he instigated the arrest of her teacher Ptolemy. When Ptolemy confessed he was a Christian, he was condemned. A bystander named Lucius protested the injustice and was executed along with a third Christian. Justin recounts these events to show that Christians are condemned merely for their name, not for any crime.

Justin argues that persecution is stirred up by demons, the offspring of fallen angels, who enslave humanity and hate those who live by reason. He presents Christ as the whole divine Word, of whom earlier philosophers and lawgivers possessed only a seed. A reader should know that the Word means the divine reason identified with Christ, and that the seed of the Word is the partial knowledge of truth planted in all people. Justin also rejects fate, insisting on free will, and warns of eternal fire for the unjust and the demons. He quotes Socrates, Xenophon, and Plato, and he challenges the Cynic philosopher Crescens, who slandered Christians, to a public debate.
