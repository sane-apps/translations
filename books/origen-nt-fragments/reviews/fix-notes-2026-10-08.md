## Page 1 — john-catena-1 (was blurb; now 10 sentences)

**Was:** "From the catena fragments on John: the Gospel's start — [[In the beginning was the Word]] — is read so that the Word is not a creature among creatures…"

**Now:** Fresh translation of the fragment on John 1:1 — the name "Word" predicated of the craftsman of all things; the Son as craftsman of wisdom and art; "the Word was named 'Flesh'" for human salvation; against those who date his existence from the virgin birth, the theologian writes "In the beginning was the Word"; the fitting use of "became" for the flesh and "was" for the Godhead; why the evangelist used "was" rather than "is"; and that verb tenses must not be pressed literally of eternal things. Breaks off at «ἀλλ' ἐπεὶ ὁ λόγος τοῦ θεοῦ ἀίδιός ἐστι…».

## Page 2 — john-catena-2 (was blurb; now 5 sentences)

**Was:** "Further early fragments keep John's witness in view: a voice in the wilderness preparing the way…"

**Now:** Fresh translation of the fragment on "he dwelt among us" / "God with us" — it was not as a man sent by the Father that he brought all things into being ("he was with God, and was God"); the Son as Wisdom, "created as beginning of God's ways" (Prov 8:22), who willed to take up a creative relation to the things to be; the Word as "beginning" not other than Wisdom in substance but in conception and relation; and Old Testament parallels (Ps 33:6; Ps 107:20). The Greek is OCR-damaged in spots (σχέοιν/ὑπάρχουαα/ἠβουλήθη/σγέσει/ἡητὰ); sense rendered, corruptions not smoothed over silently — the break at «καὶ ὁ Λουκᾶς…» is marked.

## Page 3 — john-catena-mid (was blurb; now 5 sentences)

**Was:** "Middle catena scraps on John's Gospel treat signs and speeches so that works and words together show the Son doing the Father's works…"

**Now:** Fresh translation of the fragment resolving John 3:32 ("no one receives his testimony") against John 3:33 ("he who received his testimony"): Jesus speaks a most lofty testimony of the Father and himself which no one receives *as he speaks it*, because the hearers only beginning to receive fall far short of its greatness — yet they may receive it as those coming to faith are able. A lacuna («* * *») is marked with […].

## Page 4 — john-catena-close (was blurb; now 3 sentences)

**Was:** "Later fragments touch the passion and the beloved disciple's witness so that [[these things are written that you may believe]]… JOHN CATENA CLOSEOUT."

**Now:** Fresh translation of the fragment on the many senses of "one" — by agreement ("the multitude of those who believed were of one heart and one soul," Acts 4:32), by likeness ("we were all baptized in one Spirit into one body," 1 Cor 12:13); as Adam is our head by natural birth, so Christ is our head by divine regeneration, the type of his death and resurrection. The markup-leak "JOHN CATENA CLOSEOUT." is gone.

## Page 5 — luke-catena-1 (was blurb; now 4 sentences)

**Was:** "Catena fragments on Luke gather notes on the infancy and John's role so that the forerunner prepares a people for the Lord…"

**Now:** Fresh translation of the fragment on Luke 4:38–39 (Simon's mother-in-law): Matthew and Mark's "the fever left her" shows nothing of a living agent, but Luke's "standing over, he rebuked the fever" implies a rebuked, departing living being — for one does not rebuke what is soulless; evil powers may harm the body without the sufferer's soul being accused (Job, struck by the devil with Job's leave to tempt through the body, is not to be accused); "but not his soul" — as the Lord, rebuking, heals the sick. The corrupt tail («ἀντὶ τοῦ πέταξεν») is omitted as unintelligible.

## Page 7 — matt-scholia (was blurb; now 7 sentences)

**Was:** "Scholia on Matthew compress notes on kingdom teaching and costly discipleship — take up the cross…"

**Now:** Fresh translation of the scholion on Matt 1:18 — the γέννησις/γένεσις distinction: "birth" here, "genesis" at the Gospel's opening ("Book of the genesis of Jesus Christ"); as the uttered word, intangible and invisible, becomes seen and handled when written and embodied, so the fleshless Word, become flesh, is both seen and written — hence a "book" of his genesis; genesis = the first formation by God (incorruptible, sinless), birth = succession under death's condemnation through the transgression (passible, peccable); the Lord took the sinless of genesis naturally but not the incorruptible. Breaks off at «τῆς δὲ γεννήσεως τὸ παθη[τόν…]».
