Amphilochius of Iconium (c. 340–c. 395) was a fourth-century bishop in Lycaonia, a first cousin of Gregory of Nazianzus and a close associate of Basil of Caesarea.

The homily was composed for the festal celebration of the Lord's Resurrection. Amphilochius addresses the gathered church on Easter day, urging everyone present to greet the feast with gratitude and worship. He invites all people, regardless of status or how strictly they have fasted, to share in the feast, because Christ the Passover has been sacrificed and the table is full.

The homily quotes the prophet Isaiah as foretelling Hades' bitterness at meeting Christ, and it weaves in words from Paul and the Psalms.
