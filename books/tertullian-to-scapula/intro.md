Tertullian (c. 155–c. 220) was a Christian writer and priest in Carthage, trained in Roman law. He wrote this open letter to Scapula, the proconsul of Africa, during a time when Scapula was punishing Christians. Tertullian's purpose was to urge Scapula to stop the persecution and to warn him that harming Christians brings divine punishment.

The letter responds to a specific situation in Roman Africa. Scapula had already condemned a Christian named Mavilus of Hadrumetum to the wild beasts, and crowds in Carthage were demanding action against Christians. Tertullian reminds Scapula that Christians are loyal subjects who pray for the emperor's safety and are innocent of the crimes alleged against them, such as sacrilege and treason against the imperial dignity.

A reader will meet several Roman officials whose fates Tertullian presents as warnings. Vigellius Saturninus, the first governor to use the sword against Christians in Africa, lost his eyesight. Claudius Lucius Herminianus, who persecuted Christians in Cappadocia, died of plague. Tertullian also quotes the words of Arrius Antoninus, a governor in Asia who was overwhelmed when all the Christians of a city offered themselves for martyrdom, and of Caecilius Capella, who at his death cried out, 'Christians, rejoice!' The letter uses terms such as 'faith' for the Christian way of life, 'demons' for the pagan gods, 'endurance' for the patience Christians show under persecution, and 'indictment' for the written charge brought against a Christian. Tertullian does not quote Scripture directly, but he refers to the Christian teaching of loving enemies and praying for persecutors.
