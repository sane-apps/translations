John Chrysostom (c. 349–407) is the author of this treatise, writing as a Christian teacher to persuade readers to value the monastic life.

He saw most people admiring apparent goods such as wealth, power and royal glory while despising the monastic life, so he compares a king and a monk in order to teach readers what is truly worth pursuing.

He argues that the monk who rules his passions and serves God is more truly a king than one who wears a crown, contrasting the king's wars, burdens and fears with the monk's prayer, freedom and greater honor at the future judgment, and he brings in Ahab helped through Elijah's prayer in famine, Hezekiah helped through Isaiah's prayer in illness and war, kings of Israel helped through Elisha's prayer in war, and Moses, Paul and John met through Scripture. Unnamed Scripture is quoted with the words it says. Here way of life means the Christian monastic life, kingship means royal rule, power means worldly authority and passions means disordered states such as anger and greed, and the book names follow the Septuagint while biblical persons use modern English forms, with Ahab as king of Israel and with the saying about the kingdom of heaven within you matching the Gospel saying about the kingdom of God within you.
