Severianus of Gabala (fl. c. 400) was bishop of Gabala in Syria and a well-known preacher in Constantinople. These fragments come from his explanations of Paul's First Letter to the Thessalonians. They survive only in later quotation collections, not as an independent manuscript.

The work addresses Thessalonian Christians who were suffering persecution from their Greek neighbors and grieving the loss of relatives. Severianus explains that Paul wrote to comfort them. He quotes Paul's words that the gospel came not only in word but also in power and wonders, and that Paul's boldness in preaching was not diminished by his sufferings at Philippi.

A reader will meet Paul, whose words from 1 Thessalonians are quoted and explained, and Christ, whose sayings from the Gospels are cited. Severianus argues that some righteous people will still be alive at Christ's coming, but the dead in Christ will rise first and the righteous will meet the Lord. He interprets the day of the Lord as Christ's sudden, unexpected coming, like a thief in the night, and explains that the night setting shows how the day exposes evil deeds done at night. He also urges believers to remain watchful and to test prophetic utterances rather than despise or uncritically accept them. Key terms include the coming of Christ, the resurrection, those who have fallen asleep, those who are left, the day of the Lord, spirit, soul, body, prophecy, gift, gospel, and affliction. , and the threefold blessing of spirit, soul, and body comes from 1 Thessalonians 5:23.
