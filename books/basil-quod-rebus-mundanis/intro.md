Basil of Caesarea (c. 330–379) was the preacher and bishop speaking in the first person to the church.

He exhorts his hearers not to cling to worldly goods and comforts after a recent fire against the precincts of the church, stirred up by the devil and turned back by divine grace before it could reach the sanctuary.

Basil argues that present existence is a road where the devil sets ambushes through pleasures, wealth, power and bodily indulgence, that only soul and body with virtues are truly owned while riches are alien and cannot accompany us beyond death, and that his hearers should lighten the soul by giving to the poor harmed by the fire and imitate Job in endurance until suffering becomes a crown of endurance. The reader meets Job as model, Job's wife quoted urging Job to curse God and die and rebuked by him, David and Solomon and Paul quoted in Scripture, Abraham and Lazarus and the rich man from the parable, and imagined speech of those who lived well to the unmerciful at the judgment. Here road means the journey of human existence, wealth means earthly riches, pleasures means deceptive enjoyments, soul and body are the true possessions, virtues last, endurance is steadfastness under trial, hell is punishment of the unmerciful, fire is the recent attack, church is both building and gathered people, vanities are hollow passing things, and Old Testament quotations follow the Septuagint but are cited by Hebrew Bible titles with current Psalm numbering.
