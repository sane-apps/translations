Gregory Thaumaturgus (c. 213–c. 270) was a third-century bishop of Neocaesarea in Pontus, in what is now Turkey. Born into a pagan family under the name Theodore, he studied with the Christian teacher Origen at Caesarea in Palestine for about five years and was converted to Christianity.

This short work is a statement of Trinitarian faith. The source does not name an addressee or a specific occasion for writing, and it does not identify any opponent. The title tradition says Gregory received the declaration by revelation from John the evangelist through the Virgin Mary, but the text itself simply presents Gregory's own confession.

Gregory confesses one God the Father, Father of the living Word and of the only-begotten Son. He then confesses one Lord, the Son, alone from the Father alone, God from God, the character and image of the Godhead, the active Word, the wisdom that contains the constitution of all things, and the power that creates the whole creation. He also confesses one Holy Spirit, who has existence from God and is manifested through the Son, that is, to human beings, the perfect image of the perfect Son, cause of life for the living, holy spring, and giver of holiness. The Trinity is perfect, undivided and unalienated in glory, eternity, and kingdom; nothing created, servile, or added later exists in it, and the Trinity remains unchanging and unalterable.
