Eustathius of Antioch (c. 320–337) wrote this treatise for a Christian audience. He was bishop of Beroea before becoming patriarch of Antioch, and he was a prominent opponent of Arianism at the Council of Nicaea. The surviving fragments explain the meaning of the stone pillar that Jacob set up at Bethel, interpreting it as a prophecy of Christ, the cross, and the two peoples.

The work is a theological exposition of the biblical narrative. Eustathius argues that Jacob's pillar at Bethel was not an idolatrous stele but a divinely inspired prophecy. The stone and the ladder vision prefigure Christ's body, the cross, and the church as the house of God and gate of heaven. The oil poured on the stone points to the anointing of Christ's feet and to his burial, while the stone itself signifies Christ as the rejected cornerstone.

A reader will meet Jacob, whose vision and pillar at Bethel are the subject of the treatise, and Christ, whose body, cross, and burial are prefigured in Jacob's vision and pillar. Mary is mentioned as the woman who anointed Christ's feet with ointment. Moses is cited for the story of Rebecca and the two nations, and Rebecca is the mother of Jacob and Esau to whom the Lord announced the two nations in her womb. The two nations are interpreted as Jews and Christians, with the older people serving the younger. Key terms include pillar, ladder, cross, temple, gate of heaven, cornerstone, oil, two nations, two peoples, idolatry, trophy, and house of God.
