Evagrius Ponticus (c. 345–399) was born in Pontus and served as a deacon under Gregory of Nazianzus in Constantinople before leaving the city to become a monk. He spent his later years in the monastic settlements of Nitria and Kellia in Egypt, where he worked as a spiritual teacher and supported himself by copying manuscripts. This work is a set of brief notes on selected verses from the biblical book of Proverbs, written for disciples who were beginning their spiritual training and were advised to meditate especially on this book.

The Scholia on Proverbs consists of roughly 382 short exegetical notes rather than a continuous commentary. Evagrius interprets the proverbs almost exclusively in a spiritual and symbolic way, reading them on three levels: ethical, physical, and theological. These three levels correspond to the three books of Solomon, with Proverbs assigned to the ethical or practical stage, Ecclesiastes to the physical, and the Song of Songs to the theological.

A reader will meet notes on wisdom, the fear of God, the soul, virtue, friendship, law, and the passions. To follow the work, it helps to know that Evagrius treats the sayings of Proverbs as figures for the inner life and the soul's progress, and that his explanations assume a reader interested in ascetic practice and contemplation. The notes are arranged as scholia on selected passages, so they do not cover every verse of Proverbs.
