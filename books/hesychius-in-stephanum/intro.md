Hesychius of Jerusalem (c. 412–450), a priest of the church of Jerusalem, preached this homily in praise of Saint Stephen, the first martyr. He claims Stephen for his own city: there Stephen served as deacon, gave his witness, and departed to Christ.

The false witnesses charge that Stephen speaks against the temple and the law (Acts 6:13-14). Hesychius answers that Stephen said only what God had already said through Micah, Isaiah, David and Moses, and he reads the threats of Leviticus 26 and Deuteronomy 28, with Zechariah 14, as fulfilled at the cross and the resurrection.

The homily closes with Stephen's vision of the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God (Acts 7:56), his stoning outside the city, and his two prayers: that the Lord Jesus receive his spirit, and that this sin not be set against his killers (Acts 7:59-60).
