Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) wrote these selections on the Psalms for Christian readers seeking the spiritual meaning of Scripture. He was a theologian and head of the Catechetical School of Alexandria, and later ordained a priest by the bishops of Caesarea and Jerusalem. His commentary on the Psalter survives only in fragments, preserved in later collections.

This English is those excerpts. It is not the separate book of Greek fragments that follows Psalms 1 through 22. He interprets selected Psalm verses as prophecies of Christ and the Church, and as moral instruction for the soul. He reads the Psalms spiritually, treating Zion, the earth, mountains, and the exodus as figures of the Church, the soul, and the spiritual life.

A reader will meet David the Psalmist, whose words Origen reads as prophecies of Christ. Origen also quotes Peter, Paul, Isaiah, Solomon, and the Hebrews in the wilderness, and argues against the followers of the heretic Valentinus. He cites the Greek translators Aquila and Symmachus for alternative readings. Psalm numbers follow the Septuagint, which generally differ from the Hebrew numbering used in most modern English Bibles. Key terms include the world, Zion, the meek, land, tent, manna, rock, quail, victory shout, trumpet, endurance, envy, and abandonment.
