Athanasius of Alexandria (c. 293/298–373) wrote this letter with other bishops gathered around him in Alexandria. It is addressed to Eusebius, Lucifer, Asterius, Cymatius, and Anatolius, fellow ministers working in Antioch. The letter was written in 362 to guide the reconciliation of Christian groups in Antioch that had been divided by the Arian controversy and by disputes over theological language.

The occasion was the recall of exiled bishops after the emperor Julian allowed them to return, which led many formerly separated Christians to seek peace and to break with the Arians. Athanasius and the bishops with him urge the Antiochene leaders to receive those who wish to make peace, requiring only that they condemn the Arian heresy, confess the faith of Nicaea, and reject the view that the Holy Spirit is a creature. The letter reports that those who spoke of three hypostases and those who spoke of one hypostasis were examined and found to hold the same orthodox faith, differing only in terminology.

A reader should know that the letter uses 'being' for the shared divine substance and 'person' for the three in the Trinity, and that 'creature' means a created thing, which the letter says the Holy Spirit is not. The author cites the Septuagint, so Psalm numbers may differ from modern English Bibles. The letter also records agreement on the incarnation: the Word himself became flesh, taking a complete human nature with soul and body, and it forbids reading or producing a rumored document from the Council of Sardica as a statement about faith.
