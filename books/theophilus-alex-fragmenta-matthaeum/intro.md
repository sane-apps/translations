Theophilus of Alexandria (c. 345–412) is the name on these fragments on Matthew. They are not his fragments on John.

He takes up the story of the two sons. The first stands for the Gentiles. God, the common Father, sowed a teachable and natural law in their minds. They said to the wood, "You are my Father," and later repented and went to the vineyard. The second son is Israel, called through Moses, who said they would do and hear, and then did neither.

John justifies the prostitutes who repented. The Pharisees, who keep the law in words only, are rejected. The kingdom of heaven is in deeds, not in words.
