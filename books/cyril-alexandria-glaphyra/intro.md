Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote these comments on the books of Moses. The file runs from Adam and the mystery of Christ through Abraham, Jacob, and Joseph, and on into Exodus, with openings for Leviticus, Numbers, and Deuteronomy. It is not the Dialogues on the Holy Trinity, and it is not the Thesaurus.

He reads the law as a tutor and the mystery of Christ as the better hope. Adam, Cain and Abel, Noah, Abraham and Melchizedek, Jacob, and Joseph are set out as figures. Moses in Midian, the Passover, and the unknown murder in Deuteronomy are here as well.

The church is set over against the synagogue, and righteousness is said to come through faith in Christ. Cyrus is brought in beside the mystery.
