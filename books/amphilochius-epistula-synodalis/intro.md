Amphilochius of Iconium (c. 340–c. 395) wrote this letter on behalf of a synod. He was bishop of Iconium in Lycaonia, and he wrote to a church or group of churches that had asked the synod for a ruling on the faith, especially concerning the Holy Spirit. Basil, the bishop highly esteemed by the recipients, was prevented by illness from attending, so Amphilochius replied and attached Basil's own treatise on the subject.

The letter defends the divinity of the Holy Spirit against those who denied it. Amphilochius affirms the Nicene faith as truly catholic and apostolic. He argues that the Lord's baptismal command, which names Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, excludes the errors of Sabellius, the Arians, the Anomoeans, and the opponents of the Spirit. He warns that those who blaspheme the Spirit will be condemned with the Arians, even if they reject communion with them.

A reader should know that the Anomoeans were Arians who said the Son was unlike the Father in essence, and that Sabellius taught that Father, Son, and Spirit are one person appearing in three modes. The word 'person' here renders the Greek term for one of the three distinct realities in the Trinity, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Amphilochius denounces both polytheism and atheism because the doctrine of the Trinity avoids worshipping many gods while also refusing to deny God. The letter cites the baptismal command from Matthew 28:19 and alludes to the unforgivable sin from the Gospels.
