Amphilochius of Iconium (c. 340–c. 395) wrote this homily for a Christian audience he addresses directly as “you” and once as “master.” He was a fourth-century bishop of Iconium in Lycaonia, a first cousin of Gregory of Nazianzus and a close friend of Basil the Great. He preached to defend the Son’s equality with the Father against interpretations that would make the Son a mere subordinate agent or deny his freedom.

The homily is an exposition of John 5:19, where Jesus says that the Son can do nothing of himself. Amphilochius argues that this does not mean the Son is weak or compelled. The saying must be read with its continuation: the Son does whatever he sees the Father doing, as one of the same nature and will. A reader will meet Jesus, the Father, the paralytic healed at the pool after thirty-eight years, the Jews who object to the Sabbath healing, the apostle Paul, and the apostles. Jesus’ words from the Gospel are quoted and explained, the paralytic and the Jews speak briefly, Paul is cited on the impossibility of animal blood removing sins, and the apostles are cited from Acts on the impossibility of not speaking what they had seen and heard.

To follow the homily, a reader should know that “cannot” here distinguishes impossibility from unfittingness, and “of himself” means acting independently or on one’s own apart from the Father. The Son does the same works “in the same way” as the Father, not merely similar works, and shares one nature, will, and authority with him. The Son works together with the Father, not under him, and receives equal honor. Bible citations use old book names and numbering; they are given here in modern form, such as John 5:19, Acts 4:20, and Hebrews 10:4.
