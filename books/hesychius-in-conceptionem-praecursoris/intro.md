Hesychius of Jerusalem (c. 412–450) was a priest of the church in Jerusalem and a renowned preacher and biblical interpreter in the early fifth century. He wrote this homily in Greek for the feast of John the Baptist's conception, which he preached in Jerusalem. Hesychius was also known for his opposition to Nestorianism and for his liturgical addresses on the Virgin Mary.

The homily was delivered on the feast celebrating the conception of John the Baptist. It covers the mission of the prophets, John's role as prophet, the annunciation to Zachariah and John's birth, John's asceticism, the Baptism of Christ, and John's conflict with Herod and his beheading.

The homily includes reflection on the oath and the dance connected with Herod, and it condemns the women involved in the events surrounding John's conflict with Herod.
