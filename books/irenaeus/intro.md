This passion was written by an anonymous Christian of Sirmium and names no individual reader. It belongs to the time of Diocletian and Maximian. It was written to set before its hearers the contest and victory of Irenaeus, bishop of Sirmium, and it closes by asking Christ to receive Irenaeus and to confirm his Catholic church of Sirmium in the faith.

Irenaeus is arrested and brought before Probus, governor of Pannonia, who orders him to offer sacrifice to the gods or face torture. Irenaeus refuses, welcomes suffering as sharing the passion of Christ, and holds to his confession of faith under torture and in prison. When his parents, children, wife, household, neighbours and friends beg him to spare his youth he answers no one, keeping before his eyes Christ's saying about denying Christ. After a second hearing at night he again refuses, says he worships only God and not handmade gods, disowns wife, children and parents for Christ's sake, is sentenced to drowning, asks for the sword as well, prays for his spirit and for the church of Sirmium, and is then beheaded and thrown into the Sava. The reader hears the words of Probus, the answers and prayers of Irenaeus, the plea of his family and friends, and sayings of Jesus Christ quoted by the narrator and by Irenaeus, which Irenaeus sets against the order to sacrifice.

Offer sacrifice here means performing the required pagan offering as a loyalty test. Gods are the pagan gods, described as handmade. Governor is the Roman provincial judge, bishop is the head of the local congregation, and contest is the trial pictured as ending in victory. These in the closing prayer means the people of the church of Sirmium. The work quotes Scripture without naming books. The sayings used are cited here as Exod 22:20, Matt 10:33 and Matt 10:37, and no Psalms occur.
