Severianus of Gabala (fl. c. 400) was a bishop in Syria who became a well-known preacher in Constantinople. He spoke Greek with a Syriac accent and was remembered as learned in the Scriptures and an effective public speaker. He was also an opponent of John Chrysostom and took part in the synod that condemned him.

These fragments on the Letter to the Colossians survive through the catena tradition, where excerpts from early Christian commentators were collected alongside the biblical text. They preserve Severianus's explanations of Paul's letter, often responding to errors he saw in the practices and teachings of his own day.

A reader will meet Paul's words as the starting point for each comment, and Severianus addresses those who gave devotion to angels and those who clung to legal observances.
