Evagrius Ponticus (c. 345–399) was a Christian teacher and ascetic writer. He was ordained as a reader by Basil of Caesarea, served as a deacon and archdeacon under Gregory of Nazianzus in Constantinople, and participated in the Second Ecumenical Council in 381. Around 382 he left Constantinople, joined a monastic community in Jerusalem led by Rufinus and Melania the Elder, took monastic vows in 383, and then spent the rest of his life in the Egyptian desert at Nitria and Kellia.

This English is his treatise On Evil Thoughts. It is not his Treatise to Eulogius. He sets out how malicious thoughts arise, how they differ, and how a monk can resist them. In some manuscripts the treatise was copied under the name of Nilus of Ancyra.

A reader will meet Evagrius's teaching on how malicious thoughts arise and how they can be distinguished and resisted. The treatise discusses the agitation of the soul, demonic strategy, the combat with demons, the deceptions of demons, the torment of the demon, the sting of the viper, the remedy of anger, the sword of discernment, the deception of gold, the flight of thoughts, the vulnerability of the soul, demonic interference during reading, and the recovery of the soul and the vision of the divine place.
