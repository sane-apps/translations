Amphilochius of Iconium (c. 340–c. 395) wrote this homily for a congregation celebrating the feast of the Meeting of the Lord, when the Gospel account of the infant Jesus being brought to the temple and received by Simeon and Anna was read. Amphilochius was bishop of Iconium from about 374, and before that he had practiced law. He was a cousin of Gregory of Nazianzus and a friend of Basil of Caesarea, who probably placed him in the bishopric. This sermon is the oldest known homily for this feast, which falls on February 2.

The homily expounds the Gospel of Luke's account of the Presentation. Amphilochius praises three honorable states of life: virginity, honorable marriage, and widowhood. He argues that the law's phrase about every male that opens the womb applies uniquely to Christ, since not every firstborn male was holy, as the examples of Cain, Esau, and Reuben show. He presents Anna the prophetess as the supreme model of widowhood, quoting her words to those in the temple as she recognizes the infant as the eternal Creator and Redeemer. He then turns to Simeon's prophecy that a sword would pierce Mary's soul, dramatizing Mary's objection and Simeon's reply, and identifies the sign of contradiction with the sign of the cross.

A reader should know that the homily follows the Septuagint, the Greek Old Testament, and its book names and Psalm numbering. The preacher addresses Mary as the Mother of God, a title already in use. Key terms include virginity, honorable marriage, widowhood, and single marriage, all states of life the sermon praises. The sign of contradiction is Simeon's term for the cross, and the sword that pierces Mary's soul is interpreted as the anguish she suffered at the crucifixion. The homily also quotes the apostle Paul on widows and cites Old Testament figures such as Isaiah, Ezekiel, Noah, Jonah, and Abraham.
