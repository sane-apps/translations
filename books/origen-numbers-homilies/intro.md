Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) made the pulpit at Caesarea a school of Scripture, preaching through book after book while his patron Ambrose supplied the shorthand team that preserved him.

The twenty-eight Homilies on Numbers, surviving in Rufinus's Latin, turn the wilderness census into spiritual counsel: the church's warfare, Balaam's oracle, and the cities of refuge.

This volume holds Homilies I–II and IV–XXVIII, in eighty-three sections; Homily III is lost to a torn page in the copy-text and cannot be recovered.
