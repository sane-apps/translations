Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) wrote this commentary for readers of his exposition of the Epistle to the Ephesians. He composed it as part of his extensive work on the letters of Paul, while he was active as a teacher and later as a priest in Alexandria and Caesarea. The original commentary does not survive in full, and what remains has been preserved in short passages within a Byzantine catena and in quotations by Jerome in his own commentary on Ephesians.

The work expounds Ephesians verse by verse, addressing questions of text, grammar, and theology raised by the letter. Origen often engages alternative interpretations and opposing views, quoting Paul's words as lemmata and then responding to objections introduced with phrases like 'someone will say' or 'another says.' A reader will meet Paul, whose language Origen analyzes, along with Christ, God the Father, the devil, and Tychicus. Origen also cites the Greek translators Aquila, Theodotion, and Symmachus, and argues against the heretic Valentinus, whose followers taught two baptisms.

Origen interprets Ephesians as teaching that believers are brought from non-being into being through participation in Christ, who is Wisdom, Truth, and Peace. He argues that spiritual blessings are heavenly and intelligible rather than bodily, and that redemption through Christ's blood frees believers from hostile powers. He treats the church as Christ's body and fullness, and urges moral transformation through putting off the old self and putting on the new. A reader should know that 'redemption' renders apolytrosis, 'adoption' renders huiothesia, 'fullness' renders pleroma, and 'recapitulation' renders anakephalaiosis. Origen cites the Old Testament in Greek, generally following the Septuagint, and refers to books by their Greek titles, so Psalm numbering follows the Septuagint.
