Methodius of Olympus (d. c. 311) was a bishop in Lycia and a prolific theologian. He wrote this treatise, which now survives only in fragments, probably to encourage Christians facing persecution.

The work reflects a time when Christians could be put to death for their faith. Methodius presents martyrdom as a gift from God that crowns human resolve, while also insisting that the completion of every good action belongs to God.

A reader will meet Jesus Christ, the Son of God, who honored martyrdom by bearing witness to it. The text quotes Philippians 2:6, saying that Christ did not consider equality with God something to be grasped. Methodius says that every good action has its beginning in human will, but its completion rests with God. The word "substance" here means the grounding or reality of a good action in human will, not material stuff.
