Amphilochius of Iconium (c. 340–c. 395) was a fourth-century bishop in Lycaonia, in what is now Turkey. He delivered this homily to the congregation gathered for the feast of the Nativity of Christ. His purpose was to celebrate the birth of the great God and Savior Jesus Christ and to exhort the faithful to live worthily of their adoption as God's children.

The occasion is the festal liturgy for the Nativity. Amphilochius presents the feast as the foundation of the Church's spiritual meadow, fulfilling ancient prophecy and renewing creation. He speaks directly to the worshippers, urging them to become light and leaven in the world so that God may be glorified in them.

A reader will meet the patriarch Abraham, the prophets Moses, Isaiah, and Ezekiel, and above all the Virgin Mary and Jesus Christ. The homily quotes the prophet Isaiah and the Gospel, and it argues against the perplexity of the ancients who did not celebrate the feast. Key terms include Nativity, coming, the Virgin, flesh, incorruptibility, adoption, mystery, meadow, lights, leaven, dawn, star, sun of righteousness, devil, and Jews. Old Testament citations follow the Septuagint, so Psalm numbering may differ from the Hebrew.
