Gregory Thaumaturgus (c. 213–c. 270) was a Christian bishop of Neocaesarea in Pontus, in what is now northwest Turkey. He wrote this letter to a bishop whom he addresses as a holy pope, a title used for a senior bishop rather than the Bishop of Rome. He wrote in response to a Gothic invasion of Pontus that brought violence, captivity, and moral confusion to the Christian community.

The letter answers pastoral questions about Christians taken captive by the barbarian invaders. Gregory addresses whether food placed before captives by their captors defiles them, and how to treat women who suffered sexual violation at the hands of the barbarians.

A reader should know that the surname Thaumaturgus means wonder-worker, a title given to Gregory because of miracles he was said to have performed. The term holy pope here means a bishop, not the Bishop of Rome. Gregory quotes the Septuagint, the early Greek translation of the Hebrew Scriptures, and refers to the book of Deuteronomy. Key terms include foods, captives, barbarians, defiles, immoral disposition, chastity, violation, capital sin, and prayers.
