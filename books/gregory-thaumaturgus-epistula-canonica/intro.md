Gregory Thaumaturgus (c. 213–c. 270) was a Christian bishop of Neocaesarea in Pontus, in what is now northwest Turkey. He wrote this letter to a bishop whom he addresses as a holy pope, offering pastoral guidance after a Gothic invasion brought violence and captivity to the region. The letter responds to urgent questions about how to treat Christians who had been taken captive and forced into difficult situations.

The occasion is the Gothic invasion of Pontus, during which barbarians took Christians captive, placed food before them, and violated captive women. Gregory writes to help the bishop decide how such people should be received back into the church's prayers. He argues that food eaten by captives does not defile them, since Scripture teaches that food does not defile a person. He then turns to women who suffered sexual violation, distinguishing between those whose previous life showed an immoral disposition and those who had lived in chastity beyond suspicion.

A reader will meet the apostle Paul and Jesus the Savior, whose words are quoted to show that food does not defile. Gregory also quotes Deuteronomy to argue that a woman forced in the open country is not guilty of a capital sin. The letter uses the term foods for what the captives ate, captives for the Christians taken by the barbarians, and violation for the sexual violence done to women. The book of Deuteronomy is referred to by that name, and the quotations follow the Septuagint wording where it differs from modern translations.
