Evagrius Ponticus (c. 345–399) wrote this treatise, The Gnostic, for a monk who has been trusted with knowledge. It is not The Practice.

He sets the practical monk, who has quieted the passions, apart from the gnostic, who has received knowledge and must guard it. Freedom from anger is one of the guards he names.

The counsels cover conduct, teaching, and what to say about divine things. The text names Gregory, Basil, Athanasius, Didymus, Balaam, Caiaphas. The citations use the old names of the biblical books.
