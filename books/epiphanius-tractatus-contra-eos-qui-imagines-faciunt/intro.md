Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403) was bishop of Salamis, the chief city of Cyprus, for nearly forty years. He wrote this treatise to Christians who made and honored painted images of Christ, Mary the Mother of God, martyrs, angels, and prophets. He believed the practice was a return to idolatry and wrote to correct it.

The work argues that making images of the saints dishonors them, since Scripture promises they will be transformed into glory like Christ and the angels, not represented in dead, voiceless matter. Epiphanius quotes the apostles John and Paul, the Lord, the prophet, an angel, and the apostle who refused worship from Cornelius. He also cites the fathers at the Council of Laodicea, who pronounced an anathema on angel worship, and he answers opponents who claim the images serve as memorials.

A reader should know that Epiphanius uses the Greek Old Testament, so Psalm numbering may differ from modern Bibles. He calls Mary the ever-virgin and the Mother of God. Key terms include images for painted likenesses, worship for the act due to God alone, incomprehensible and uncircumscribable for what cannot be grasped or pictured, and idolatrous custom for the practice he opposes. The Council of Laodicea was a regional church council that condemned angel worship.
