Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444), bishop of Alexandria from 412, is best known for his part in the controversies over the person of Christ. He also wrote long commentaries on Scripture, among them this Commentary on Isaiah.

In the prologue Cyril says that the prophet's words need two kinds of explanation: the history set out exactly, and the spiritual meaning given its due. He calls Isaiah both prophet and apostle. The prophecy mixes threats against an unfaithful Israel with promises of good things, and everywhere it looks toward the redemption through Christ and the calling of the nations.

This volume gives passages from the prologue and from each of the five books, translated from the Greek text in Patrologia Graeca 70.
