This homily was written by Eustathius of Antioch (c. 320–337). It is a moral exhortation on the story of Joseph, focusing on his self-control in the house of his master.

The occasion is a homiletic treatment of the Joseph story. Eustathius praises Joseph for preserving his self-control when the master's woman desired him for his beauty, and he contrasts Joseph's virtue with the violence of Simeon and Levi.

A reader will meet Joseph, his master described as a eunuch, the master's woman, and Joseph's brothers Reuben, Judah, Simeon, and Levi. Eustathius argues that Joseph, though sold to a eunuch and living before the written law, kept a pure conscience and defended a natural law of self-control. He cites the commandment 'You shall not commit adultery' as an unwritten law. The surviving fragments suggest a moral exhortation on self-control and the superiority of Joseph's conduct over his brothers' murder of the Shechemites. The biblical references are to Genesis.
