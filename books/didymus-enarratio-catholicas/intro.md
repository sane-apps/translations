Didymus the Blind (c. 313–c. 398) was a Christian teacher in Alexandria. Athanasius appointed him head of the catechetical school. He remained a layman, followed Origen, and taught for about half a century. In 553 a council condemned his works.

This English is his notes on the catholic epistles. The notes survive in manuscripts in Rome and at the Escorial. They are not a commentary on one of Paul's letters.

A reader meets notes on divine wisdom, the nature of love, the seed of God, the world's hatred, the possession of the Father and the Son, the coming judgment, and false teachers. The letters are those of James, Peter, John, and Jude.
