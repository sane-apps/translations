## u01-open (chs. 1–3) — M-1–M-15

- M-1 — **Was:** "do so." (dangling; ἀτενίζουσι dropped) — **Now:** "gaze intently neither at the belly nor at anxious care for perishable things." Why: ἀτενίζω = "gaze intently"; the verb was omitted.
- M-2 — **Was:** "rather, like those who make prayers for the sake of profit" — **Now:** "— not like those who offer prayers for the sake of gain —." Why: the negation is required; the Greek contrasts, not compares (οὐχ ὡς… ἀλλ᾽ ὡς).
- M-3 — **Was:** "my works" — **Now:** "your works." Why: σῶν ἔργων = "your works" (2nd person).
- M-4 — **Was:** "to not miss the fruits of the seed" — **Now:** "to reap the fruits of the seed." Why: ἀμήσασθαι (aor. mid. inf. of ἀμάω) = "to reap"; the harvest image was lost.
- M-5 — **Was:** "existence" — **Now:** "property." Why: ὕπαρξις = "property, possessions" (mundane), not philosophical "existence."
- M-6 — **Was:** "the soul of familiar places" — **Now:** "familiar places." Why: "soul" is invented; Greek has only οἰκείων τόπων.
- M-7 — **Was:** "instability" — **Now:** "sickness." Why: ἀῤῥωστία = "sickness" (false friend).
- M-8 — **Was:** "the night-owl, bringing on the night of thoughts, watches over the soul of the ray of the superiors" — **Now:** "the night-raven, bringing on the night of thoughts, darkens the soul from the ray of the superior ones." Why: ὁ νυκτικόραξ is the subject (night-raven); ἐπισκοτεῖ = "darkens"; "soul of the ray" was invented.
- M-9 — **Was:** "Apith, he says" — **Now:** "‘Depart!’ he says." Why: Ἄπιθι is the imperative "Depart!" — the exorcism formula, not a proper name.
- M-10 — **Was:** "the more we should strike them down" — **Now:** "let us chant psalms against them all the more." Why: καταψάλλωμεν (hortatory subj.) = "let us chant [psalms] against"; the psalmody-against-demons image became physical violence.
- M-11 — **Was:** "I was desolate in the desert" — **Now:** "I lodged in the desert." Why: ηὐλίσθην (aor. pass.) = "I lodged/spent the night" (Ps 55:7 LXX).
- M-12 — **Was:** "(Psalm 101:7)" — **Now:** "(Ps 55:7 LXX)." Why: the Greek quotes Ps 55:7 LXX (ὡσεὶ νυκτικόραξ…), not Ps 101:7.
- M-14 — **Was:** "widen the nets with much despair" — **Now:** "widen the nets with much security." Why: ἄδεια = "security, safety"; "despair" is a 180° inversion.
- M-15 — **Was:** "Chapter 4." — **Now:** "Chapter 3." Why: ΚΕΦΑΛ. Γʹ = 3.

## u01-rem-early (chs. 3–6) — M-30–M-62

- M-30 — **Was:** "he calls virtue an invincible possession" — **Now:** "he who claims to possess virtue unassailed is still passion-driven." Why: ἀπολέμητον = "unwarred-upon, unassailed" (not ἀνίκητος "invincible").
- M-31 — **Was:** "for according to the arrangement of the strategy of virtuous labors, the vice of opposites is set in opposition" — **Now:** "for against the battle-array of the virtuous strategy of labors, the vice of the adversaries is also drawn up in counter-formation." Why: ἀντιπαρατάττεται = "is arrayed in counter-formation against"; the vice of the *adversaries* (τῶν ἐναντίων), not "of opposites."
- M-32 — **Was:** "To be in virtue, the heart must not have war" — **Now:** "It has been taken away for a heart without war to be in virtue." Why: ἀφῄρηται (perf. pass.) denies the possibility; the English prescribed it (180°).
- M-33 — **Was:** "For virtue is called a name of excellence in practice" — **Now:** "For virtue is called a practical name from deeds of valor." Why: the ἀρετή/ἀριστεῖα etymology was garbled.
- M-34 — **Was:** "brings himself to nothingness" — **Now:** "does not know how to bear being made nothing." Why: ἐξουδένωσιν φέρειν = "to bear being made nothing" (φέρω = bear/endure, not bring).
- M-35 — **Was:** "he who destroys the sufferings of the flesh" — **Now:** "O you who destroy the passions of the flesh." Why: vocative participle (address, not relative clause); πάθη = "passions," not "sufferings."
- M-36 — **Was:** "accumulates hatred toward jealousy" — **Now:** "heaps hatred upon jealousy toward him." Why: datives (τῷ ζήλῳ = "upon jealousy"); the heaping is *upon* jealousy.
- M-37 — **Was:** "he treats the low-aiming glory like a nurse" — **Now:** "he mocks the low-seeking glory." Why: κωμῳδέω = "mock, lampoon."
- M-38 — **Was:** "he does not bear the one who succeeds in honoring him away" — **Now:** "he cannot bear to honor the approved man in his absence." Why: οὐ βαστάζει ἀπόντα τὸν εὐδοκιμοῦντα τιμᾷν.
- M-39 — **Was:** "he was sold to many masters; he will be lifted up by pride, and by envy, and by jealousy, and by the words previously spoken of the spirits" — **Now:** "he was sold to many masters — to pride, envy, jealousy, and the aforesaid picked troops of the spirits." Why: the datives *are* the "many masters"; λογάσιν = "picked troops," not "words."
- M-40 — **Was:** "but he who, pouring out the spirit of honor, will humble the one speaking of demons" — **Now:** "but he who shatters the spirit of honor by humility will utterly destroy the whole legion of demons." Why: ῥάσσω = "shatter" (not "pour out"); τὸν λεγεῶνα = "the legion" (not "the one speaking").
- M-41 — **Was:** "those with low minds" — **Now:** "the humble-minded." Why: ταπεινοφροσύνη = "humility," not "low minds."
- M-42 — **Was:** "ease" — **Now:** "vileness." Why: εὐτέλεια = "vileness, cheapness."
- M-43 — **Was:** "Abyssinia" — **Now:** "Abishai." Why: Ἀβεσσᾶ is Abishai (2 Sam 16:9–10), a person, not a country.
- M-44 — **Was:** "let the one avenging you rightly restrain you" — **Now:** "rightly calm the one avenging you." Why: imperative καταπράϋνε with σε as object of calming — subject inverted (180°).
- M-45 — **Was:** "the door of the mind" — **Now:** "the door of thumos." Why: θυμός is the technical term (irascible faculty), not "mind."
- M-46 — **Was:** "you will answer those who threaten" — **Now:** "you will answer those who threaten not at all." Why: οὐδ᾽ ὅλως ("not at all") was dropped — the whole point is *not* answering.
- M-47 — **Was:** "who then threaten and insult" — **Now:** "you will pain those who then threaten and insult." Why: ὀδυνήσεις (2nd sg. fut.) = "you will pain/grieve"; the verb became a relative clause.
- M-48 — **Was:** "so as to gain the praise of men from the inner part of the secluded one" — **Now:** "shake off the praise of men from within." Why: ἀποσείου (imperative) = "shake off" — 180° (gain ↔ shake off).
- M-49 — **Was:** "the litigious thought" — **Now:** "the ostentatious thought." Why: φιλεδείκτης = "showy, ostentatious."
- M-50 — **Was:** "being content" — **Now:** "self-satisfaction." Why: τὸ αὐτάρεσκον = "self-satisfaction" (the vice).
- M-51 — **Was:** "let no one magnify you" — **Now:** "having magnified yourself." Why: σε μεγαλύναν is a participle ("having magnified yourself"), not a jussive with a new subject.
- M-52 — **Was:** "Some of the brothers are insolent toward the pious" — **Now:** "One of the brothers was insulted by a pious one." Why: passive ὕβρισται — the victim became the aggressor, singular became plural (180° + number).
- M-53 — **Was:** "the one grief because… and the other joy because" — **Now:** "partly because… and partly because." Why: τὸ μὲν… τὸ δὲ is an adverbial pair, not two persons.
- M-54 — **Was:** "having been disturbed" — **Now:** "he disturbed." Why: ἐτάραξε is active — voice flip.
- M-55 — **Was:** "in the same state, the insolence not having been exhausted" — **Now:** "not inflamed unto the same grating of insults." Why: ξέσμα = "grating"; ἐξάπτω = "inflame" (not "exhausted").
- M-56 — **Was:** "may depart by breaking it" — **Now:** "when they depart we may speak boldly against." Why: τούτοις ἀπιοῦσι is a dative absolute ("when these depart").
- M-57 — **Was:** "of the insolent" — **Now:** "of the insults." Why: τῶν ὕβρεων is genitive of thing, not person.
- M-58 — **Was:** "the suggestion to bless only the one" — **Now:** "to call the solitary blessed." Why: τὸν μόνον μακαρίζειν is the monastic technical sense (praise the solitary life).
- M-59 — **Was:** "so that, having emptied long-suffering and having separated love" (trails off) — **Now:** "so that, having emptied us of long-suffering, they may separate us from love." Why: ἀφορίσωσιν is subjunctive of purpose ("they may separate").
- M-60 — **Was:** "fighting the passions" — **Now:** "fighting with thumos." Why: θυμομαχοῦντας (participle of θυμομαχέω) describes the beasts.
- M-61 — **Was:** "he who makes peace with those who are angry" — **Now:** "he who makes peace among the angry." Why: no preposition in the Greek (τοὺς ὀργιζομένους εἰρηνεύων).
- M-62 — **Was:** "but he who is of the same kind will be angered at him for being insolent" — **Now:** "but the anger of kinsmen will rage against him for his insolence toward him." Why: ὀργὴ ὁμογενῶν is the subject ("the anger of kinsmen"), not a person.
