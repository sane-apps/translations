Procopius of Gaza, a Christian sophist and head of the rhetorical school in Gaza, wrote this letter to a correspondent named Hieronymus. The letter belongs to a large surviving collection of his elaborately rhetorical letters, which provide most of what is known about his life. It was written from Gaza to Elusa in Palestine.

He then introduces Dionysus, who might be blamed for overlooking a poor man devoted to the Muses and Graces, a man who dreams of taxes even from his bedding.

In an imagined speech, Dionysus explains that one cannot be rich in both words and money. Procopius concludes that one should be content with present circumstances, since singing alone can make tax collectors immediately forget about money.
