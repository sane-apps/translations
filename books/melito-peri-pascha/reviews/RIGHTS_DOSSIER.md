# Melito of Sardis, On the Pascha (Peri Pascha) — rights dossier

Filed 2026-10-24 (Spark), owner directive of the same day. This file is the
auditable record behind the book's route. It supersedes the "genuinely
blocked" conclusion of TIMELINE_COPYRIGHT_WALL_REHUNT_2026-10-20.md §3 in one
respect only: that audit correctly found no usable existing text; this dossier
establishes why, and opens the project's own-translation route instead.

## 1. The work and what the library holds

- The work: Melito of Sardis, Peri Pascha (On the Pascha), a Greek paschal
  homily of c. 180 AD. Melito died in the 2nd century; the work itself has
  never been copyrightable in any modern term and no one claims otherwise.
- What the library holds today: NOTHING usable. No book folder existed before
  this filing; the work appears in the queue records only as "Melito skipped"
  and as the open Greek gap in Timeline dossier answer-pagans-jews (§96).
- Why the gap existed: the homily's text was lost for over a millennium. It
  survives because of 20th-century papyrus finds, principally P. Chester
  Beatty XII and P. Bodmer XIII (with later medieval manuscript witnesses).
  There is therefore no pre-modern printed edition, Greek or English, of the
  kind this library normally draws on (contrast Migne PG, which prints only
  the fragments, PG 5). Every edition and every translation is a 20th-century
  work.

Source: Catalog of Copyright Entries, Renewals, as digitized by Project

The homily on the Passion. SEE Melito, Saint, BP. of Sardis":

> The homily on the Passion by Melito, Bishop of Sardis, with some fragments
> of The apocryphal Ezekiel. Edited by Campbell Bonner. (Studies and
> documents, v.12) © 18Oct40; A147791. Campbell Bonner (A); 10Sep68; R442133.

- Original US registration: 18 Oct 1940, A147791.
- Renewal: registered 10 Sep 1968, R442133.
- Consequence: the renewed US copyright runs 95 years from 1940 publication;
  Bonner's edition and translation enter the US public domain on
  1 January 2036. Route "use Bonner" is closed until then. This also settles
  the question the 2026-10-20 audit left open ("revisit if a US renewal lapse
  for Bonner 1940 is ever established"): there was no lapse.

Note on the archive.org copy (item homilyonpassion0000meli): metadata records
the publisher as "Phila.: Univ. of Penn. Pr.", 1940 — the US co-publication
that made US registration and renewal operative. The copy is lending-restricted
in any event.

## 4. What circulates online, and why it is not a route

Full English texts of On Pascha are posted on devotional and study websites
(e.g. Crossroads Initiative; CCJR; several blogs, some crediting Crossroads).
Word-for-word comparison across those copies shows they reproduce a single
modern scholarly translation — the Hall 1979 rendering — posted without any
license. Downloading our site text or narration script from them would copy an
in-copyright translation. Not used, and not to be used.

A Scribd upload reproduces Hall's whole OECT volume (Greek, translation,
apparatus). Same bar: it is the in-copyright edition, and an infringing upload
does not launder it.

## 5. The lawful route the project takes (route b)

The Greek words are Melito's. What modern law protects in Bonner, Testuz,
Perler, or Hall is each editor's own contribution (selection of conjectures,
apparatus, translation wording) — not the 2nd-century text. The project
accordingly makes its own text and its own translation:

1. Transcribe the Greek from the manuscript witnesses' images: P. Chester
   Beatty XII (images via the Center for the Study of New Testament
   Manuscripts) and P. Bodmer XIII (images via the Fondation Martin Bodmer).
   Both sets are view-only; viewing and transcribing them is ordinary
   scholarly use, and the transcription is the project's own work. Precedent
   in this library: Gregory of Tours, Gloria Martyrum ch. 4 (2026-10-20),
   transcribed from page images and translated fresh.
2. Collate the transcription against Hall (1979) and Perler (SC 123) in print
   to catch transcription errors. Divergences are logged in reviews/. The
   printed texts are consulted, never copied: wording, punctuation, and
   paragraphing of the project text are the transcriber's.
3. Lacunae: bracketed in the transcription and flagged in the translation.
   A reading is adopted only where a witness supports it; editors'
   conjectural supplements are not imported silently. Where the papyri are
   simply lost, the translation says so.
4. Translate fresh at the pipeline standard, with the usual review pass.
