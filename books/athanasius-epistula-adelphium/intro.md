Athanasius of Alexandria (c. 293/298–373) wrote this letter to Adelphius, a bishop and confessor.

The occasion is a dispute over worship. Athanasius praises Adelphius for exposing this new blasphemy and identifies it with the errors of Valentinus, Marcion, and Mani, earlier teachers who denied or divided the incarnation. The letter argues that Christians do not worship a creature but the Creator, the Word of God, who became flesh.

The Arians' objections are quoted and argued against throughout. Key terms include the Word, flesh, creature, Creator, temple, worship, incarnate presence, incarnation, make divine, and redemption.
