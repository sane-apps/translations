## u01-open
- **Was:** "Even if swift doom quenched the lamp of my life, robbing me of its light, still the demon, in the rage of his wickedness, had power enough to make my whole life gloomy. He made me a widow while a child was still being lifted to my breast, and I never saw a beautiful child playing before me, a sweet delight at parents' knees. So unbearable grief subdued me with the consuming firebrand of wasting. This tomb, host to strangers, received me from my family as I was completing twenty-three years. Here my aged parents wove their lament, tearing out their grey hair, and the chorus of noble brothers groaned, fondly remembering Anna's beauty and splendor."
- **Now:** "Even though swift doom quenched the lamp of my life and stripped my torch of its light, the evil spirit, in the frenzy of his malice, still managed to make my whole life wretched. He made me a widow when my breast had only begun to rise, and I never looked on a lovely child at play, a sweet delight to its parents, held in their arms. So unbearable grief overcame me with the fierce fire of wasting. In my twenty-third year this tomb, which hosts our family, took me in. Here my aged parents wove a lament for all to hear, tearing their grey hair, and the company of noble brothers groaned, fondly remembering the splendor of Anna's beauty."
- **Why:** θῆκέ με χήρην ἐγγὺς ἀειρομένου μοι μαζοῦ is "he made me a widow with my breast near to rising":
  Anna was widowed as a girl, before she was grown. The old English invented a nursing child ("while a child
  was still being lifted to my breast"); the next clause, οὐδ' ἐπὶ τέκνον ἴδον καλὸν ἀθυρόμενον, says she never
  saw a child of her own. Also: ἡδὺ τοκεῦσιν ἄγαλμα παραγκάς = "a sweet delight to its parents, held in their
  arms" (not "at parents' knees"); μαλερῷ τηκεδόνος πυρσῷ = "the fierce fire of wasting"; τύμβος ὅδε ξεινοδόκος
  γενέθλης ἡμετέρης = "this tomb, which hosts our family" (the family tomb); ἤρκεσε ... τελέσαι = "still managed
  to make"; ὁ δαίμων = "the evil spirit".
- Translator notes (justification): ἐγγὺς ἀειρομένου μοι μαζοῦ: literally 'with my breast near to rising', i.e. Anna was widowed as a very young girl, before she was fully grown. Earlier English read a nursing child into the phrase; the Greek names no child, and the next clause (οὐδ' ἐπὶ τέκνον ἴδον, 'nor did I look on a child') says she never had one. | ὁ δαίμων: the hostile spirit blamed for her misfortunes (death's malice), rendered 'the evil spirit'; not a pagan god. | τύμβος ὅδε ξεινοδόκος γενέθλης ἡμετέρης: ξεινοδόκος is 'host, receiver of guests'; with the genitive it is the family tomb that receives our family. | The two heading lines of the copy-text ('Fragmenta in epistulam ad Romanos'; ΑΡΕΘΑ ΔΙΑΚΟΝΟΥ, 'of Arethas the deacon') are edition headings, not part of the epigram, and are not translated.
