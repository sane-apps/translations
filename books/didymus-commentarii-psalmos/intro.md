Didymus the Blind (c. 313–c. 398) was a Christian teacher in Alexandria. He lost his sight at the age of four, before he learned to read, and he remained a layman all his life. Athanasius, bishop of Alexandria, made him head of the Alexandrian school. He taught there for about half a century, followed Origen, and opposed Arianism.

This English is his commentary on the Psalms, the work stored as Commentarii in Psalmos. It is not his separate book of fragments on the Psalms. He explains the biblical text from his teaching in Alexandria.

A reader meets discussions of the power of God and the nature of the Word, the distinction of divinity and humanity, the eternity of Christ's reign, the precious stones of virtue, hope, and the psalm as the voice of the Savior. Terms such as Word, right hand, and face of God carry theological weight in this commentary.
