- τὰ πρὶν θήρεθνα … πρόβατα 'the nations that were once wild beasts, now sheep' read as 'those once hunted by beasts'; ἀνορύκτως 'undug' read as 'without uprooting'; αἰτοῦμεν 'we ask' read as third person.

> She keeps a feast of feasts, holding in her midst the King of the angels as both bridegroom and king. She keeps the feast like a fruitful olive tree in the house of God, decking herself without shedding leaves. She keeps the feast like a spring lily of paradise, in whom Christ is the truly flourishing lily, the lily that does not merely bloom but saves the world. In her is Christ, the violet that truly heals the sufferings of the sick. In her is the vineyard that says, “I am the true vine” (John 15:1). In her is the olive tree that truly has mercy on those who hope in him. Planted in the house of the Lord, blossoming in springtime in his courts, we keep the feast as we watch the winter of the law withdraw. Keep the feast, Church of Christ, not in type, not in a bodily way, but dancing in a spiritual chorus. Keep the feast as you watch the downfall of idols and hold the rising of the churches. For with Paul I cry out to you with that sacred and great voice: “The old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new” (2 Corinthians 5:17), and indeed marvelous things. Therefore rejoice in the Lord, young people of Christ, rejoice! Rejoice, Church of Christ; be glad and make merry, daughter Zion. Kronos has fallen, because God the Word has become flesh, not from a man’s passion, but appearing in divine-human fashion from Mary. See the grace of the day! See the splendor of the feast!

See every tongue joined in one doxology! See every mouth as one mouth! See those once hunted by beasts now sheep within you! See the acclamation of the nations keeping feast, an imitation of the angels! See the harmony, the chorus of the angels! See the ranks like orders of angels! I see the hymns like the hymns of angels. I see the children like newborn lambs, saying to Christ, “Hosanna in the highest, blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord!” Clap your hands loudly with them and cry out with a bright and lion-like voice, speaking festal words and thanksgivings: “Here am I and the children whom God has given me” (Isaiah 8:18), to her who was once childless and unknown, a barren woman. Blessed is he who came, and indeed who comes, in the name of the Lord. "God is the Lord, and he has appeared to us" (Psalm 117:27 (118:27)). He who comes, whom no place contains; he who comes, whom no place holds. Blessed is he who comes and is not separated from the heavens. Blessed is he who comes as befits himself, and again who comes as befits God. Blessed is he who came to me on a colt in a figure, as on a cherubic colt.
