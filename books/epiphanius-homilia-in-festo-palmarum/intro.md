Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403) wrote this homily for the Christian congregation gathered to celebrate Palm Sunday. He was bishop of Salamis on Cyprus and addressed the Church as daughter Zion, calling the people assembled for the feast to rejoice. The homily was composed for the movable feast that commemorates Christ's triumphal entry into Jerusalem.

It presents the Palm Sunday entry as the Lord's royal coming, fulfilling prophecy and uniting heaven and earth in praise. The children's cry of Hosanna is shown to be a true confession of Christ's divinity, inspired by Christ himself, while the priests and Hebrews reject him with the cry Let him be crucified.

A reader should know that Hosanna is the Hebrew acclamation cried to Christ, and that the colt is the animal on which Christ rides into Jerusalem as on a throne.
