Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403) was bishop of Salamis, also called Constantia, on Cyprus. He wrote this homily in Greek for the Church, addressing the congregation as daughter Zion and as the people assembled for the feast. The occasion is Palm Sunday, the movable feast commemorating Christ's triumphal entry into Jerusalem, and the homily calls the whole Church to rejoice because Christ the King and Bridegroom comes again to Zion on a colt.

The homily presents the Palm Sunday entry as the Lord's royal coming, fulfilling prophecy and uniting heaven and earth in praise. A reader will meet Christ, who enters Jerusalem on a colt; Lazarus, whom Christ raised from the dead the day before; Mary the sister of Lazarus; David, the ancestor of Christ; the four evangelists Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John; and the apostle Paul, whose words 'the old things have passed away' are quoted. The children's cry of 'Hosanna in the highest, blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord' is shown to be a true confession of Christ's divinity, inspired by Christ himself, while the priests and Hebrews reject him and cry 'Let him be crucified.'

To follow the homily, a reader should know that 'Hosanna' is the Hebrew acclamation cried to Christ, that 'daughter Zion' is a prophetic title for the Church, and that the 'colt' is the animal on which Christ rides as on a throne. The author uses Septuagint book names and Psalm numbering, so citations are given in modern form with Septuagint Psalm numbering noted where it differs from the Hebrew.
