Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403) wrote this homily for the Church gathered to celebrate Palm Sunday. He was bishop of Salamis on Cyprus, and he addresses the congregation directly as daughter Zion and as the people assembled for the feast. The sermon was composed for the movable feast that commemorates Christ's triumphal entry into Jerusalem.

The homily calls the whole Church to rejoice because Christ the King and Bridegroom comes again to Zion on a colt, bringing salvation, light, and truth while error, demons, and the curse are undone. It presents the Palm Sunday entry as the Lord's royal coming, fulfilling prophecy and uniting heaven and earth in praise. The children's cry of 'Hosanna' is shown to be a true confession of Christ's divinity, inspired by Christ himself, while the priests and Hebrews reject him.

A reader should know that 'Hosanna' is the Hebrew acclamation cried to Christ, meaning 'Save now.' The 'colt' is the young donkey on which Christ rides into Jerusalem as on a throne. 'Daughter Zion' is a prophetic title for the Church called to rejoice at the King's coming. The homily quotes the children's acclamation, the evangelist, prophets, and the hostile cry of the Hebrews, 'Let him be crucified.' It closes by affirming that the one seated on the colt is the eternal Creator and Lord of all, who remains in the Father's bosom and rules all creation.
