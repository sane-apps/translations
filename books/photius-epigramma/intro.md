This epigram is attributed to Photius of Constantinople (c. 810–c. 893), the ninth-century patriarch, though the manuscript tradition notes that others assign it to Leo the Philosopher, a Byzantine scholar. It is addressed to a friend who wishes to live chastely, and it serves as a verse preface commending the novel Leucippe and Clitophon by Achilles Tatius to such a reader.

The poem presents the story of Clitophon as displaying bitter love but a chaste life. It singles out Leucippe, whose most chaste life amazes everyone because she endured beating, shearing, abuse, and three deaths. The speaker urges the reader not to dwell on the incidental spectacle of the writing but to attend first to the movement of the story, which escorts those who love wisely to the bridal chamber.

A reader should know that the key term rendered here as chaste translates the Greek word for self-controlled or temperate, and it is the central virtue the epigram celebrates. The word story translates the Greek term for the narrative or plot, as distinct from the writing, whose incidental spectacle the reader is told not to dwell on. No biblical citations or scriptural conventions are used in this epigram.
