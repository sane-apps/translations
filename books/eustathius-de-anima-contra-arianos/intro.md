Eustathius of Antioch (c. 320–337) wrote this treatise against Arian teachers who claimed that Christ took a body without a human soul. He was bishop of Beroea before becoming bishop of Antioch shortly before the Council of Nicaea in 325. He wrote to defend the full humanity of Christ and to show that the Word was truly united to a human soul as well as a body.

The Arians taught that the divine Word assumed a soulless body, so that all the changes and sufferings of Christ could be attributed to the divine Spirit. Their goal was to persuade people that what is changeable could not be born from the unchangeable nature, and so to deny that the Son is begotten from the Father's unchangeable nature. Eustathius answers by pointing to Christ's infancy, growth, suffering, death, and resurrection as proof of a real human body with a soul.

A reader will meet Christ, the thief crucified with him, the blessed Stephen, Herod, and the prophets Jeremiah, Moses, and Samuel. The treatise uses the promise to the thief on the cross to show that Christ's human soul, united to the Word, led the thief's soul into paradise while Christ's body still lay in the tomb. Key terms include soul, Word, tabernacle, body, passion, resurrection, paradise, changeable, unchangeable nature, and Spirit. Old Testament names appear in their Greek forms, such as Mōysēs for Moses, Samouēl for Samuel, and Hieremias for Jeremiah, and Psalm numbering follows the Septuagint.
