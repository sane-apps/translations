Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote these fragments on Jeremiah. They take up idolatry, human wisdom, the Spirit, and the refusal of the law.

The mountains and hills had temples for demons. The prophet looks for one faithful man. The wise of this world, the logicians and the poets, are called foolish, and the wisdom of this world is foolishness before God.

The Spirit is compared to fire. John baptizes with water, and Christ says he came to bring fire on the earth. The last fragment turns to Ammon, Egypt, and Idumea, and to a Babylon that is named Jerusalem, and it says they refuse to honor the law.
