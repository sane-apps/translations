Apollinaris of Laodicea (c. 310–c. 390) was a bishop in Syria, a teacher of rhetoric, and a skilled reader of Hebrew. His full commentaries are lost, but these fragments survive in later collections of excerpts from early Christian writers.

The work is a commentary, not a continuous treatise. Apollinaris moves through individual psalms, explaining their prophetic and Christological meaning. He often reads David's words as spoken in the person of Christ, especially in psalms of suffering, and he sees the church gathered from the nations as the bride and kingdom of Christ. He also argues against views that diminished the full divinity of the Son and the Spirit, insisting that the one God's creative and saving work is indivisibly Trinitarian.

The prophets Isaiah and Jeremiah are quoted, as is the apostle Paul. Apollinaris also compares the Greek translations of Symmachus, Aquila, and Theodotion. Key terms include church, Christ, resurrection, mercy, righteousness, Spirit, kingdom, bride, nations, Israel, law, death, life, salvation, and sin. Old Testament books appear under their older names, such as the first of Kingdoms for 1 Samuel and the second of Esdras for Nehemiah.
