## u01-open

- Old: and I made a memory when he fell away from you and I looked after him when he was brought down into weakness
  New: and you remembered him when he fell away from you, and you looked after him when he was brought down into weakness
  Why: μνήμην ἐποιήσω … ἐπεσκέψω are second person ('you remembered … you looked after'), not 'I'.

- Old: But, he says, you who are the maker of the works of a human being who is altogether small care for him,
  New: But, he says, you, the maker of such great works, care for a human being who is altogether small,
  Why: ὁ τῶν τηλικούτων ποιητὴς ἔργων μικροῦ παντελῶς ὄντος ἀνθρώπου φροντίζεις: 'maker of such great works' cares for a very small human being. τηλικούτων had been dropped and the genitive misattached.

## u01-rem-close

- Old: “So that my glory may be praised to you, and I will not regret.”
  New: “So that my glory may sing praise to you, and I may not be pierced with sorrow.”
  Why: Ὅπως ἂν ψάλῃ σοι ἡ δόξα μου καὶ οὐ μὴ κατανυγῶ: active 'may sing praise to you'; κατανυγῶ is 'be pierced (with sorrow)', not 'regret'.

- Old: For we obtain what comes from God in this way, as he says:
  New: For in this way we human beings obtain what comes from God: Christ, on our behalf, is the one who receives the good meant for us, as he says:
  Why: The clause ὅτι Χριστὸς ἀνθ' ἡμῶν ὁ τὸ εἰς ἡμᾶς ἀγαθὸν ὑποδεχόμενος had been dropped.

## u01-rem-early

- Old: when the mind occupies itself with prayer and the invocations slip into the bosom of the soul.
  New: when the invocations linger in the mind during prayer and slip into the bosom of the soul.
  Why: ὅτε ἐνσχολάζουσι τῇ διανοίᾳ κατὰ τῆς εὐχῆς: the subject is the invocations (3rd plural), and τῇ διανοίᾳ is 'in the mind'.

- Old: foreseeing him coming for the punishment of the unjust
  New: foreseeing him coming from the punishment of the unjust
  Why: ἐκ τιμωρίας τῶν ἀδίκων ἐρχόμενον: he comes back from the punishment (Isa 63:1, from Edom), not 'for' it.

- Old: if I will lie to David (Psalm 88:36 (89:36)).
  New: if I will lie to David (Psalm 88:36 (89:35)).
  Why: LXX Ps 88:36 = English 89:35.

## u01-rem-mid

- Old: he bore the false accusations without resentment
  New: I bore the false accusations without resentment
  Why: ἀνεξικάκως ἔφερον is first person, as are ἐσιώπων and the rest of the verse.

- Old: and taking their sins upon himself
  New: and taking what is theirs upon himself
  Why: τὰ ἐκείνων εἰς ἑαυτὸν ἄγων: 'their things'; 'sins' was an interpretive addition.

- Old: but I wait expecting you and what is yours,
  New: but I wait, expecting what is with you and what is yours,
  Why: παρὰ σοὶ καὶ τὰ σὰ προσδοκῶν: what is with you and yours is expected, not God as object.

- Old: In David's case this was also fulfilled bodily, that they will strike David with the very sword they had drawn against David.
  New: In David's case this was also fulfilled bodily: David struck with their own sword, the one they had drawn against David (cf. 1 Samuel 17:51).
  Why: τὸ τῇ ἰδίᾳ μαχαίρᾳ παίσειν τὸν Δαυὶδ ἣν κατὰ τοῦ Δαυὶδ ἐσπάσαντο: τὸν Δαυίδ is the subject of παίσειν. The point, as with the wicked slain by their own sword, is that David struck with the enemy's own sword (Goliath). The English had made David the victim.

## u02-open

- Old: These things are understood concerning the future and the promises according to the law, referring back to what was said above, and also what was said afterward by Paul:
  New: These things are intelligible concerning the future and the promises according to the law, and they reach higher, to what was said afterward by Paul [the text is corrupt here]:
  Why: ἀνωτέρω χωροῦντα is 'reaching higher', not 'referring back to what was said above'. The crux † προφήτας ὧν is now marked instead of passed over silently. συνετά = 'intelligible'.

- Old: (Psalm 89:15 [90:14])
  New: (Psalm 89:14 [90:14])
  Why: 'Fill us in the morning with your mercy' is LXX Ps 89:14 (English 90:14).

- Old: “His life was taken from the earth” (Psalm 40 [41])
  New: “His life was taken from the earth” (Isaiah 53:8)
  Why: Ἤρθη ἀπὸ γῆς ἡ ζωὴ αὐτοῦ is Isa 53:8 LXX (Acts 8:33), quoted while commenting on Ps 40, not a verse of Ps 40.

## u02-rem-close

- Old: Therefore those whom he called young maidens he now says are fathers, having received the rank of sons
  New: Therefore those young maidens whom he said were fathers he now calls sons, since they have received the rank of sons
  Why: ἃς εἶπεν νεάνιδας πατέρας εἶναι, νῦν υἱοὺς λέγει: he now calls them sons (Ps 44:17), not 'now says are fathers'.

- Old: the words of grace that came from his mouth (Psalm 44 (45):8-9).
  New: the words of grace that came from his mouth (cf. Luke 4:22).
  Why: The parenthesis followed a Gospel allusion (πάντας θαυμάζειν ἐπὶ τοῖς λόγοις τῆς χάριτος). 'Ps 44,8bc' is the edition's next lemma heading, not a reference for this sentence.

- Old: so that through him he might show us, who are led to participation in the Spirit through kinship according to the body.
  New: so that he might show us as led from him to participation in the Spirit through kinship according to the body.
  Why: ἵνα εἰς ἡμᾶς ἐξ αὐτοῦ πρὸς τὴν μετοχὴν τοῦ πνεύματος ἀγομένους δείξῃ: ἐξ αὐτοῦ goes with ἀγομένους, 'led from him'.

- Old: Not even when you were handed over to the nations, he says, by God, were you handed over in vain,
  New: Not even when you were handed over to the nations, he says, abandoned by God, were you handed over in vain,
  Why: ἐξεδόθητε τοῖς ἔθνεσιν ἐαθέντες ὑπὸ θεοῦ: ὑπὸ θεοῦ goes with ἐαθέντες ('left, abandoned'), not with the handing over.

## u02-rem-early

- Old: “It is not for your sake that I work victory, O house of Israel, but for the sake of my name” (Ezekiel 36:22).
  New: “It is not for your sake that I act, O house of Israel [the printed text reads νῖκος, 'victory', for οἶκος, 'house'], but for the sake of my name” (Ezekiel 36:22).
  Why: Δι' ὑμᾶς ἐγὼ οὐ ποιῶ νῖκος Ἰσραήλ: the English rendered both 'victory' and 'house', merging the printed νῖκος with the LXX οἶκος Ἰσραήλ (Ezek 36:22). The edition's reading is now marked instead of conflated.

- Old: (Psalm 12 (13):2)
  New: (Psalm 12:2 (13:1))
  Why: Ἕως πότε, κύριε, ἐπιλήσῃ μου εἰς τέλος is LXX Ps 12:2 = English 13:1; the reference had paired the LXX verse with the English chapter.

- Old: (Psalm 29 (30):10)
  New: (Psalm 29:10 (30:9))
  Why: Τίς ὠφέλεια ἐν τῷ αἵματί μου is LXX Ps 29:10 = English 30:9.

- Old: (Psalm 17 (18):2)
  New: (Psalm 17:2 (18:1))
  Why: Ἀγαπήσω σε, κύριε ἡ ἰσχύς μου is LXX Ps 17:2 = English 18:1.

- Old: I would not have gone about in gloom or be oppressed by the unjust.
  New: I would not be going about in gloom and being oppressed by the unjust.
  Why: σκυθρωπὸς ἂν διῆγον καὶ … ἐθλιβόμην: imperfects with ἄν, a present contrary-to-fact (οὐ negates the apodosis, the participle ὤν being the protasis).

## u04-rem-close

- Old: the fear of the coming of Christ the judge from heaven
  New: the fear of the coming of the judge from heaven
  Why: τῆς ἐξ οὐρανοῦ παρουσίας τοῦ κριτοῦ: the Greek says 'the judge', and 'Christ' was an addition.

- Old: so that for the sake of their destruction some turning back to God on the part of the nation comes about.
  New: so that on account of their destruction some turning of God toward the nation comes about.
  Why: θεοῦ τινα εἰς τὸ γένος ἐπιστροφήν: the turning is God's, toward the nation (cf. Ps 79:4/8 'Turn us'), not the nation's toward God.

- Old: Since such things as they suffered from the Assyrians have come upon them for now because of their abandonment, he asks that he who is not present come to them.
  New: Such were the things they suffered from the Assyrians; and he asks that the one who for the present is not with them, because of their abandonment, should come to them.
  Why: οἷα πέπονθεν ὑπὸ Ἀσσυρίων ἐλθεῖν τέως, οὐ παρόντα, αὐτοῖς ἀξιοῖ διὰ τὴν ἐγκατάλειψιν: τέως and διὰ τὴν ἐγκατάλειψιν qualify God's present absence (οὐ παρόντα), the object of ἀξιοῖ … ἐλθεῖν; the English had attached them to the sufferings.

- Old: (Psalm 119:36)
  New: (Psalm 118:36 (119:36))
  Why: LXX numbering first, English in parentheses, as elsewhere in the work.

- Old: about which the previously mentioned psalms made their requests to God.
  New: about which the previously mentioned psalms made their requests to God. [The rest follows in agreement with the preceding psalm.]
  Why: The edition's bracketed note [τὰ δ' ἑξῆς τῷ πρὸ αὐτοῦ ἀκολούθως.] had been dropped; now rendered, as the parallel Theodoret note is in u05.

## u04-rem-early

- Old: “You led your people like sheep by the hand of Moses and Aaron” (Psalm 77:52).
  New: “You led your people like sheep by the hand of Moses and Aaron” (Psalm 76:21 (77:20)).
  Why: Ὡδήγησας ὡς πρόβατα τὸν λαόν σου ἐν χειρὶ Μωυσῆ καὶ Ἀαρών is LXX Ps 76:21 (Eng 77:20), the closing verse of this psalm. Ps 77:52 is a different verse.

- Old: hail came down upon the Canaanites
  New: hail came down also upon the Canaanites

## u04-rem-mid

- Old: that I led the people on their journey out of Egypt
  New: that you led the people on their journey out of Egypt
  Why: καθηγήσω is second person ('you led').

- Old: yet they are distinguished before God.
  New: yet they are distinguished before God. Such was the case of Jeremiah, taken captive among the ungodly, yet holding some distinction even among men after the captivity, in the honor he received from the one who took him captive.
  Why: The sentence οἷον ἦν τὸ τοῦ Ἱερεμίου … ἐν τῇ τιμῇ τῇ παρὰ τοῦ αἰχμαλωτίσαντος had been dropped.

- Old: —Aquila says "righteous and impious"—"and I will cut off from you righteous and impious"
  New: —Aquila says "righteous and impious"; the Septuagint, "and I will cut off from you righteous and impious"
  Why: The Greek has the siglum Ο (ο', the Septuagint) before καὶ ἐκκόψω ἐκ σοῦ δίκαιον καὶ ἀσεβῆ; the English had dropped it, merging the two readings.

- Old: —Aquila says "moist," and "every dry tree"
  New: —Aquila says "moist"; the Septuagint, "and every dry tree"
  Why: Ο καὶ πᾶν ξύλον ξηρόν: restores the Septuagint siglum.

## u05-open

- Old: For the Lord promised God’s righteousness on behalf of humanity
  New: For the Lord brought in God’s righteousness on behalf of humanity
  Why: ἐπηγάγετο is 'brought in, introduced', not 'promised'.

- Old: and Korah was the son of Kohath,
  New: and Korah was the grandson of Kohath,
  Why: ὁ δὲ Κορὲ τοῦ Καὰθ υἱωνός: υἱωνός means 'grandson' (Korah son of Izhar son of Kohath).

- Old: He sings a most lordly song, the world-saving sufferings of the Savior. For he dances
  New: He sings a most lordly song. For he dances
  Why: ᾠδὴν ᾄδει κυριωτάτην. χορεύει γάρ …: the phrase 'the world-saving sufferings' belongs only to the later clause ᾄδει δὲ τὰ πάθη τοῦ σωτῆρος τὰ κοσμοσωτήρια, which the English already renders. It had been duplicated into the first clause.

## u05-rem-close

- Old: and in his second coming he will reign by destroying evil and corruption through each of those who share in him,
  New: and in his second coming he will reign through each one of those who share in him, having destroyed evil and corruption,
  Why: ἐν τῇ δευτέρᾳ βασιλεύσει διὰ τῶν καθ' ἕκαστον τῶν μετόχων αὐτοῦ λελυκὼς τὸ κακὸν καὶ τὴν φθοράν: he reigns through each partaker; λελυκώς is perfect ('having destroyed').

## u05-rem-early

- Old: mercy preparing human beings for the salvation of the fearful judge who is about to appear.
  New: as the fearful judge who is about to appear prepares human beings for salvation by mercy.
  Why: Genitive absolute: the judge (τοῦ μέλλοντος ἐπιφανήσεσθαι φοβεροῦ κριτοῦ) prepares people for salvation by mercy (ἐλέῳ); not 'salvation of the judge'.

- Old: Another ancient interpreter, explaining
  New: Another, explaining
  Why: Ἄλλος φησί: simply 'another'; 'ancient interpreter' was added.

- Old: but is a true image and mark of God, God alone accomplishing all things through it.
  New: but is uniquely the true image and mark of God, God accomplishing all things through it.
  Why: ἀλλ' εἰκὼν ἀληθινὴ καὶ γνώρισμα τοῦ θεοῦ μόνον, πάντα δι' αὐτῆς ἐπιτελοῦντος θεοῦ: μόνον qualifies the image/mark clause, not God's accomplishing.

## u05-rem-mid

- Old: If he has spoken this way, it is clear
  New: If he has spoken this way about a thousand years, it is clear
  Why: περὶ χιλίων ἐτῶν had been dropped.

- Old: As Paul also says, "Is it not possible that the word of God has failed?" (cf. Romans 9:6).
  New: As Paul also says, "It is not as though the word of God has failed" (cf. Romans 9:6).
  Why: Οὐχ οἷόν τε ὅτι ἐκπέπτωκεν ὁ λόγος τοῦ θεοῦ is Paul's denial ('It is not as though…', Rom 9:6). The English had turned it into a question suggesting the word might have failed, which reverses the point (the promise 'will be accomplished and not fail').

- Old: instead of "By my holiness" (Psalm 89:36).
  New: instead of "By my holiness" (Psalm 88:36 (89:35)). [On Psalm 88:39–46 (89:38–45): Theodoret set out these things in the same way as Apollinaris.]
  Why: Ὤμοσα … ἐν τῷ ἁγίῳ μου is LXX Ps 88:36 (Eng 89:35); the English had given 89:36. The edition's bracketed note under fragment 153 ([Ταῦτα μὲν ὁ Θεοδώρητος ὁμοίως Ἀπολιναρίῳ ἐξέθετο.]) had been dropped and is now rendered.

- Old: (Psalm 90:10)
  New: (Psalm 89:10 (90:10))
  Why: Fragment is on LXX Ps 89:10; LXX numbering first, English in parentheses.

## u06-open

## u06-rem-close

- Old: because the fleshly nature is undone by the spirit, and by its own pleasure and ease it obliterates the good that belongs to that other.
  New: because the fleshly nature ravages the spiritual and by its own pleasure and ease obliterates the good that belongs to the other.
  Why: τῆς κατὰ σάρκα φύσεως τὴν κατὰ πνεῦμα λυμαινομένης: the fleshly nature ravages the spiritual; the agency had been reversed.

- Old: although the incense offering was more impious
  New: although the making of the calf was more impious
  Why: μοσχοποιΐα is the making of the (golden) calf, not an incense offering.

- Old: and the Father saves through Christ.
  New: and the Father is Savior through Christ.
  Why: πατὴρ διὰ Χριστοῦ is verbless; 'is Savior' carries over the predicate instead of adding a new verb.

- Old: For the covering is a shadow, the humiliation is poverty, and the necessity is iron.
  New: For “shadow” is what covers, “poverty” is the humiliation, and “iron” is the necessity.
  Why: σκιὰ μὲν γὰρ τὸ καλύπτον, πενία δὲ ἡ ταπεινότης, σίδηρος δὲ ἡ ἀνάγκη glosses the psalm's words (Ps 106:10 σκιᾷ … πτωχείᾳ καὶ σιδήρῳ); the English had reversed lemma and gloss.

  Why: μηδὲ τὸ πικρὸν καὶ ὀλέθριον αὐτοῖς εἰς πέρας ἐκβῆναι καὶ συγχωρηθῆναι κρατῆσαι: 'come to fulfillment' had been dropped, and 'but that they be forgiven and restrained' was invented.

  New: Psalm 139:9 (140:8).

- Old: that no thought be given to lawlessness, no transgression against righteousness,

## u10-rem-early

- Old: distinguishing the nature and power of each one as easily as one distinguishes names.
  New: since he distinguishes the nature and power of each one as by names that mark them off.
  Why: διακρίνοντι καθάπερ ὀνόματα τῶν διορισμῶν: God distinguishes each as by names of their distinctions (Ps 146:4); 'as easily as one' was an addition.

- Old: For this is a provision both for life and for existing eternally,
  New: For this is a provision both for true life and for existing eternally,
  Why: εἰς τὴν ὄντως ζωήν: ὄντως ('true/real life') had been dropped.

- Old: but he will make all people equal in the endless age,
  New: but he will do the same for all people [...] into the endless age,
  Why: πάντας ἀνθρώπους τὸ ἴσον ποιήσειν *** εἰς τὸν ἄπειρον αἰῶνα: the edition marks a lacuna (***), now shown as [...] per the work's convention; τὸ ἴσον ποιήσειν is 'do the same/like', not 'make equal'.

- Old: if we are joined to life and to the one who truly is
  New: if we are joined to true life and to the one who truly is
  Why: εἰ πρὸς τὴν ὄντως ζωὴν συναπτόμεθα: ὄντως ('true life') had been dropped.

## u10-rem-mid

- Old: since his power and wisdom come and go.
  New: since he has power and wisdom that come and go.
  Why: προσγινόμενόν τε καὶ ἀπογινόμενον ἔχων τὸ δυνατὸν καὶ σοφόν: ἔχων makes the human agent the subject, who has power and wisdom that come and go.
