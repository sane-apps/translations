> …considering this equal to that saying, “Cease from your wicked deeds” (Isaiah 1:16).
> (Psalm 98:5) He says to worship the footstool, the worldwide authority that will then appear, which rules not only the sanctified race but all things. It is allegorized as God’s footstool, occupying the whole inhabited earth, which is itself also called a footstool by the same name, just as Israel too has been called an authority. The throne that is above the authority would be allegorized as God’s kinship with what is sanctified—above all with the heavenly things, but already also with the things on earth. By the same name, according to Isaiah, heaven is allegorized as a throne, and Jerusalem is also a throne because of the saints in her, in the manner of heaven. This is proof that heaven is not called a throne in a bodily sense, nor the earth a footstool in a bodily sense, since we say that Jerusalem, a small part of the earth, is a throne, and that the authority set over those who are ruled is a footstool. Just as God’s throne in heaven is said to be the kinship that attends the heavenly beings as holy—heaven itself being called God’s throne by the same name—so also the Lord calls those who received the good seed of the kingdom “good seed,” by the same name. So he commands us to worship not the earth that is under authority but the authority over it, as we perceive it in its works.
