Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444), bishop of Alexandria, wrote this letter to the emperor Theodosius. His purpose is to affirm one Christ from two natures and to reject any mixing or confusion of the divine Word with the flesh.

The letter is a confession of faith. Cyril states that the only-begotten Son of God is perfect God, consubstantial with the Father in divinity and consubstantial with us in humanity. He explains that a union of natures has occurred, so that Christians confess one Christ, one Son, one Lord. He illustrates this with the human composition of soul and body, where two natures form one human being, not two.

A reader will meet Cyril speaking in a first-person plural confession. He also quotes an objection from opponents, introduced by the Greek word ἐροῦσιν, and identifies them as those on the opposite side. No scripture is cited in this letter.
