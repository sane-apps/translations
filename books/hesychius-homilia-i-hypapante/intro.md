This homily was written by Hesychius of Jerusalem (c. 412–450), a presbyter of the Church of Jerusalem known for his biblical commentary and preaching. He delivered it for the feast of the Presentation, when the Church commemorates the infant Christ being brought to the Temple and met by Simeon and Anna. Hesychius wrote to celebrate that feast and to explain why Christ, though above the Law, submitted to its purification rites.

The homily was preached on 14 February at the Martyrion, the church at Golgotha in Jerusalem. Hesychius presents the Presentation as the feast of feasts, recapitulating the whole mystery of Christ's incarnation. He argues that Christ, as the lawgiver, submitted to the Law's purification rites for our sake, not because Mary or the child needed cleansing. The reader will meet Mary, Joseph, Simeon, and Anna, and will hear the Gospel of Luke quoted at length, along with the prophets Ezekiel and Zephaniah.

A reader should know that the feast is called the Presentation, and that the purification rites are those prescribed by the Law of Moses after childbirth. Simeon's words, the Nunc Dimittis, are quoted directly. The homily closes by interpreting the sign of contradiction as the cross, which causes the fall of the faithless and the rising of those who turn to faith. Zephaniah is a prophet who speaks of God searching Jerusalem with a lamp. Annas and Caiaphas are the high priests warned not to fear for their priesthood. Cleopas is one of the disciples on the road to Emmaus in Luke 24.
