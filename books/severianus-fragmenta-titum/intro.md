Severianus of Gabala (fl. c. 400) is the author of these fragments on Titus. They are not Chrysostom's homilies on Titus, and they are not Oecumenius's fragments on that letter.

Paul quotes the Cretan poet's line about liars, beasts, and gluttons. Severianus says Paul uses it only for the Cretans' character, and not for the poet's claim about a tomb of Zeus. He then takes up the sharp rebuke, and the line that to the pure all things are pure, because God made all things pure.

The fragments also say that Paul calls women to a share in the clerical inheritance, since he calls them reverent. Integrity belongs to the mind, and dignity to the work. The citations use the old names of the biblical books.
