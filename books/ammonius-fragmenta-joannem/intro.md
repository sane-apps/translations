Ammonius of Alexandria (c. 5th cent.) was a presbyter of the church in Alexandria who wrote biblical commentaries. This work, preserved only in fragments within later chains of quotations from church fathers, addresses Christian readers seeking commentary on the Gospel of John. He wrote to explain the Gospel's teaching on Christ's divinity, the Trinity, and the incarnation while answering doctrinal controversies of his time.

The fragments survive as verse-by-verse comments on the Gospel of John, gathered from catenas. Ammonius wrote in a period of sharp theological dispute, and his comments directly oppose Arianism, Sabellianism, and Manichaeism. He defends the full divinity and consubstantiality of the Son with the Father, insists that the Word's incarnation was real rather than apparent, and teaches that Christ's flesh is life-giving.

A reader will meet Jesus Christ as the incarnate Word, along with John the Baptist, Peter, Judas Iscariot, Mary Magdalene, Thomas, Nicodemus, the Samaritan woman, Martha, Mary of Bethany, Lazarus, Pilate, Caiaphas, Philip, and Andrew. Ammonius quotes the words of Jesus, John the Baptist, the Jews who dispute with Jesus, and the evangelist John himself, and he also quotes and argues against heretics. To follow the work, know that "consubstantial" means sharing the same essence, "person" means a distinct member of the Trinity, "divine plan" means God's saving work through the incarnation, and "Word" means the second person of the Trinity who became flesh. Old Testament books are cited from the Greek Septuagint, so Psalm numbering follows the Greek rather than the Hebrew.
