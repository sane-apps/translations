Serapion of Antioch (fl. c. 190–211) wrote this letter to the church at Rhossus, a Christian community in Syria. He was the eighth bishop of Antioch and served as a pastor concerned with the sound teaching of the churches under his care. He wrote to correct an earlier decision he had made during a visit to Rhossus, when he had allowed the community to read a gospel attributed to the apostle Peter.

The occasion for the letter was Serapion's discovery that the gospel being read at Rhossus was being used to support Docetic teaching, which denied the true humanity of Christ. He had initially assumed the church held right faith and had permitted the reading. After learning that the community's mind was harboring heresy, he now writes to set matters right and announces his plan to return to them soon. He explains that he obtained the gospel from the successors of those who had originated it, whom he calls Docetists, and found that most of it agrees with the true teaching of the Savior, but some things have been added.

A reader should know that Serapion distinguishes between the apostles themselves, whom he and the church receive as they receive Christ, and writings falsely attributed to them, which he calls forgeries. The letter refers to another document written to the Rhossus community, but its contents are unknown.
