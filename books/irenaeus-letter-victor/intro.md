Irenaeus of Lyons (c. 130–c. 202) wrote this letter to Victor, bishop of Rome. Irenaeus was bishop of Lyons in Gaul, but he had grown up in Asia Minor and had known Polycarp of Smyrna, giving him a direct connection to the apostolic generation. He wrote during a dispute over the day and manner of the Paschal fast, urging Victor not to break communion with churches that observed a different custom.

The occasion was Victor's decision to excommunicate the Asian churches, often called Quartodecimans, over the date of Easter. Irenaeus protested this decision. He argued that differences over the fast were long-standing and did not break the unity of faith. He reminded Victor that earlier Roman bishops, though they did not observe the Asian custom themselves, had lived in peace with those who did and had even sent the Eucharist to them.

A reader will meet Polycarp, bishop of Smyrna and a disciple of John the apostle, who observed the Asian custom. Irenaeus recounts Polycarp's meeting in Rome with Anicetus, an earlier bishop of Rome. The two men disagreed on the fast but remained in communion, and Anicetus yielded the Eucharist to Polycarp as a sign of respect. Irenaeus also lists earlier Roman bishops who did not observe the Asian fast: Xystus, Telesphorus, Hyginus, Pius, and those before Soter, who was Victor's immediate predecessor. The letter closes with a warning that anyone who can do good to a neighbor and fails to do so will be considered a stranger to the love of the Lord.
