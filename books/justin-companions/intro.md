The work is anonymous and is dated here to (c. 165). It names no addressee. It was written to preserve for the faithful the interrogation, confession, condemnation and death at Rome of Christians arrested for refusing orders to sacrifice to idols and brought before the Roman prefect Rusticus.

Justin is ordered to obey the gods and the emperors but answers that obedience to Jesus Christ is blameless, summarizing Christian belief in one creator God and in Christ foretold by the prophets. Pressed about meetings, he says God is not confined to place and that he teaches the words of truth to visitors at his lodging in Rome above the bathhouse of Martinus son of Timothinus. The reader meets Rusticus who questions and sentences, Justin the teacher and chief speaker, and Chariton, Charito, Euelpistus, Hierax, Paeon and Liberianus, each declaring himself or herself a Christian and explaining that loyalty as from parents, from Christ, or by command and gift of God. The narrator frames direct speech by Rusticus, by Justin in reply, by each companion in turn, and finally by the martyrs together as they refuse to move from true worship to impiety, affirm hope of heavenly reward and salvation at the judgment seat of Christ, and are scourged and beheaded and then buried by fellow believers.

In this rendering Christian means a person loyal to Christ and to the one God, God means the one creator contrasted with idols, true worship means right loyalty to God, idol means a man made cult object, and offer sacrifice means the demanded cultic act the prisoners refuse. Teaching covers both reasoned account and held belief, while confession means the open declaration I am a Christian, witness means that public confession sealed by suffering and death, and salvation means rescue through suffering for Christ to heavenly dwelling. No Bible book is named or quoted word for word. There is only a general appeal to what was foretold by the prophets about the coming of Christ among humans.
