Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote this commentary on Daniel. He served as Patriarch of Alexandria from 412 to 444, succeeding his uncle Theophilus, and is best known for his campaign against Nestorius of Constantinople. The surviving fragments belong to his extensive exegetical writings, which fill seven of the ten volumes of his works in Migne's Patrologia series.

The fragments comment on Daniel's vision of the Ancient of Days and the Son of Man. Cyril interprets the boast of the little horn as arrogance against God's glory and against the ruler set over those from Israel, as though that ruler could not benefit his own people. He explains the opening of the books at the incarnation: the Father opened the books when the only Son came in human form, releasing sinners from judgment and enrolling the courageous among the heavenly choirs.

A reader will meet God the Father, called the Ancient of Days, and the only Son, who came in human form. Cyril asks whether the Son's coming to the Ancient of Days is spatial, and rejects this as foolish because the divine is not in a place but fills all things. He answers that the Son came to the Father's glory, as shown by the words that to him was given honor and kingdom, and by the Father's invitation to sit at my right hand.
