Athanasius of Alexandria (c. 293/298–373) was bishop of Alexandria and a leading defender of Nicene orthodoxy against Arianism in the fourth century. He wrote these notes on select verses of Job, using the text to refute Arian claims that the Son of God is a creature and to explain how created beings, angels, and the devil relate to God's unbounded nature.

The work engages directly with the Arians, who argued from Scripture's use of 'made' that the Son is a created being. Athanasius counters that the same language is used for natural children, citing Hezekiah's words in Isaiah. He then explains that created things, including angels and the devil, are located and changeable, while God alone is unbounded and unchangeable. The devil deceives by disguising himself, but Christ has exposed his illusions.

A reader will meet Job, the devil, Job's wife, Eve, Hezekiah, Moses, the apostles, and the Jews, along with quoted words from Scripture and from God speaking to the devil. Key terms include 'creature' for a thing made, 'offspring' for what is begotten, 'unbounded' for God's nature, 'endurance' for steadfastness under trial, and 'illusion' for the devil's deceptive appearance. Athanasius interprets Job's cosmic language about mountains, the shaking of heaven, and the stars as referring to Christ's renewal of the old covenant and the events of his passion.
