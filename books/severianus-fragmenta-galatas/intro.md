Severianus of Gabala (fl. c. 400) was bishop of Gabala in Syria and a well-known preacher in Constantinople. These fragments come from a commentary he wrote on Paul's Letter to the Galatians. They are not the fragments of Eusebius of Emesa. The work is addressed to Christian readers, especially those tempted to keep the Mosaic law, and it aims to counter the view that Gentile believers should live as Jews.

The commentary survives only in fragments preserved in Greek catena manuscripts. Severianus explains that Paul wrote Galatians because some from the circumcision were persuading Gentile believers to live as Jews, even though the apostles had already ruled against imposing the law. He argues that the law was a temporary, bodily discipline that could not save or give life, while grace and faith in Christ bring freedom and a heavenly citizenship.

A reader will meet Paul, Peter, Moses, Jeremiah, the Hagarites, and Ishmael. Paul's words are quoted directly from Galatians. Severianus argues that the law was given to teach the mind to recognize sin and prepare people for grace, but after Christ it is obsolete. Baptism, not law-keeping, makes Jews and Gentiles equal by clothing them all in Christ. He also explains that Paul's use of 'allegory' really means prophecy enacted through events, as with Hagar and Sarah. Key terms include law, grace, faith, circumcision, uncircumcision, flesh, elemental things, and new creation. Severianus cites the Septuagint and follows its book names and Psalm numbering.
