## Finding 1 — §37: "errasset in regem" mistranslated (MAJOR)

**Was:** "Mucius Scaevola, who would have perished among the enemy if he had offended the king, had he not lost his right hand."

**Finding:** *Errare in* + accusative = "to miss one's aim at." Livy 2.12: Mucius attempted to assassinate King Porsenna but killed the secretary by mistake — "having missed the king" — was captured, and saved himself by thrusting his right hand into the fire. "If he had offended the king" mistranslates *errasset* and reverses the anecdote's logic.

**Now:** "Mucius Scaevola, who, having missed the king, would have perished among the enemy, had he not lost his right hand."

**Why (Latin):** «Mucium Scaeuolam, qui cum errasset in regem perisset in hostibus, nisi dexteram perdidisset» — *errare in* + acc. = "miss"; *cum* + pluperfect subjunctive = circumstantial "having missed."
