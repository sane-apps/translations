Evagrius Ponticus (c. 345–399) wrote these sentences to a virgin. They are short counsels in the manner of Proverbs. This file is not his sentences to the monks.

He tells her to love the Lord and to serve him, to honor her mother, and to love her sisters. He tells her to pray without ceasing, to keep away from the company of men, and to put anger far off. He warns her against envy, slander, and a lack of mercy.

A virgin without mercy, he says, will have her lamps put out and will not see the bridegroom. Nothing is sweeter than the heavenly bridegroom. She will light a lamp that does not go out, and the oil will not fail.
