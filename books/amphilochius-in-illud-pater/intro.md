Amphilochius of Iconium (c. 340–c. 395) was a fourth-century bishop in Asia Minor, placed in his see early in 374. He was regarded by his contemporaries as one of the foremost leaders of the Eastern church after Basil of Caesarea and Gregory of Nazianzus. Most of his writings have been lost, and only eight homilies survive.

This homily was preached against heretics who misunderstood Christ's words in the garden, 'Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from me.' Amphilochius argues that these words do not indicate cowardice or weakness in Christ. He responds directly to those who used the saying to deny the full reality of the incarnation or to question Christ's divine power.

Amphilochius discusses the resurrection, the defeat of death and Hades, and the way Christ's incarnation worked through what he calls the economy of the flesh.
