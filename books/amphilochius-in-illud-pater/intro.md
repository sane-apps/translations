Amphilochius of Iconium (c. 340–c. 395) was the Christian bishop of Iconium in the fourth century. He preached to a Christian congregation and wrote against heretics who called the Son fearful, needy and inferior to the Father.

This work is a homily to that congregation, with the heretics addressed as opponents. It answers their use of Christ's prayer Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from me to charge him with fear and inferiority.

Amphilochius opens as a soldier fighting with the help of Stephen the protomartyr, called the general of piety, then gives Christ's own first person answer to the heretics' quoted questions: his fear hides his divinity and shows human weakness to lure the devil and death to attack him as a mere man, so the flesh suffers while the Word remains impassible, meaning unable to suffer, and the Father's refusal to remove the cup of suffering is neither lack of power nor lack of mercy. The reader also meets Moses and Joshua, Peter, Paul, Hosea, Isaiah, Jacob and Job, Adam, the devil as tyrant and deceiver, those raised by Christ such as Lazarus, Jairus's daughter and the widow's son, and the named heretics Eunomius and Arius who rejoice over the prayer and Apollinaris whose error that Christ took a body without a soul is refuted. Here plan of salvation renders God's saving arrangement, flesh is the human body, divinity is the divine nature, Word is the Son, hook and bait describe the trap for the devil, and Bible books follow Septuagint names and Psalm numbering.
