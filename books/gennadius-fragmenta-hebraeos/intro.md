Gennadius of Constantinople wrote these fragments as part of a commentary on the Epistle to the Hebrews. Only a few pieces of his biblical work survive, and these fragments on Hebrews are among them.

The fragments explain difficult passages in Hebrews for readers who need help with its compressed or unusual expressions. They are preserved mainly in later collections of excerpts from biblical commentators.

A reader will meet Abraham and Moses among those to whom God spoke in former times, and Christ as the Son through whom God has spoken in the last times. Gennadius explains that the Son is the radiance of the Father's glory, coeternal with him as radiance is with the sun. He discusses Christ's obedience learned through suffering, the foundation of Christian teaching, and the symbolism of the lampstand, the table, and the presentation of the loaves. Bible citations use the name Hebrews with chapter and verse numbers.
