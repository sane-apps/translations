Amphilochius of Iconium (c. 340–c. 395) wrote this homily for the newly baptized. He was a fourth-century bishop of Iconium in Cappadocia. The work is linked to the resurrection of the Savior Jesus Christ.

The homily celebrates those who have just received baptism. Amphilochius calls them the church's spiritual spring meadow, filled with the fragrance of the Spirit. He urges the congregation to sing the new song, using the Song of Moses at the Red Sea as a type of baptism, in which sin and the devil are drowned.

A reader will meet Christ, whose many names Amphilochius expounds: door, way, sheep, shepherd, pearl, light, cornerstone, sun of righteousness, mustard seed, worm, and man. Each name reveals a work of Christ for believers. The homily also mentions Adam, renewed in baptism, and Mary the Virgin, from whom Christ blossomed. Miriam, the sister of Moses, is invoked for her song at the Red Sea. Paul is quoted on being caught up in clouds to meet the Lord. The homily closes by deferring to the teaching of the fathers and blessing the congregation. A reader should know that the Psalms are quoted according to the Septuagint numbering, and the term baptismal washing refers to the bath of regeneration.
