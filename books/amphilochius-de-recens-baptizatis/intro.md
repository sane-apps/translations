Amphilochius of Iconium (c. 340–c. 395) wrote this homily for the newly baptized and the congregation celebrating their baptism. He was a bishop of Iconium in Cappadocia during the fourth century. The homily was delivered at Easter, when the church welcomed those who had just received baptism and celebrated the resurrection of Christ.

The occasion is a festal Easter gathering. He urges the congregation to sing the new song, using the Song of Moses at the Red Sea as a type of baptism, in which sin and the devil are drowned. The apostle Paul is quoted on being caught up in clouds to meet the Lord, and Christ's own words are quoted several times.

A reader should know that the homily expounds the many names of Christ, including door, way, sheep, shepherd, pearl, light, cornerstone, sun of righteousness, mustard seed, worm, and man. Each name reveals a work of Christ for believers. The Psalms are quoted according to the Septuagint numbering, so Psalm numbers may differ from those in modern Bibles based on the Hebrew text. The homily closes by deferring to the teaching of the fathers and blessing the congregation.
