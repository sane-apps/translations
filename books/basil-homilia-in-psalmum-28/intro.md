This homily was written by Basil of Caesarea (c. 330–379). He preaches to his hearers on Psalm 28 and urges them to worship God within his holy court, which he identifies with the Church.

Basil reads Psalm 28 as a prophecy of the ministry and baptism of John the Baptist. The voice of the Lord upon the waters is John, whose preaching of repentance and baptism prepared a people for Christ by crushing pride like the cedars, stirring the wilderness and exposing hidden sins, so that through baptism the Lord may dwell in purified souls and give strength to his people. The reader will meet John the Baptist, Abraham, Isaiah, Moses and Aaron, and will hear quoted words from John, from God the Father as a voice from heaven, from a divine voice commanding Isaiah to cry out, and from Abraham in dialogue with God, with God's speech to Abraham also quoted.

To follow it, it helps to know how words are used here. Court means the holy court identified with the Church, voice of the Lord means John the Baptist and his preaching, and waters means the Jordan and baptism. Cedars stand for proud opposing powers, forests stand for hidden places where sins lurk, flood is an image of baptism, and unicorn and deer are animal images used of the Lord and of holy souls. Psalm 28 follows the Septuagint numbering and corresponds to Psalm 29 in modern Hebrew-based numbering, and Basil also cites Genesis, Exodus, Isaiah and the Gospel of John, with Kadesh explained as meaning holy.
