Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) wrote these notes on selected verses from the book of Numbers for Christian readers seeking to understand the text's moral and prophetic meanings. He was a theologian who headed the Catechetical School of Alexandria, and his comments reflect a teacher's concern with how the Greek text of Scripture should be read and applied.

The work survives through a Latin translation made by Rufinus, who undertook it at the request of his brother Donatus during a time of barbarian threat and completed it when a quiet night allowed work. The original preaching likely occurred during a three-year liturgical cycle sometime between about 238 and 244 CE. These homilies are the oldest surviving corpus of Christian sermons and shaped biblical exegesis for succeeding centuries.

A reader will meet Moses, his sister Miriam, Balak king of Moab, and the seer Balaam. Origen quotes the Greek translators Aquila and Symmachus on the description of manna, and he reports a tradition that Balaam's disciples preserved his prophecies, which later guided the Magi to Bethlehem. To follow the argument, know that manna is the miraculous food God gave Israel in the wilderness, that Miriam was struck with leprosy for speaking against Moses, and that the spies who slandered the promised land effectively made God a liar. Origen treats Balaam's utterance as an oracle with symbolic meaning, and he identifies the man from Israel in the prophecy with Jesus Christ.
