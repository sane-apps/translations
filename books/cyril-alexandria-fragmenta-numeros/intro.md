Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote these two fragments as part of his commentary on the Book of Numbers.

The occasion is the interpretation of specific passages from Numbers. Cyril explains that the Levitical priests received tithes from the Israelites as their portion and inheritance from God. These priests in turn offered a tenth to Aaron, the head of the priesthood, who is presented as a type of Christ ministering not in the outer tabernacle but in the inner and hidden one. Cyril then notes that a command about the rod, the one used to strike the river, confirms faith by recalling the wonders performed in Egypt. Finally, Christ is compared to a rock because of its unbreakable and unshakable nature.

A reader will meet Moses, the lawgiver whose law governed the Levitical priesthood, and Aaron, who received a tenth from the Levitical priests and is a type of Christ. Israel appears as the people who gave tithes to the Levitical priests. Scriptural phrases are embedded within Cyril's explanatory text without explicit 'Scripture says' formulas. The Book of Numbers is cited from the Septuagint, and modern citations should use the book name 'Numbers' with standard chapter and verse numbering.
