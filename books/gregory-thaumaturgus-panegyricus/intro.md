Gregory Thaumaturgus (c. 213–c. 270) wrote this address to his teacher Origen. The speech is an expression of thanks and praise, though Gregory feels inadequate to the task of honoring his teacher.

The occasion is Gregory's preparation to deliver a speech of thanks and praise to Origen. He explains that he has neither spoken nor written for eight years, and has only listened to philosophers who value accurate investigation over polished speech.

A reader will meet Gregory himself as the speaker, and Origen as the subject of the speech. Origen is described as a man who appears human, but to those who can perceive the greatness of his character and habits, seems endowed with greater and nearly divine ornaments. Key terms include silence, philosophy, laws, language, words, painters, charcoal, potsherds, impressions, and divine.
