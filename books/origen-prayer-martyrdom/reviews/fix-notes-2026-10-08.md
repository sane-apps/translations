## Finding 1 — §1: ἡγεμονικόν flattened to "mind's eye" (MAJOR)

**Was:** "…if, in the season of the afflicters, who wish as it were to press our souls, we turn our mind's eye from the toilsome sights, and look not at the present toils…"

**Finding:** τὸ ἡγεμονικόν is the technical term (Stoic, taken up by Origen) for the "ruling faculty" / commanding part of the soul — not the "mind's eye" (an ocular metaphor absent from the Greek).

**Now:** "…if, in the season of the afflicters, who wish as it were to press our souls, we turn our ruling faculty away from the toilsome things, and look not at the present toils…"

**Why (Greek):** «ἀποστρέψαντες τὸ ἡγεμονικὸν ἡμῶν ἀπὸ τῶν ἐπιπόνων σκοποῖμεν οὐ τὰ ἐνεστηκότα ἐπίπονα ἀλλὰ τὰ διὰ τὴν ἐν τούτοις ὑπομονὴν… ἀποκείμενα» — τὸ ἡγεμονικόν = "our ruling faculty"; ἀπὸ τῶν ἐπιπόνων = "away from the toilsome things."
