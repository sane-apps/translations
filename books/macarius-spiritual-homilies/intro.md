The Fifty Spiritual Homilies are transmitted under the name of Macarius the Egyptian (c. 300–391), the desert father of Scetis. Scholars now ascribe them to an anonymous Syrian monk of the late fourth century, sometimes identified with Symeon of Mesopotamia.

Whoever wrote them, the Homilies rank among the most beloved works of Christian spirituality: intimate, experiential sermons on the heart's encounter with grace — prayer, temptation, the Spirit's transforming work, and the soul as God's dwelling.

This volume holds Homilies 5 and 6; the remaining homilies toward the full fifty will follow.
