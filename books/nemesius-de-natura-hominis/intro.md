Nemesius of Emesa (fl. c. 390) was bishop of Emesa (Homs, Syria) near the end of the fourth century; almost nothing else is known of him, yet his one surviving work became among the most widely read Christian books of the Middle Ages, East and West.

On the Nature of Man joins Christian doctrine to Greek philosophical psychology — against Platonists, Stoics, and physicians alike, Nemesius argues the soul is an immortal rational substance, not blood, breath, harmony, or number, and defends free will and providence.

This volume holds the opening soul arguments (1.1–3.1).
