Augustine of Hippo (354–430) preached this sermon to the Catholic congregation of Caesarea in Mauretania. He was bishop of Hippo and had come to Caesarea in 418 on church business. The occasion was a public meeting with Emeritus, the Donatist bishop of the city, who had entered the Catholic church and begun a conversation with Augustine.

He argues that Donatists are received as brothers because their baptism and faith belong to Christ, not to Donatus, but that these gifts are useless without love, which is found only in Catholic unity.

Augustine quotes Emeritus's opening words, the congregation's acclamation, and passages from Paul, the Psalms, and the Gospels. Key terms include 'unity' for the Catholic Church's oneness, 'schism' for the Donatist separation, 'mark' for the seal of baptism that belongs to Christ, and 'sign of peace' for baptism as a sign posted on a person in whom discord dwells.
