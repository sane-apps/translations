Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403) wrote this work to the presbyters and deacons of Syedra in Pamphylia. He had been a monk in Egypt and Judea, founded a monastery, and became bishop of Constantia (Salamis) in Cyprus in 367.

Epiphanius opens by praising his addressees for seeking the Holy Spirit through their request, seeing it as a sign of God's grace. He states that those who speak in harmony with Peter's confession will be blessed by the Lord, as Peter himself was blessed when he heard the words 'Blessed are you, Simon son of Jonah'. He argues that knowledge of the only true God leads to unity with God and away from polytheism, which scatters human thoughts toward 'such fornication', for Scripture says, 'the invention of idols is the first fornication'.

A reader will meet the apostle Peter, whose confession of Jesus as the Christ is cited as the model of blessed faith, and the evangelist John, quoted as calling Jesus the Only-Begotten who is in the bosom of the Father. Jesus Christ himself is quoted directly, and Scripture is cited generally. Epiphanius insists that Jesus Christ, whom the Father sent, is himself true God, as John says of the Only-Begotten. Key terms include 'plan' for God's saving dispensation, 'one rule' for the sole rule of God, 'polytheism' for belief in many gods, 'fornication' for idolatry as unfaithfulness to God, 'only Son' for the Only-Begotten, 'Trinity' for the three persons, 'unity' for their oneness, and 'true God' for the one God as opposed to false gods. The name Simon bar Jonah is rendered Simon son of Jonah.
