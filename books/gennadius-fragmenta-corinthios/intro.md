Gennadius of Constantinople wrote these brief notes on selected verses of Paul's First and Second Epistles to the Corinthians. Only fragments of his biblical commentaries remain, including these explanations of difficult passages in the Corinthian correspondence.

Gennadius follows the Antiochene school of literal exegesis, explaining the text plainly rather than seeking hidden meanings.

A reader will meet Paul the apostle, Moses the leader of Israel, and the Lord Jesus Christ, whose words from the Gospels are quoted directly. Gennadius explains that the cloud and the sea in Exodus were types that prefigured the grace of the Spirit and baptism. He argues that baptism has become for Christians what the sea crossing was for Israel, a defense against enemies, and that in baptism believers receive the Spirit of adoption and make covenants to live according to Christ's commandments. He distinguishes the spiritual gift of faith from the common faith of all believers, citing the Lord's saying about faith as a mustard seed. On 2 Corinthians 10:7, he instructs that the verse should be read with reproachful hypocrisy, criticizing those who judge by outward appearances rather than by the truth of works.
