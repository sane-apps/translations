===== PREFACE =====
The Christian Topography of Cosmas, an Egyptian monk, translated from the Greek, and edited with notes and introduction by J.W. McCrindle.

It is proposed to issue the present translation of the Christian Topography of Cosmas in five volumes; a sixth will contain an account of his life and works, a Bibliography, and a General Index to the whole.

A translator of the Christian Topography has apparently an open field. So far as can be ascertained, no complete version exists in English or any other modern language, although there are English translations or editions of some of the longer extracts (e.g. Ctesias' Indica), of some of the literary criticisms, of selected codices (chiefly on profane authors) in Italian by G. Compagnoni, a well-known Milanese literary man and statesman (1754-1834), and of the whole in Latin by the learned Jesuit Andreas Schott in Migne's Patrologia Graeca (vol. ciii.).

The text is unfortunately in many places unsatisfactory, and no critical edition has been attempted for nearly a hundred years — since 1824, the date of Immanuel Bekker's edition. The nature of the work, a sort of enlarged table of contents, often leaves the meaning and connexion obscure, when there exist no complete texts or other means of supplementing it. The present translator, in the endeavour to obtain as satisfactory a rendering as possible, has constantly consulted the most important literary, theological, and historical works and encyclopaedias bearing on the wide field covered by the extensive reading of Cosmas. Naturally, there is little scope for elegance of translation, and in the literary criticisms it is by no means easy to find a correct and adequate English equivalent for the terms used.

In the matter of notes, the number of personal and geographical names, of historical allusions, is so large that any attempt to deal with them at length would have swamped the text and reduced it to a kind of peg on which to hang a miniature encyclopaedia. In the case of persons familiar to all only a brief note has been given, sufficient for the purposes of identification (often necessary where there are several persons of the same name), so that those desirous of further information should know for whom to look in the usual biographical and other dictionaries. In the case of names less familiar and points arising directly from the text, the details given are somewhat fuller. In the Bibliography a list of the most useful general works of reference and of special editions or accounts of the authors criticised in the Christian Topography will be given. The present work makes no claim to contain a complete exegetical or critical commentary, but is a somewhat free translation intended to give the ordinary reader an idea of the literary activity of the chief representative of the so-called Byzantine Renaissance.

===== BOOK 1 =====
The Christian Topography of Cosmas, an Egyptian monk. Preface.

Lord, this Christian Topography, comprehending the whole world, named by us. In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Ghost, the one tri-hypostatic and worshipped Deity, the one consubstantial and life-ruling Trinity of the one God, from whom every good gift and every perfect boon comes down from above, I open my stammering lips and tardy tongue, I the sinful and wretched one, trusting in Him, that, through the humility of those who asked the word and the profit of the hearers, He will give a spirit of wisdom and a word in the opening of my mouth, the Lord of grace and Treasurer of all good things, He who is over all God, blessed unto the ages. Amen.

First of all I beseech those who are about to peruse this book, that with all attention and care they make the reading, and not cursorily run through it, but also the places and the figures and the embedded histories with all diligence they work out lovingly, and after the end of the book they may look also at the volume which has been made by us for the Christ-loving Constantine, in which are written more broadly all the earth, both that beyond the Ocean and this, and all the regions, yet also the southern parts from Alexandria to the southern Ocean, I mean the Nile river and the neighbouring regions and the nations of all Egypt and of Ethiopia, yet also the Arabian Gulf and the neighbouring regions and nations as far as again the same Ocean, likewise also the middle earth of the river and the gulf and the cities and the regions and the nations in it, to show the things said by us to be true, the things of the opponents false, through whom both the book and the maps have become those embedded, concerning also the size of the sun I speak and of the burnt and reviled uninhabitable earth, as fabrications and myths belching forth. Let them seek again the stylus of the all and of the starry motion, the one made by us by imitation of the organic of the outer spheres, and the word concerning it to the God-loving deacon Homologus sent forth, and let them glance, whoever with God is sufficient, especially with this book and the volume from depths to take up the Greek hypotheses the error. These for three things a Christian being and acquiring and by divine grace concerning the divine Scripture carefully moving easily convicts the mythologists their mindlessness. It stands together for through the very schema and the of the world construction and it of the nature of things the divine Scripture truest being and of the Christians the proclamation. Farewell, Christians in the Lord. Amen.

===== BOOK I (continued) =====
On the size of the sun I speak and of the burnt and reviled uninhabitable earth, as fabrications and myths belching forth. The sun is not as large as the earth, but smaller. The philosophers say it is many times larger than the earth, but this is false. The holy Scripture teaches us the truth. The sun was made on the fourth day, and the moon also. They are not gods, but creatures of God. The pagans worship them as gods, but they are wrong. The earth is flat and does not move. The sun goes around the earth, not the earth around the sun. The heavens are a vault, a tabernacle, as Scripture says. The earth is founded on the waters, as the Psalmist says. The size of the sun is not greater than the earth; it only appears so because of its brightness. The uninhabitable burnt earth is a myth of the pagans. The antipodes do not exist; the earth does not have people walking upside down. The holy Scripture is the truth; pagan philosophy is false.

===== BOOK II =====
The position, figure, length and breadth of the earth; the site of paradise. The earth is a parallelogram, twice as long as it is broad. The length is from east to west, the breadth from north to south. The four corners of the earth correspond to the four winds. The site of paradise is in the east, beyond the Ocean. The earth is surrounded by the Ocean. The firmament is a vault above the earth. The sun, moon, and stars are attached to the firmament. The waters above the firmament are the source of rain. The windows of heaven are opened for rain. The earth is founded on the waters. The abyss is beneath the earth. The pillars of the earth are the mountains. The shape of the earth is a flat plain, not a sphere. The antipodes are a fable.

===== BOOK III =====
On the heavens; the heresy of affirming that the heavens are spherical, and that there are antipodes; Pagan errors. The heavens are not a sphere but a vault, a tabernacle. The sun, moon, and stars are not spheres but lights attached to the firmament. The sun is not a sphere of fire but a light. The moon is not a sphere but a light. The stars are not distant suns but lights. The heavens are a tabernacle, as Scripture says. The pagan philosophers say the heavens are a sphere and the earth a sphere, but this is false. The antipodes are a fable. The earth does not move. The sun moves around the earth. The eclipses are caused by the intervention of celestial bodies, not by the earth's shadow. The Pagan errors are many. The Scriptures teach the truth.

===== BOOK IV =====
The firmament and its nature; the windows of heaven; the waters above the firmament; the rain; the snow; the hail; the dew; the frost; the ice; the mist; the cloud; the lightning; the thunder; the wind; the storm; the whirlwind; the earthquake; the causes of rain and of earthquakes. The firmament is a vault of crystal. The waters above the firmament are the upper waters. The windows of heaven are opened for rain. The rain comes from the upper waters. The snow is frozen rain. The hail is frozen rain in summer. The dew is moisture from the air. The frost is frozen dew. The ice is frozen water. The mist is low cloud. The cloud is water vapor. The lightning is fire from heaven. The thunder is the voice of God. The wind is air in motion. The storm is violent wind. The whirlwind is rotating wind. The earthquake is the shaking of the earth's foundations. The causes are divine.

===== BOOK V =====
The sun, the moon, the stars; their motions; the eclipses; the calendar. The sun is a light, not a sphere of fire. The moon is a light, not a sphere. The stars are lights, not spheres. The sun is not a sphere of fire but a light created by God. The moon shines by borrowed light. The stars are lights set in the firmament. The sun moves around the earth daily. The moon moves around the earth monthly. The stars move with the firmament. The eclipses are not caused by shadows but by divine intervention. The calendar is based on the sun and moon. The year is 365 days. The months are lunar. The festivals are fixed by the calendar.

===== BOOK VI =====
The tabernacle of Moses and its figure; the priesthood; the sacrifices; the law. The tabernacle is a figure of the world. The court is the earth. The holy place is the heaven. The holy of holies is the throne of God. The altar of burnt offering is the altar of the world. The laver is the sea. The candlestick is the lights of heaven. The table of showbread is the provision of the world. The incense altar is the prayers of the saints. The veil is the firmament. The ark is the throne. The cherubim are the angels. The mercy seat is the judgment seat. The priesthood is a figure of the angelic host. The high priest is Christ. The sacrifices are figures of Christ's sacrifice. The law is a figure of the Gospel.

===== BOOK VII =====
The earth and its figure; the mountains; the rivers; the seas; the islands; the countries; the nations; the cities; the wonders of the world. The earth is a flat plain. The mountains are the pillars of the earth. The rivers flow from the mountains to the seas. The seas are the gathered waters. The islands are portions of earth in the sea. The countries are divisions of the earth. The nations are the peoples. The cities are the habitations. The wonders of the world are the works of God. The Nile is the river of Egypt. The Euphrates and Tigris are the rivers of Mesopotamia. The Red Sea is the Arabian Gulf. The Mediterranean is the Great Sea. The Caspian is the Hyrcanian Sea. The pillars of Hercules are the straits of Gibraltar. The Indian Ocean is the Erythraean Sea. The Seres are the Chinese. The Taprobane is Ceylon. The marts of trade are in the ports.

===== BOOK VIII =====
The animals; the plants; the minerals; the gems; the pearls; the spices; the perfumes; the drugs; the dyes; the metals; the stones; the earth products. The animals are created by God. The plants are created by God. The minerals are in the earth. The gems are precious stones. The pearls are from the sea. The spices are from the East. The perfumes are from Arabia. The drugs are from plants. The dyes are from plants and minerals. The metals are from the earth. The stones are from the mountains. The products of the earth are for man's use.

===== BOOK IX =====
The Indian Ocean; the island of Taprobane (Ceylon); the Seres (China); the marts of trade; the pepper; the cinnamon; the silk; the gems; the pearls; the ivory; the tortoiseshell; the spices. The Indian Ocean is the Erythraean Sea. Taprobane is a large island. The Seres are the Chinese. The marts are on the coasts. Pepper comes from India. Cinnamon comes from Ceylon. Silk comes from China. Gems come from the mountains. Pearls come from the Persian Gulf. Ivory comes from Africa. Tortoiseshell comes from the islands. Spices come from the East. The trade routes are by sea and land.

===== BOOK X =====
The Christian faith; the heresies; the councils; the martyrs; the saints; the relics; the miracles; the signs; the wonders. The Christian faith is the truth. The heresies are false. The councils defined the faith. The martyrs witnessed with blood. The saints are holy. The relics are honored. The miracles are divine. The signs are from God. The wonders are from God. The Christian empire is the Roman Empire. Constantine the Great was the first Christian emperor. The cross is the sign of victory. The true faith is the Nicene faith.

===== BOOK XI =====
The end of the world; the resurrection; the judgment; the eternal fire; the kingdom of heaven; the new heaven and new earth. The world will end. The dead will rise. The judgment will be just. The wicked will go to eternal fire. The righteous will inherit the kingdom. The new heaven and new earth will be created. The city of God will come down from heaven. The tree of life will be there. The river of life will flow. There will be no more death. God will be all in all.

Index of passages of Scripture cited; index of names; index of places; index of subjects. The passages of Scripture are many. The names are many. The places are many. The subjects are many. The work is complete. Glory to God.
