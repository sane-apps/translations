Basil of Caesarea (c. 330–379) addressed this homily to the Church of God, especially to those leaving the Church to gather in private houses. He preaches on Psalm 115 to encourage faith rather than logical demonstration, to speak of martyrdom and the death of the holy, and to warn against leaving public worship.

Basil argues that faith must lead speech about God and that every discipline rests on unproved first principles. He presents Psalm 115 as the speech of David in humility, answers sophistical opponents who turn "every man is a liar" into wordplay by saying "men" means those ruled by human passions, and reads the cup of salvation as the offering of life in martyrdom and the vow as the public sacrifice of praise. The reader meets David as speaker of the psalm, Achish the Philistine king, the Apostle whose words are quoted without a name, and Jesus Christ quoted from the Gospels, alongside opponents whose objection is paraphrased and dismissed.

Basil quotes the Septuagint and cites Psalms by its numbering, which differs from modern Hebrew-based numbering, and cites other Old Testament books by their Septuagint names. In this rendering faith is assent through the Spirit, demonstration is logical demonstration, ecstasy is the psalmist's amazement, cup of salvation is martyrdom, sacrifice of praise is the public offering in the Church, and Jerusalem is the Church. The Apostle quoted is Paul though Basil does not name him, and Achish is the Hebrew name for the king called Anchous in the Septuagint.
