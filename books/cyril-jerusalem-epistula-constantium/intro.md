Cyril of Jerusalem (c. 313–386) wrote this letter to the emperor Constantius Augustus. He was bishop of Jerusalem at the time, and he sent it in May 351. His purpose was to report a remarkable event in the city and to assure the emperor of divine favor.

The letter was written while Constantius was facing a military conflict with the usurper Magnentius. Cyril reports that during the days of Pentecost a huge cross of light appeared in the sky over Jerusalem, stretching from Golgotha to the Mount of Olives. It was visible to the whole city for hours and brighter than the sun. Cyril presents this as a heavenly sign confirming the Christian faith and the emperor's piety, following the discovery of the true cross under Constantius's father Constantine.

A reader will meet Cyril himself, the emperor Constantius, and the memory of Constantine. Cyril quotes Jesus from the Gospel according to Matthew, where the Savior speaks of the sign of the Son of Man. He urges Constantius to read that Gospel closely and take courage with God as his ally. To follow the letter, know that 'cross' means the cross of Christ as a triumphant sign, 'sign' means a heavenly sign, and 'piety' means devout reverence toward God. The letter refers to the Gospel according to Matthew, and the quoted prediction is now cited as Matthew 24:30.
