Hesychius of Jerusalem (c. 412–450) was a priest of the Church of Jerusalem and a renowned theologian, biblical commentator, and preacher in the Eastern Church. He was active during the first half of the fifth century and became presbyter at Jerusalem in 412. By 429 he was recognized as the pre-eminent biblical interpreter and teacher of the church in Jerusalem and Palestine.

This work is a homily in praise of the holy apostles Peter and Paul, preached during the feast of Peter and Paul on 28 December in Jerusalem. It was composed in Greek in the early fifth century. The homily is an encomium, a speech of praise, on the two apostles.

The homily includes praise of Peter and an account of Paul's conversion from persecutor to apostle. A reader will meet Peter and Paul as the central figures, and will hear the story of Paul's encounter with Christ and his transformation.
