Severianus of Gabala (fl. c. 400) was a bishop in Syria who became a popular preacher in Constantinople. He was born in Syria and spoke Greek with a Syriac accent. He was learned in the Divine Scriptures and known for his sermons and biblical commentaries. He became an enemy of John Chrysostom and helped condemn him at the Synod of the Oak.

These fragments on the Letter to the Romans survive as excerpts in catena, or chain, commentaries. They are Severianus's fragments, not those of Apollinaris of Laodicea and not those of Diodorus of Tarsus. The text is fragmentary, not a continuous commentary, and reflects early Christian exegetical scholarship on Paul.

A reader will meet Severianus explaining Paul's argument in Romans. The sections address topics such as the judgment of God and human hypocrisy, the distinction between sin and impiety, the Law and the Spirit, divine sovereignty, the measure of faith and diverse gifts, separation from Christ's love, the fruit of love and the urgency of the time, and progress in understanding and the Law. The reader should know that the text is fragmentary and that it reflects the concerns of an early Christian interpreter of Paul.
