Eustathius of Antioch (c. 320–337) wrote this address to Emperor Constantine.

Eustathius praises God for giving Constantine earthly rule and for abolishing idolatry through him, so that the world is now illumined by the knowledge of God and the consubstantial Trinity is proclaimed.

He asks Constantine either to persuade Arius to abandon his errors or, if he persists, to remove him from the Church so that his sophistry cannot ensnare simpler souls. Key terms include consubstantial, Trinity, persons, Godhead, heretic, creation, Creator, worshiper of what is created, only-begotten Son and Word, true faith, false teaching, and sophistry. The fragment contains no direct biblical citations.
