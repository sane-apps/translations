Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) delivered this homily as bishop of Alexandria, a position he held from 412.

The homily was delivered in the church of Cyrinus on the thirteenth of the Egyptian month Phamenoth. It opens by introducing a saying of Isaiah: 'Death swallowed and prevailed, and again God removed every tear from every face.' The extant text presents this quotation and then moves into a narrative about God and an unnamed adversary.

A reader will meet God, described as life and life-giving, who willed that humanity be an incorruptible living creature on earth even though its nature is corruptible. An unnamed wicked and envious adversary plots against humanity and opposes the Creator's will, cunningly bringing humanity down into disobedience, knowing that through disobedience humanity would fall into death. The excerpt does not contain the actual interpretation of the Isaiah quotation. The Isaiah text quoted corresponds to Isaiah 25:8 in modern citation.
