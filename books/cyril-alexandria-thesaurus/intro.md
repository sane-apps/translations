Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote this Thesaurus on the holy and consubstantial Trinity. This file is the prologue and a short opening of each discourse. The first discourse goes a little further than the rest. It is not the Dialogues on the Holy Trinity.

The prologue sets the contest and the aim, that Jesus is Lord. The first discourses say it is better to call God Father than unbegotten, and that there was not a time when the Son was not. Later openings take up the Son as consubstantial, the words "the Father is greater than I," and "the Lord created me."

Other openings take up the day and the hour, the human affections of the Savior, and the Son who will be subjected. The last discourses turn to the Spirit, who is God by nature from the Father, and close with testimonies that the Son is begotten, not made.
