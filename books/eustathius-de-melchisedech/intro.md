Eustathius of Antioch (c. 320–337) wrote this treatise to correct errors about Melchizedek, especially the view that he was greater than Christ or was the Holy Spirit. He also sought to explain why Scripture calls Melchizedek without father, without mother, and without genealogy.

The occasion for the work was the spread of mistaken teachings about Melchizedek. Some claimed he was a heavenly being or the Holy Spirit.

Paul's statement in Hebrews about Melchizedek being without father and mother frames the discussion. The omission of Melchizedek's ancestry points readers to Christ, who is truly without father on earth according to the flesh and without mother in heaven according to his divinity. Melchizedek, moved by the prophetic Spirit, honored God with bread and wine, prefiguring the eternal offering Christ would make according to the order of Melchizedek rather than the bloody sacrifices of Aaron.
