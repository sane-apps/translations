This fragment is attributed to Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444), Patriarch of Alexandria from 412 to 444. It survives as a papyrus fragment of a letter, written to defend the Nicene faith against two christological errors. The writer distances himself from both the opinion of Apollinarius and the blasphemy of Nestorius, and responds to disturbances in the church.

Cyril of Alexandria was a central figure in that dispute, writing multiple works against Nestorius, Patriarch of Constantinople. The disagreement over Christological views eventually led churches supporting Nestorius to split from the Orthodox church.

The author equates the opinion of Apollinarius and the blasphemy of Nestorius as the same impiety. He identifies two errors: attaching division or a duality to the Only-Begotten Son of God according to the incarnation, and not confessing his ensouled and all-holy flesh as human except for sin alone. He holds to the orthodox faith confirmed by the blessed and saving creed of Nicaea, and rejects communion with those who hold such errors. He quotes a saying close to Matthew 22:29, that they err not knowing the Scriptures, though the fragment has a slight textual variation.
