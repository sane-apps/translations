Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote these dialogues on the holy and consubstantial Trinity. The prologue is addressed to Nemesinus and promises seven dialogues. This file carries the first two through, and gives the opening of each of the rest. It is not the Thesaurus.

Hermias has been kept home by a storm. The speakers ask how a man learns the hard things in Scripture. At Horeb the people ask not to hear the fire again. The names Father and Son mark a begetting, not two ranks that can be swapped.

The second dialogue says the Son is coeternal with the Father and begotten from him by nature. The later openings ask whether the Son is true God, whether he is a creature, whether the properties of deity are in him, how the human sayings belong to the economy with the flesh, and whether the Spirit is God and from God.
