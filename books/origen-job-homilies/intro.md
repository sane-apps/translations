Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) wrote these homilies for a Christian congregation as he preached through the book of Job, explaining its Greek text verse by verse. He was a teacher and later a priest, first in Alexandria and then in Caesarea, and he was among the first Christian writers to treat Job in this comprehensive way. His goal was to defend Job's righteousness against readings that made suffering a simple proof of sin.

These homilies are not Didymus the Blind's commentary on Job, and they are not Severian of Gabala's sermons on Job. The occasion is a series of sermons on the biblical book of Job, delivered to a congregation and preserved only in fragments, mainly through later catenae and a few Greek manuscripts. Origen presents Job as a blameless, righteous, and God-fearing man whose sufferings are not punishments for hidden sins but are measured by God's providence. He rejects fate and the friends' assumption that Job's misery proves great wickedness, while still using their accusations to warn against real sins such as harsh lending and neglect of the poor.

A reader will meet Job himself, his accusers Eliphaz and Elihu, and God, whose speeches about creation and the beasts show divine wisdom and power. Origen also quotes the Greek translators Aquila, Symmachus, and Theodotion, and argues against heretical teachers such as Valentinus and Basilides. The closing restoration is read as proof of the resurrection, since Job's children are not doubled on earth but will be doubled in the age to come, and details of Job and the dragon are read as figures of Christ's victory over the devil. Key terms include blameless, God-fearing, providence, fate, trial, overseer, vice, sin, confession, resurrection, dragon, Leviathan, cornerstone, night watches, and pledge. Bible books follow the Greek Old Testament, normally with Septuagint book names and Psalm numbering, and modern citations should note where the Septuagint numbering differs.
