Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote this fragment from a treatise on the solution of dogmatic questions for a pious reader whom he addresses as 'your reverence'. The work answers objections and questions about biblical narratives, especially whether the rich man and Lazarus account describes events that actually happened and how to understand the prophet Hosea's marriage and Melchizedek's origin.

The fragment opens with the gospel story of the rich man and Lazarus and their opposite fates after death. An unnamed interlocutor then poses the question whether this story already happened and each received due recompense, or whether it typifies the coming judgment. The interlocutor argues that because Jesus names Lazarus, the account is true and was really enacted. Cyril's own response is only partially preserved in the lacunose heading 'Solution: the judgment...' that follows.

The fragment then raises further questions about whether Hosea really married a prostitute and had children by her in actual deed, or whether these things are said prophetically, and about whether Melchizedek was an ordinary man, a spirit, or a man newly created by God without human origin. Cyril refers the reader to his fuller discussions in his commentary on Hosea and in his work on Genesis.
