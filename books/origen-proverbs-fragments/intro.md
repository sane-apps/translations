Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) was a Christian teacher in Alexandria and later at Caesarea. These fragments come from his comments on Proverbs. They are not Didymus the Blind's Fragments on Proverbs, and they are not Evagrius Ponticus's Notes on Proverbs or his Exposition on Proverbs.

He distinguishes parable, dark saying, sayings of the wise, and riddle as modes of Scripture that conceal and reveal divine truth. Origen quotes Scripture throughout, and at one point introduces another interpreter with the words 'another will say' to argue against a different reading.

The deeper meaning of Scripture, he insists, is available to disciples who enter the house of Jesus, not to the crowds who remain outside.
