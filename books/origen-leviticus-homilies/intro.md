Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) wrote these homilies for a Christian congregation, addressing them as those who are of the Church. He preached through the book of Leviticus to show that its sacrificial and purity laws, read spiritually, reveal Christ and the life of the Christian soul.

The homilies were delivered in Alexandria during a three-year liturgical cycle between 238 and 244 CE. Theoctistus gave Origen the responsibility to deliver homilies during this cycle. The work survives through Rufinus's Latin version of Origen's original Greek text, and Rufinus admitted to condensing the homilies and expanding some of the explanations, making more changes here than to Origen's homilies on other books of the Pentateuch.

Origen argues that Leviticus is a spiritual book whose rituals are figures of Christ and the Church. The sacrifices, priesthood, purity laws, and festivals all point to Christ's once-for-all offering, the sanctification of the soul, and the moral life of believers. A reader will meet Moses the lawgiver, Aaron the first high priest, and Paul, cited as authority for the spiritual reading of the law. Key terms include burnt offering for the whole burnt offering, high priest for the chief priest, and letter for the literal sense that veils the spiritual meaning. The book names and Psalm numbering follow the Septuagint, so modern citations use standard English Bible book names and Hebrew Psalm numbering where they differ.
