Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) preached these homilies on Leviticus. Rufinus translated them. They are not his notes on Leviticus.

They begin with the sacrifice: the hand on the victim's head, the calf divided, and the priest who eats the offering. They take up holy and common, the beasts that chew the cud, the leper shut up seven days, and the woman who bears a child.

Later homilies take up the feasts, the lamp and the bread of the presence, and the blessings and the rod if the people will not hear. The citations use the old names of the biblical books.
