Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403) was bishop of Salamis, the chief city of Cyprus, from about 367 until his death. The work urges them to keep the traditions they had received and not to turn aside from them.

He extends the prohibition even to an ordinary house, because a Christian must not be distracted through the eyes and the wanderings of the mind.

A reader will meet only Epiphanius as the speaker and the citizens as the recipients. The argument is simple: keep God in memory in your hearts, not in pictures. Anyone who deliberately tries to comprehend the divine character of God's Word according to the incarnation from material colors is to be anathema. The work does not cite specific books of the Bible. A reader should know that testament here means a binding ordinance, images means religious pictures, and cemeteries means the burial places of the saints.
