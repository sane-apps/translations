Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote this answer to Julian. The preface says Julian set an eloquent tongue against Christ and wrote three books against the holy Gospels. This file then gives the first nine books of the reply. It is not the festal letters, and it is not the dialogue with Nestorius.

He answers Julian on Noah and the flood, and on the oneness of God in the faith of Abraham. He takes up the poets' fables of Earth, Coeus, Hyperion, and Prometheus, and he sets those fables against the writings of Moses. He also answers, from the Greek historians, the charge that altars were polluted with human blood.

Later books speak of sacrifice, of a divine physician, of oracles that have fallen silent, and of Asclepius. They set the truth of Scripture above elegant Greek speech, and they speak of the Lord taking on humanity, and of a worship that is more than the slaughter of animals.
