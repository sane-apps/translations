This homily is attributed to Hesychius of Jerusalem (c. 412–450), a priest and biblical commentator active in the fifth century. Modern scholars regard the attribution as mistaken, and the author is often called Pseudo-Hesychius of Jerusalem. The work was probably composed in the sixth century or later, perhaps in Cappadocia or Palestine.

The homily celebrates Longinus, the centurion who stood at the crucifixion of Christ and confessed him as the Son of God. It tells how Longinus was tortured under King Herod of Judaea for acknowledging the newborn Christ as God, lived until the crucifixion, and was later martyred at the village of Andralis near Tyana in Cappadocia. A shrine was founded at the site after a widow recovered his head from Jerusalem.

The text survives in abridged form in tenth-century Synaxaria, and readers should be aware that it presents a legendary account rather than a historical record.
