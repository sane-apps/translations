This homily was written by Eustathius of Antioch (c. 320–337), bishop of Antioch and a leading opponent of Arianism at the Council of Nicaea. He addressed it to a Christian congregation, opening with the words 'Let us listen, beloved.' He preached it on a festal occasion to proclaim Christ's divinity and defend the full reality of his incarnation against Jewish objections.

The homily begins with John's account of the supper at Bethany, where the risen Lazarus reclines with Jesus while his sisters Martha and Mary serve him. Eustathius presents Lazarus' presence at table as proof that his resurrection was real, not a phantom. He then interprets the fire Christ came to cast on earth as the life-giving fire of the knowledge of God, and sets Martha's confession alongside those of Peter and John as a proclamation that Christ is the eternal Son of God, not a mere anointed man.

A reader will meet Lazarus, Martha, and Mary as models of faith, love, and hospitality, and will hear Mary's anointing of Jesus praised as worship of the incarnate Word. Eustathius quotes John the Evangelist, Jesus, Martha, Peter, Isaiah, Jacob, Matthew, and Solomon, and he answers a blind Jew's objection that no one can see God and live. To follow the homily, know that 'consubstantial' means sharing the same being, 'incarnation' means God the Word becoming human, and 'the son of thunder' is John the Evangelist. The homily closes with a doxology to the incarnate Son, consubstantial with the Father and the Holy Spirit.
