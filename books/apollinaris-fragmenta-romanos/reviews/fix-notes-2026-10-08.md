## F1 — u02-rem-close §8 [0]: μετουσίαν → "transformation"
- **Was:** "**Because of the transformation of the Spirit**, as he has previously stated, we have grace in having the ruling principle in creation, receiving the glory of the true Son; so that it is not beyond glory that such a great and such a creation should be subjected to men, who appear small in creation."
- **Now:** "Because of the participation of the Spirit, as he has previously stated, we have by grace the ruling principle in creation, having received the glory of the true Son; so that it is not beyond glory that such a great and such a creation should be subjected to men, who appear small in creation."
- **Why:** μετουσίαν = "participation" (διὰ τὴν τοῦ πνεύματος μετουσίαν); "transformation" reads μεταμορφούσης. Authority: review Greek + page Greek.

## F2 — u02-rem-early §6 [5] (reviewer said u02-rem-close [5] — actual location): agent inverted
- **Was:** "…just as Paul said **Satan would deliver one** to destruction of the flesh so that the spirit might be saved (1 Corinthians 5:5)."
- **Now:** "…just as Paul said he would hand him over to Satan for the destruction of the flesh, so that the spirit might be saved (1 Corinthians 5:5)."
- **Why:** ὁ Παῦλος ἔφασκε τῷ σατανᾷ παραδώσειν — Paul (subject, infinitive παραδώσειν) hands the man over to Satan (dative); Satan was never the deliverer (1 Cor 5:5: παραδοῦναι τὸν τοιοῦτον τῷ σατανᾷ). Authority: review Greek + page Greek.

## F3 — u02-rem-mid §7 [11]: ἀκόλουθον + ἕπεσθαι → "falls to"
- **Was:** "**But creation falls to the sons of God**, being God's possession; for the slave follows the free, and the possession is subject to the son because of his nobility."
- **Now:** "But it is fitting for creation to follow the sons of God, being God's possession; for the slave follows the free man, and the possession is subject to the son because of his nobility."
- **Why:** ἀκόλουθον... τὴν κτίσιν ἕπεσθαι = "it is fitting for creation to follow"; the rest was sound. Authority: review Greek + page Greek.

## F4 — u04-rem-early §14 [13]: Aquila has καταφοράν ("slumber")
- **Was:** "…whereas the version of **Aquila has a clearer transmission**, but the version of Symmachus **has strength**."
- **Now:** "…which Aquila's has more clearly as 'slumber,' but Symmachus's as 'torpor.'"
- **Why:** σαφέστερον ἔχει καταφοράν = "has more clearly: slumber"; ἡ δὲ τοῦ Συμμάχου κάρωσιν = "but Symmachus's: torpor" (Isa 29:10). Authority: review Greek + page Greek.

## F5 — u05-rem-mid §19 [3]: πρὸς ἀνδρός → "with a man"
- **Was:** "For to treat with contempt **the one committing adultery with a man** is **to threaten** the one who shall be saved according to the Psalm…"
- **Now:** "For to despise the evildoer is the mark of the man who will be saved, according to the Psalm; but one should not despise the one who does something out of excessive piety, lest we be despised by God like those who are convinced they are righteous and despise others."
- **Why:** τὸν πονηρευόμενον πρὸς ἀνδρός ἐστι τοῦ σωθησομένου = "to despise the evildoer is characteristic of the man who will be saved" (Ps 14:4 LXX; "adultery" was invented, πρὸς ἀνδρός is genitive of the man). Authority: review Greek + page Greek.

## F6 — u05-rem-mid §19 [14]/[15]: θανάτῳ τὴν ζωὴν ἐωνημένος
- **Was:** [14] "…he who **has become dead to life**." [15] "He will be lost by being separated from the union and being deprived of love, or even by being compelled to eat unfaithfully."
- **Now:** [14] "…he who purchased life with death." [15] "For he will perish, being severed from the union and deprived of love, or even compelled to eat without faith."
- **Why:** the purchased-with-death Christ motif (cf. 1 Cor 6:20); the reverse reading made the sentence theologically incoherent. Authority: review Greek + page Greek.

## F7 — u03-open §9 [9]: διῄρηται → "it is stated"
- **Was:** "For **it is stated that to judge is to show mercy**, and each man becomes the cause of himself regarding the responses he is obligated to, reaping that which he sows, according to the Apostle."
- **Now:** "For mercy is distinguished from judging, and each man becomes the cause of himself regarding the answers he owes, reaping what he sows, according to the Apostle."
- **Why:** διῄρηται γὰρ τοῦ κρίνειν τὸ ἐλεεῖν = "for mercy is distinguished from judging" (cf. Jas 2:13, mercy triumphs over judgment) — the exact opposite of the base. Authority: review Greek + page Greek.
