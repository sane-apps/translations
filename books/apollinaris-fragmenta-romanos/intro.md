Apollinaris of Laodicea (c. 310–c. 390) was bishop of Laodicea in Syria and a prominent opponent of Arianism. He wrote this commentary on Paul's Letter to the Romans in the fourth century. The original work has been lost, and only short excerpts survive in later chains of patristic commentary and in the polemical writings of fifth- and sixth-century authors who cited him to refute his Christology.

Apollinaris interprets Romans as teaching that salvation comes through faith in Christ, not through the law or human works. He emphasizes that Christ is both divine and human, and that the Spirit, not the letter of the law, brings life and righteousness. He explains Israel's unbelief as part of God's plan to bring the gospel to the Gentiles, while a remnant of Israel will still be saved. He also gives practical instruction on love and encouragement within the church.

A reader will meet Paul, whose words are quoted and explained throughout, along with Christ, whose sayings are introduced by phrases like 'the Savior says.' Old Testament figures such as Moses, Abraham, Pharaoh, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and Daniel appear, as do the Greek translators Aquila and Symmachus, whose versions of Old Testament passages are cited. Key terms include righteousness, faith, law, Spirit, grace, flesh, adoption, promise, election, firstfruits, indifferent things, plan, harden, atonement, and giving. Apollinaris uses the Septuagint for Old Testament quotations and follows its book names and Psalm numbering.
