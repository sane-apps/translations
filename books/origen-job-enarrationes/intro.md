Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) wrote this commentary on the Book of Job. He was an early Christian theologian and ascetic who served as head of the Catechetical School of Alexandria. This English is his Notes on Job. It is not his Homilies on Job, not Didymus the Blind's commentary on Job, and not Severian of Gabala's sermons on Job.

Origen expounds selected verses from the Greek text of Job, often responding to the friends' speeches and to the final divine speeches. He reads Job as the model athlete of piety whose endurance under every kind of trial defeats the devil. The devil attacks Job through losses, bodily pain, his wife, and finally through the friends' harsh words, but Job refuses to blaspheme or to surrender his integrity.

A reader will meet Job, his wife, and his three friends Eliphaz, Bildad, and Zophar, whose words are quoted and argued against. Origen treats the friends' speeches as containing some sound doctrine but misapplied to Job, since suffering is not always punishment for sin. He repeatedly applies the text to heretics, whose prayers, teachings, and apparent prosperity are deceptive and destined for judgment. The final divine speeches reveal that God allowed Job's trials so that his hidden virtue might be made manifest, and Job receives double as the reward of his endurance. Key terms include athlete, endurance, courage, trials, ungodly, heretic, dragon, providence, wisdom, overseer, mask, and unquenchable fire. Origen cites the Greek Old Testament, generally following its book names and Psalm numbering.
