Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote these festal letters for the paschal season. This file holds the homilies of the first letter, the second, and the fifth through the fifteenth. It is not his Dialogues on the Holy Trinity, and it is not the Thesaurus.

The homilies urge humility in the ministry of preaching, a fast that puts earthly passions to death, and a warning against hypocrisy. They speak of renewal in Christ, the law of the whole burnt offering, the light of Christ, Abraham's sons, faith and obedience, the folly of idolatry, free will, and a circumcision that is spiritual.

Later homilies take up love and repentance, judgment on earthly prosperity, the forming of Christ in the believer, spiritual warfare and gentleness, wealth and piety, double-mindedness, the one God, and the Son born from his substance. One homily refuses to seek knowledge of the future from the stars. Another takes up the stater in the fish and the word that the sons are free.
