John Chrysostom (c. 349–407) addressed these instructions to catechumens preparing for baptism. He gave them as two catechetical homilies for those about to be baptized, to guide baptismal preparation, moral purification and renunciation of Satan.

He first celebrates their decision and contrasts deliberate preparation with deathbed baptism, then explains the many names of baptism, above all the washing of regeneration, as recreation of the whole person rather than only forgiveness of sins. He urges control of the tongue by renouncing all oaths and reform of habits before baptism, and warns against luxury, especially women's jewelry, and pagan practices such as omens, amulets and incantations, calling readers to use the renunciation of Satan as a daily weapon. The reader meets Joseph, Pharaoh, Moses, David, Job, John the Baptist, Peter, Paul and Alexander of Macedon. Paul, Christ, David, John the Baptist, Peter and an unnamed speaker of wisdom sayings are quoted at length, and demons are quoted speaking to Christ.

To be baptized renders the word for being enlightened, and enlightenment, initiation and sacred rites also refer to baptism and its rites, while washing of regeneration means complete re-creation. I renounce you, Satan is the baptismal renunciation, Satan's pageantry covers theaters, races, omens, amulets and all sin, and covenant and deposit describe the agreement with Christ and the grace entrusted at baptism. Oaths of every kind are forbidden, including perjury and truthful oaths, and omens, amulets and incantations mean pagan signs and spells. Uz renders Ausitis, the land of Uz and home of Job, and the coins of Alexander of Macedon were used as amulets. The quotations follow the Septuagint, here given with standard English Bible book names and Hebrew Psalm numbering, with a note where the Greek numbering is one behind.
