Hesychius of Jerusalem (c. 412–450) preached this second homily on the holy Pascha. It is translated whole, from the opening to the closing doxology.

A sacred and royal trumpet, which Bethlehem filled and Zion tempered in fire, gathers the hearers. The tomb is a bridal chamber where the bridegroom slept for three days and from which the bride was raised incorrupt. The soldiers guard him as one dead, and the gatekeepers of Hades see him as God and cower.

He is the same one, not one and another. The Word made flesh is one, and it is not right for the Word to be separated from the flesh. The angels tell the women that the Nazarene who was crucified is not here, for he has risen. Hosea foretells the third day, and Isaiah's "Now I will arise" is the Lord's own word about his rising. The homily ends with glory to the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit.
