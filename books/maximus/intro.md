This account was written by an anonymous writer in Asia. It is not sent to any named person, and it states no occasion or purpose. It belongs to the time of Emperor Decius and the proconsul Optimus, and it records the decree, the voluntary appearance of Maximus, his interrogation, torture, sentence and stoning.

Decius ordered Christians throughout the world to leave the living and true God, offer sacrifice to demons, or face punishment. Maximus, a freeborn commoner who lives by his own business and calls himself a Christian and servant of Christ, presents himself voluntarily, rejects the order as unjust, refuses to offer sacrifice to any but God, and seeks eternal life in place of miserable temporal life. The reader meets Maximus, Decius behind the decree, and Optimus the proconsul in Asia who interrogates, tortures and condemns him for refusing to offer sacrifice to Diana, then has him led outside the walls and struck with stones until he dies. Most of the text is direct speech in which Maximus answers Optimus, and Maximus argues against the words of Optimus and the order of Decius, all framed by an anonymous narrator who closes with thanks and a doxology to Christ.

In this rendering, offer sacrifice means performing the ordered pagan offering, demons is the Christian word for the pagan gods named in the decree, and torture covers beating and the rack. Anointing is Maximus word for his sufferings, which he says soothe rather than hurt, and he says he is sustained by Christ's grace. The language about divine mercy belongs to the formal sentence spoken by Optimus, not to God, and the king of this age means Decius. No book of the Bible is named, quoted or numbered in the text.
