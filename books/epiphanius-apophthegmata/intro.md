Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403) was bishop of Salamis, the chief city of Cyprus, for nearly forty years. Before becoming bishop he had lived as a monk in Egypt and founded a monastery in Palestine.

The sayings open with stories of divine judgment on paganism, including the destruction of the Serapeum idol by Theophilus of Alexandria and the death of the emperor Julian foretold by crows. Epiphanius then rebukes his monks for keeping only the third, sixth, and ninth hours while neglecting prayer during the other hours of the day. He exchanges mutual respect with Abba Hilarion, who acknowledges Epiphanius's greater way of life.

Epiphanius contrasts humble faith with proud religiosity. The closing sayings emphasize Scripture reading as protection against sin, the small cost at which God sells righteousness, God's readiness to forgive repentant sinners while demanding more from the righteous, and the beatitudes and Ten Commandments as the substance of the Christian way.
