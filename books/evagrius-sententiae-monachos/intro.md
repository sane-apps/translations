Evagrius Ponticus (c. 345–399) is the name on these sentences for monks. This file is not the sentences to a virgin, and it is not the list of vices opposed to the virtues.

The sentences warn the monk against pride and vanity. Trials test the heart as fire tests silver and gold. He names the offspring of serpents, and he says the fear of the Lord generates prudence.

Knowledge of the Trinity is set above knowledge of the incorporeal ones. The sentences cite the Psalms and Matthew.
