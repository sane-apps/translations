Evagrius Ponticus (c. 345–399) wrote this collection of sayings for monks living in cenobia or communities. He was a Christian monk and ascetic who left Constantinople around 382, took monastic vows in Jerusalem, and spent the rest of his life in the Egyptian monastic settlements of Nitria and Kellia. The work offers practical and contemplative guidance through short sayings, warning against vices, urging virtue, and pointing toward knowledge of God.

The sayings are addressed to monks in community as heirs of God, calling them to receive and pass on divine words. Evagrius speaks directly to the individual monk, warning against passions that destroy the soul, especially anger, resentment, lust, greed, and pride, while urging self-control, gentleness, vigilance, repentance, humility, and obedience to one's spiritual father. The monk is also called to love his brothers and not scandalize them.

A reader should know that the path described moves from the active life of virtue through freedom from passions and love to knowledge, culminating in knowledge of the Holy Trinity. Key terms include knowledge, freedom from passions, active life, contemplation, listlessness, resentment, thoughts, self-control, gentleness, stillness, mind, demons, and bodiless beings. Psalms are cited according to the Septuagint numbering, which sometimes differs from the Hebrew numbering found in modern English Bibles.
