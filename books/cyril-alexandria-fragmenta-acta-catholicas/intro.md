Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote these fragments on the Acts of the Apostles and the Catholic Epistles. They are short notes on selected verses. This file is not his fragments on Hebrews, and it is not a commentary that walks through either book from the first chapter to the last.

The Acts notes take up the times and seasons the Father has kept in his own authority, the raising of Jesus, and the sandal as a sign of death and corruption. Other notes turn to James and to the letters of Peter and John: hope in the resurrection, faith and works, the tongue and its power, patience, hospitality, and the call to be merciful as the Father is merciful.

A reader also meets the divinity of Christ and the incarnation, a warning against false teachers, the prophetic word as a light until the morning star rises, and the judgment that the things of this world are vanity.
