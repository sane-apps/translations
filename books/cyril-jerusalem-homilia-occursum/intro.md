Cyril of Jerusalem (c. 313–386) wrote this homily for the faithful gathered to celebrate the feast of the Meeting of the Lord, also called the Presentation, when Christ was brought to the temple forty days after his birth. This homily is Cyril's, not Amphilochius of Iconium's sermon for the same feast. Cyril served as bishop of Jerusalem from around 350 and was known for his catechetical teaching. The homily was composed in Greek and later circulated in Coptic, Arabic, Ethiopic, and Slavonic translations.

The occasion is the feast of the Presentation, commemorating the infant Jesus being met in the temple by the righteous old man Simeon and the prophetess Anna. The homily opens with a call for all creation to rejoice at God appearing in Zion. It then proclaims the paradox of the infant Christ: a forty-day-old child who is also the eternal God, creator and sustainer of all things.

A reader will meet Simeon, who receives the child and speaks a long confession of Christ's divine titles and works, including words put into the mouth of Adam rejoicing at release from death. The preacher also cites Moses, David, Isaiah, Jeremiah, and Habakkuk as foretelling God's appearing, and draws on the Gospel of Luke for the account of the Presentation. Key terms include the saving plan, condescension, one in being, radiance, exact likeness, mercy seat, lamb, high priest, and virginity. Old Testament citations follow the Septuagint, so book names and Psalm numbers are converted to standard English usage. The homily closes by exhorting the congregation to meet Christ spiritually with pure souls and shining lamps, joining the angels, shepherds, magi, Mary, Joseph, Simeon, and Anna in the feast.
