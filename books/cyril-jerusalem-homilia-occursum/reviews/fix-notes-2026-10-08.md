## Finding 1 — Title: Συμεῶνα τὸν θεοδόχον → "Simeon the God-bearer" (MAJOR)

**Was (u01-open [0]):** "…regarding the meeting of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ and Simeon the God-bearer."

**Finding:** θεοδόχος (θεός + δέχομαι) = "God-receiver" — Simeon's epithet from Luke 2:28, where he receives the infant Christ into his arms. "God-bearer" renders a different word (θεοτόκος/θεοφόρος) and transfers Mary's epithet to Simeon.

**Now:** "…and Simeon the God-receiver."

**Why (Greek):** "…καὶ εἰς τὸν Συμεῶνα τὸν θεοδόχον" — δέχομαι = receive; the -δόχος suffix marks the receiver, as Luke 2:28 (εἰς τὰς ἀγκάλας αὐτοῦ, "into his arms") confirms.

## Finding 2 — u01-rem-close [0]: Ἀμβακοὺμ → "Amos"; wrong added reference (MAJOR)

**Was:** "Concerning this child, Amos proclaimed, saying: 'God will come from Teman,' that is, from the south (Amos 9:2)."

**Finding:** The Greek names Habakkuk (Ἀμβακοὺμ); the verse is Habakkuk 3:3 (ὁ θεὸς ἀπὸ Θαιμὰν ἥξει).

**Now:** "Concerning this child, Habakkuk proclaimed, saying: 'God will come from Teman,' that is, from the south (Habakkuk 3:3)."

**Why (Greek):** "Περὶ τούτου τοῦ παιδίου Ἀμβακοὺμ προεκήρυξε λέγων· ὁ θεὸς ἀπὸ Θαιμὰν ἥξει, τουτέστιν ἐκ τοῦ νότου" (source JSON confirms Ἀμβακοὺμ).

## Finding 3 — u01-open [1]: broken sentence at παστάδος (MAJOR — redaction artifact)

**Was:** "Today the heavenly Bridegroom arrives in the temple with the God-bearer [untranslated word]. Daughters of Jerusalem, go forth to meet him." (The live site served a literal `<redacted>` token at this point; the Air book file carried the mistranslation "procession" for παστάδος.)

**Finding:** παστάς = "bridal chamber" — the sentence needs the actual word.

**Now:** "Today the heavenly Bridegroom arrives in the temple with the God-bearer's bridal chamber. Daughters of Jerusalem, go forth to meet him."

**Why (Greek):** "σήμερον ὁ οὐράνιος νυμφίος μετὰ θεομήτορος παστάδος ἐν τῷ ναῷ παραγίνεται" — μετὰ + genitive phrase θεομήτορος παστάδος = "with the bridal chamber of the God-bearer" (Mary as the bridal chamber of the Word, a standard patristic image).
