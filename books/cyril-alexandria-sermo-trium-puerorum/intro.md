Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote this sermon for his congregation, whom he addresses as beloved. He was archbishop of Alexandria, and he delivered it on a timely occasion when the memory of good martyrs was brought forth, though the text does not name a particular memorial day. His purpose was to honor the three young men as martyrs and confessors of Christ even before the incarnation.

The sermon centers on Ananias, Azarias, and Misael, the three holy young men commemorated in the book of Daniel. Cyril presents them as royal children of the same race, and he states that the providence of the holy Trinity worked for their sake. He intends to explain their way of life, advocacy, alliance, and perfection for the Church of God.

A reader should know that Cyril encountered the testament of Daniel and wrote down the symbol and way of life of the holy children and their perfection in Christ. Key terms in this translation include martyrs, confessors, way of life, perfection, incarnation, bodily coming, intercession, prize, victorious, advocacy, alliance, and testament. Cyril closes by praying that the Church may flourish through their intercession and that parents and young people may imitate their virtue.
