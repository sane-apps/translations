## Finding 1 — omission: the Secundo/Tertio distinctions on existentia vs. subsistentia (MAJOR)

**Was:** the translation jumped from the end of the *primo* distinction ("…this singular humanity or this singular horseness do not subsist, but exist in their supposita and by their benefit.") directly to the numbered section "7." — the entire *Secundo* paragraph (the office/function distinction between existence and subsistence), the *Tertio* paragraph (the determining/determined distinction), and the formal definition of subsistence had no English counterpart.

**Now:** two new English entries inserted at that point, translating the Latin fresh:

1. "Secondly, existence and subsistence differ by reason of their charge or office; for the office of existence is to make it so that some thing actually be in the world, outside the power or potency of causes; but the office of subsistence is to make it so that a thing can in no way be in another — neither as a part in a whole, nor as an accident in a subject, nor as a nature or essence in that which has the essence."

2. "Thirdly, they differ as the determining and the determined; for just as essence is of itself indifferent to existing and not existing, so existence is of itself indifferent to being in another and to being through itself, that is, to subsisting: that is, existence is indifferent to the mode of existing by leaning upon another as sustaining it, and to the mode of existing through itself, without dependence on another that receives and sustains it. Subsistence, therefore, can be described thus: subsistence is a superadded mode of existence, from which a thing has it that it can exist not in another but through itself. Hence it is called by some the total perseity of existing."

**Why (Latin):** "Secundo, differunt existentia et subsistentia ratione muneris, seu officii sui; munus enim existentiae est efficere, ut res aliqua actu sit in mundo, extra causarum virtutem, aut potentiam: munus vero subsistentiae est, efficere, ut res nullo modo possit esse in alio, vel ut pars in toto, vel tanquam accidens in subjecto, vel tanquam natura seu essentia, in eo quod habet essentiam. Tertio, differunt ut determinans et determinatum; sicut enim essentia est ex se indifferens ad existere et non existere, ita existentia est ex se indifferens ad esse in alio, et esse per se, seu subsistere: h.e. existentia est indifferens ad modum existendi innitendo alteri ut sustentanti, et ad modum existendi per se, sine dependentia ab alio recipiente et sustentante. Subsistentia igitur sic describi potest: Subsistentia est modus existentiae superadditus, a quo res habet, quod non in alio, sed per se existere possit. Unde a quibusdam dicitur omnimoda perseitas existendi." — munus/officium is kept as "office/charge" per this file's convention (cf. entry [3]: "the office of essence is to constitute…"); "per se" is kept as "through itself" per the file's usage.
