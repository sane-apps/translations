Oecumenius wrote this fragment on the Letter to Philemon. It explains why Paul introduces his appeal for Onesimus by mentioning his own imprisonment.

The fragment comments on Philemon 1:1. Oecumenius observes that in many letters Paul places an unusual word that prepares for the argument he is about to introduce. Here, because Paul was about to make a request on behalf of Onesimus, he shows the credibility of his appeal by appealing to his own worth. That worth consists in his being in chains.

A reader will meet Onesimus, the person on whose behalf Paul is about to make a request. The key terms are rendered as worth, imprisonment, appeal, and credibility. Worth refers to Paul's worth, which consists in his being in chains. Imprisonment refers to Paul's being in chains, presented as the ground of his worth. Appeal refers to Paul's appeal or request on behalf of Onesimus. Credibility refers to the credibility or trustworthiness of Paul's appeal.
