Cyril, bishop of Alexandria, wrote this letter to Xystus, bishop of Rome. The surviving fragment does not state the occasion, but Cyril speaks in his own voice to declare what he believes.

Cyril denies that he ever held any view contrary to the truth or ever called the divine nature of the Word capable of suffering. He affirms that the nature of God is incapable of suffering, unchangeable, and invariable, even in the nature of humanity. He confesses one Christ in both natures and from both natures.

A reader should know that the divine nature is described as incapable of suffering, unchangeable, and invariable. The Word is the divine Son of God. Christ is the one subject who is in and from both the divine and human natures. No scripture is cited in this fragment.
