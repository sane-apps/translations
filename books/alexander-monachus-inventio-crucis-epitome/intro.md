Alexander Monachus (fl. 6th century) wrote this festal oration for Orthodox Christians celebrating the feast of the Exaltation of the Cross.

The work opens under Bishop Macarius of Jerusalem and Emperor Constantine, whose laws establish Christian peace until Arius disturbs the churches. Helena orders the demolition of the pagan shrine, the tomb and three crosses are found, and Part of the Cross and the nails are sent to Constantine, churches are commissioned, and the feast of the Cross is established. The oration closes with a long rhetorical praise of the Cross and a doxological prayer.

A reader should know that the author quotes Scripture in Greek, following Septuagint book names and Psalm numbering, so quotations are identified by modern English Bible references with Septuagint Psalm numbering noted where it differs from the Hebrew. The term "one in being" renders the Greek word for the Trinity's shared essence, used against Arius. "Confessors" are those who suffered for the faith under persecution.
