Alexander Monachus (c. 6th cent.) wrote this encomium for Orthodox Christian faithful celebrating the feast of the Exaltation of the Cross. The occasion is the anniversary of the finding and exaltation of the Cross, and the work retells how Constantine and Helena recovered the holy places before ending in praise of the Cross.

The oration opens under Bishop Macarius of Jerusalem and Emperor Constantine, whose laws establish Christian peace until Arius disturbs the churches. Constantine summons the Council of Nicaea, then sends his mother Helena to Jerusalem to search for the Cross and holy places. Part of the Cross and the nails are sent to Constantine, churches are commissioned, and the feast of the Cross is established.

A reader will meet Macarius, bishop of Jerusalem, who oversees the finding and identifies the Cross through the healing miracle, along with Constantine, Helena, Arius, Eusebius of Nicomedia, Eusebius of Caesarea, Hermonas, and Maximonas. The author quotes Scripture, introduced by Greek formulas meaning 'saying' and 'he says.' Key terms include Cross, life-giving, discovery, exaltation, dedication, one in being, orthodox, confessors, sign, thrice-holy, and idolatry.
