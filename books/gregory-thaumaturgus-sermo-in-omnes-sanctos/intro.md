Gregory Thaumaturgus ("the Wonderworker," c. 213–c. 270), bishop of Neocaesarea in Pontus, was revered across the early church — and, like all revered names, gathered writings he never wrote.

The Sermon on All Saints, preached for a martyrs' feast, moves from the speaker's unworthiness to Christ's despoiling of Hades. It is transmitted under Gregory's name.

This volume holds the opening: silence preferred, yet the martyrs' feast compelling speech, through Christ despoiling Hades.
