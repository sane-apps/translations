Gregory Thaumaturgus (c. 213–c. 270) was a third-century bishop of Neocaesarea in Pontus. He studied with Origen in Caesarea in Palestine for about five years before returning to his native city, where Phaedimus, bishop of Amasea, sought him out and ordained him bishop even though Gregory initially tried to hide from the responsibility. During the Decian persecution of 250–251 he withdrew with his community into the mountains, and after peace was restored he instituted liturgical celebrations honoring the martyrs who had died.

This homily was delivered at a festal assembly in honor of the holy martyrs, a crowded gathering of believers celebrating their victory over death and the devil. The preacher begins by wishing to remain silent because his speech is unpolished, but the splendor of the festival and the zeal of the congregation compel him to speak.

The sermon presents the martyrs as those who strengthen the weak and draw all ages to the celebration. They leap against death, laugh at swords, plunder the tyrant's rage, and make victory their own in their very fall, scattering their own limbs in order to bind their own souls. The climax is the victory of Christ over Hades and death, quoting the taunt, 'Where, O death, is your sting? Where, O Hades, your victory?' A reader should know that 'Hades' is kept as the name for the realm of the dead, not hell, and that the quoted taunt echoes Hosea 13:14 and 1 Corinthians 15:55. The phrase 'the infant age crawling here' means babies and young children brought to the festival.
