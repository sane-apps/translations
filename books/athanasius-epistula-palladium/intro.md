Athanasius of Alexandria (c. 293/298–373) wrote this letter as Bishop of Alexandria to Palladius, a presbyter. He wrote after receiving a letter from Palladius and after learning through Innocentius and Dianius that monks in Caesarea were grieved and resisting their bishop, Basil. His purpose was to commend Palladius's orthodoxy and to urge that the monks be persuaded to accept Basil.

The occasion is a dispute in Caesarea in Cappadocia, where monks were opposing Bishop Basil. Athanasius had already written to the monks himself, telling them to obey Basil as children obey a father. Now he asks Palladius to make the same point known to them, so that they may be persuaded, show themselves well-disposed toward their father, and preserve peace in the churches.

A reader will meet Athanasius addressing Palladius directly, with Innocentius and Dianius mentioned as beloved associates, and Basil described as a boast of the Church who contends for the truth and teaches those in need. The letter contains no explicit biblical citations, but it alludes to 1 Corinthians 9:22, where Paul says that to the weak he became weak. Key terms include orthodoxy, monks, truth, bishop, pastoral accommodation, and peace in the churches.
