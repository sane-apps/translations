Gregory Thaumaturgus (c. 213–c. 270) was a Christian bishop of Neocaesarea in Pontus, consecrated by Phaedimus of Amasea. The surviving fragments on Jeremiah come from Greek, Syriac, and Armenian catenae, and his theological writings remain incomplete.

The fragments open by applying Jeremiah's warning about a neighbor who speaks peace while harboring enmity to the hypocrisy of the scribes and Pharisees. Gregory argues that hypocrisy and hidden deceit are destructive, and that every good or evil done to a brother is done to God. He then addresses how Jeremiah can call Egypt, Edom, Ammon, and Moab circumcised alongside Judah. He explains that Egypt and Edom are called circumcised because they descend from Abraham through Ishmael and Esau, while Ammon and Moab descend from Lot, Abraham's nephew.

A reader will meet Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Esau, Edom, Lot, Ammon, Moab, Hagar, and Sarah as Gregory traces the genealogies behind Jeremiah's words. , Key terms include hypocrisy, circumcision, uncircumcised in heart, deceit, pride, empty glory, descendants of Abraham, pretense of righteousness, evil, and law of God. The author uses Septuagint book names and numbering, including Genesis and Jeremiah.
