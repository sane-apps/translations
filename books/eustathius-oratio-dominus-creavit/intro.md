Eustathius of Antioch (c. 320–337) wrote this treatise to defend the full divinity of the Word against those who read Proverbs 8:22, “The Lord created me,” as a statement about the divine Son. He was bishop of Antioch and a leading opponent of Arianism, and he composed this work during his years of literary polemic against the Arians, before his deposition and exile.

The occasion is the Arian claim that the Word was a created being. Eustathius argues that the Word is God from eternity with the Father, uncreated, and that the one born of the Virgin, placed under the law, crucified, and raised is the man whom the Word fashioned and wore. He distinguishes the impassible divine Spirit of Christ from the passible human temple, so that suffering and weakness belong to the man, not to the Godhead. He supports this from the Gospels, Paul, and the apostles’ preaching that God made the crucified Jesus Lord and Christ.

A reader should know that “Word” renders the Greek term for the divine Son eternally with the Father, “Wisdom” is identified with the incorporeal Spirit of Christ, and “temple” means the human body of Christ in which God dwelt. “Man” refers to the human nature the Word took on, and “image” is used for the Son as image of the Father and the man as image of the Son. The author cites the Gospels and Pauline epistles by traditional names such as Luke and Philippians, and Psalm numbering follows the Septuagint where applicable. The work survives only in fragments.
