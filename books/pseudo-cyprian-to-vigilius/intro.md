Pseudo-Cyprian (fl. c. 250) is the name on this letter to Vigilius. The writer calls himself Cels. It is not a letter of Cyprian of Carthage.

He writes against Jewish unbelief, then of Christ as the light shown to the Magi and to Simeon, and of the prophets who foretold him. He also speaks of a dispute between Jason and Papiscus, and of his translation of that dispute.

The close turns to Vigilius's election and to the bond of friendship. A reader meets Abraham, the Magi, Simeon, and the prophets. The citations use the old names of the biblical books.
