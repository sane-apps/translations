Olympiodorus of Alexandria, the deacon, is the name on this file. It is the short fragment on the line "Rejoice in that day," not his commentary on Job, Jeremiah, or Ecclesiastes, and not the pagan philosopher of the same name.

A healthy person takes pleasure even without luxury, because health itself wards off bad weather and meager fare. The same is said of the soul. Paul, in the midst of terrible things, rejoiced in his sufferings, not only for the kingdom of heaven, but because the suffering itself is a prize: to suffer something for the truth.

The apostles left the council of the Jews rejoicing, not only for the kingdom of heaven, but because they had been counted worthy to be dishonored for the name of Jesus. Solomon's words in Ecclesiastes are set beside the tears of the slandered. Christ tells those in this contest to rejoice and leap for joy.
