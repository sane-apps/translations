Athanasius of Alexandria (c. 293/298–373), Archbishop of Alexandria, wrote this letter to Maximus the Philosopher in answer to writings Maximus had sent. He dictates a concise reply based on what Maximus wrote so that the opponent may be persuaded to cease his evil speaking.

Athanasius explains his reluctance to answer shameless opponents by recalling the silence of Jesus before Pilate and Caiaphas, then argues that the crucified Christ is God the Word himself made flesh once for all from the Virgin Mary for our salvation, not a holy man entered by the Word. The reader meets Maximus, Thomas who touched Jesus and confessed him as Lord and God, Lazarus whom Jesus asked after and then raised, Mary, and Adam in whom death was spoken to all, while quoted words from Jesus, Thomas, the opponents and Scripture from Genesis are set out and answered, against both those who make the body a mere appearance and Paul of Samosata who made Christ a mere man, ending with the confession of Nicaea against every heresy and especially the Arian.

In this rendering the divine Son is called the Word of God, his full human bodily nature is called flesh, and other key words appear as cross, man, God, virgin, nature, salvation, heresy and Nicaea. Bible books are cited in modern form where the author used Septuagint names, with Psalms as in standard English Bibles and differences of numbering noted. Paul of Samosata is the teacher who said Christ was a mere man distinct from God the Word, and by sequence of nature the letter means ordinary human birth, which Athanasius denies of Christ.
