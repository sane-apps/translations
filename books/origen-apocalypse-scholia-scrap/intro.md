Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) is the named author of this work, though modern scholarship has shown that the Scholia on the Apocalypse were falsely attributed to him and were likely composed by Cassian the Sabaite in the sixth century. The text presents itself as Origen's own explanation of his activity as a Christian teacher in Alexandria.

The occasion described is personal and retrospective. The speaker says that because his reputation spread, both heretics and people trained in Greek learning, especially philosophy, came to him. He therefore decided to examine the doctrines of the heretics and what the philosophers claimed to teach about truth. He did this in imitation of Pantaenus, an earlier Christian teacher who had helped many and had considerable preparation in these matters.

A reader will meet Pantaenus and Heraclas. The speaker says he found Heraclas at the teacher of philosophical learning, and that Heraclas had already spent five years with that teacher before the speaker began to attend those lectures. Heraclas had exchanged the common clothing he was wearing for the philosopher's garb, and he still keeps that garb while continuing to study Greek books as much as he can.
