Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) wrote this commentary for Christian readers studying Paul's Epistle to the Romans. This English is the Greek catena excerpts of that commentary. In them he answers objections from readers who deny free will and from Jewish and Gentile readers who misunderstand Paul's statements about the law.

The work was motivated by a need to counter Gnostic readings of Paul and to refute the doctrine of natures, which taught that human destiny is fixed at birth. Origen defends the Christian doctrine of free will against deterministic interpretations of predestination, arguing that God's foreknowledge does not destroy human free will, since God foreknows future choices without causing them.

A reader will meet Paul, Abraham, Moses, David, Adam, Eve, Cain, Sarah, Isaac, Ishmael, Keturah, Balaam, Balak, Job, Joseph, Timothy, Aquila, Daniel, and Satan. Origen quotes Paul extensively and argues against objectors who raise questions about free will and the law. He distinguishes multiple senses of 'law' in Paul, especially the written Mosaic law, the natural law, and the spiritual law, to show that Paul does not contradict himself. He explains justification by faith as compatible with moral effort, using Abraham as the model of faith reckoned as righteousness. He treats the Christian's death to sin and life to God in Christ as a real transformation, while acknowledging the ongoing struggle with sin. Finally, he urges readers to overcome evil with good, interpreting the 'coals of fire' as true words that consume falsehood and turn an enemy into a friend. Origen uses the Septuagint book names and Psalm numbering, so his 'thirteenth Psalm' is Psalm 14 in modern English Bibles, and his 'fifty-second Psalm' is Psalm 53.
