Amphilochius of Iconium (c. 340–c. 395) was a fourth-century bishop of Iconium in Asia Minor. He was born into the Christian aristocracy of Cappadocia, studied law in Antioch under Libanius, and practised at Constantinople before retiring to a religious life. He became bishop of Iconium around 374, probably through the influence of Basil of Caesarea, and was active against the Messalians, Arians, and Macedonians.

This homily is a sermon on the Gospel account of the sinful woman who anointed the Lord with myrrh and on the Pharisee who hosted the meal. It survives in Greek and is listed as CPG 3234. The work is also known as Homily IV among Amphilochius's surviving works.

A reader meets the shepherd and the lost, the woman's tears as her confession, the Pharisee who does not understand her, and the contrast between the woman and Judas.
