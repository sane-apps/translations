This work is attributed to Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 310/320–403), a bishop in Cyprus who was born in Palestine and served the church for nearly forty years. He is best known for his writings against heresies, especially the Panarion. The attribution of this particular text is not certain, since it also circulated under other names, including Dorotheus, and in anonymous versions.

The work collects traditional accounts of the lives and deaths of the prophets. It gives details such as their tribal origins, birthplaces, and places of burial. The material survives in several forms, and a critical edition was published by Theodor Schermann in 1907 under the title Prophetarum vitae fabulosae.

The text is arranged as a series of short biographical notices rather than a continuous narrative.
