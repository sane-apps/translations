Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote this note on the falling away of the synagogue. The note is the renaming of Abram as Abraham, taken from a discourse called the Demonstration, on the oracles spoken to the patriarchs about the exchange of the Jews and the nations. It is not the festal letters, and it is not the dialogue with Nestorius.

He reads the letters of the name Abraham as three groupings. With the first letters the name says father. With the next it says son. Read the other way, it says Most High, and he takes Most High as a title of the Holy Spirit, because the Most High is God by nature.

The renaming was given to the patriarch by God. Cyril says the name had already outlined the doctrine of the holy Trinity, the Father and the Son and the holy Spirit, in agreement with the Savior's naming and numbering of them.
