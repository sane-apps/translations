This fragment comes from Epiphanius of Salamis, identified in the source as Epiphanius of Cyprus. It survives as a short doctrinal statement defending the equal glorification and worship of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, apparently excerpted from a longer work on the Trinity. The date and original audience are not stated in the surviving text.

Epiphanius argues that Father, Son, and Holy Spirit receive one single glorification and one single act of worship. He supports this by interpreting the seraphim's threefold cry of 'Holy, holy, holy' as glorifying the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit together.

The key terms are rendered here as worship, glorify, holy, Father, Son, Holy Spirit, and glorification. The fragment alludes to the seraphim's cry from Isaiah 6:3, using the Septuagint form 'Holy, holy, holy'.
