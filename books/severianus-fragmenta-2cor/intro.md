Severianus of Gabala (fl. c. 400) was bishop of Gabala in Syria and a well-known preacher in Constantinople from about 400 until 404. He became an enemy of John Chrysostom and took part in the Synod of the Oak that condemned him. His contemporaries regarded him as an effective preacher and a writer of biblical commentaries and sermons, though many of his works survive in Greek only among the writings of Chrysostom.

These fragments on 2 Corinthians come from catena manuscripts, including Vaticanus gr. 2217, where passages from various commentators were gathered around the biblical text. They are Severianus's fragments, not Didymus the Blind's fragments on the same letter. They are not a continuous commentary but a series of excerpts on selected verses. The surviving material reflects Severianus's work as a preacher and expositor of Paul, preserved in this indirect form rather than as a complete treatise.

The fragments address themes from Paul's second letter to the Corinthians, including the boasting of the gospel and the conscience, the contrast between the law as a ministry of death and the Spirit as a ministry of life, transformation from law to Spirit, the weight of glory and the two dwellings, death to the world and the new creation, Paul's exhortation and the Macedonian example, and the defense of apostolic integrity and the nature of Paul's afflictions.
