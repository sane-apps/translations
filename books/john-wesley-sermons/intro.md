John Wesley (1703–1791), Anglican clergyman and itinerant preacher, founded Methodism with his brother Charles and George Whitefield. A fellow of Lincoln College, Oxford, he dates his own evangelical conversion to the Aldersgate Street meeting of 24 May 1738, where, as his Journal records, hearing Luther preface read, I felt my heart strangely warmed.

His Sermons on Several Occasions — the standard forty-four — carry his own rule from the preface: I design plain truth for plain people. Written ad populum, to the bulk of mankind, and gathered in the 1771 edition of his Works and the 1872 edition, they stand among the doctrinal standards of Methodism beside his Notes on the New Testament.

This volume holds all forty-four sermons in modern English readings, each carrying the 1771 or 1872 check text beside the reading.
