Augustine of Hippo (354–430) delivered this treatise as a presbyter, at the request of the bishops assembled for a council of the whole North African episcopate at Hippo-Regius. He later issued it as a book at the desire of friends. The work offers a fuller treatment of the brief creed memorized by new Christians, to protect it against heretics.

Augustine explains the articles of the creed in order, beginning with God the Father almighty as creator of all things from nothing. He then treats the eternal generation of the Son, the incarnation, crucifixion, resurrection, ascension, and future judgment. He defends the full divinity and co-equality of the Holy Spirit within the one divine substance, and finally expounds the church, forgiveness of sins, and the resurrection of the body, urging that faith must be held before it can be understood.

Augustine quotes Scripture throughout and reports the claims of heretics, Gentiles, and philosophers. Key terms include 'creed' for the baptismal rule of faith, 'Word' for the eternal Son, 'only Son' for the only-begotten, 'earthly mission' for God's saving plan in time, 'substance' for the shared divine reality, and 'resurrection of the body' for the future rising of the flesh. Augustine uses Old Latin versions of the Bible, with Psalms numbered according to the Septuagint.
