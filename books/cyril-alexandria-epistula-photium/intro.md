Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote this letter to Photius, a presbyter of Alexandria. The surviving text is very short, and its occasion is not fully preserved.

The letter contains a single conditional argument. Cyril states that if two natures of different substance are blended into one mixture, then neither nature is preserved. Instead, both are confused together and destroyed. No opponents are named, and no scriptural citations appear in the surviving text.

A reader should know that the key terms are rendered here as natures, blended, mixture, of different substance, confused, and destroyed. The argument is hypothetical and does not explicitly identify a Christological error.
