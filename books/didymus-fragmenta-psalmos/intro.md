Didymus the Blind (c. 313–c. 398) was a theologian in Alexandria, Egypt, who led the city's catechetical school after being appointed by Bishop Athanasius. Blind since childhood and a layman throughout his life, he wrote extensively on the Bible and opposed Arianism. His works were later condemned in 553 for teachings associated with Origen, so most were not copied in the Middle Ages and were lost.

This English is his fragments on the Psalms, from the locked text in Patrologia Graeca 39. It is not the papyrus commentary found at Tura, which is a different work. A reader follows the fragments section by section, in the order the source gives them.

The section titles indicate the themes Didymus develops, such as the tree of life, divine judgment, the return of the soul, the cry of the Gentiles, and the spiritual inheritance of the saints. Didymus quotes the Bible at length and interprets it allegorically, finding symbols throughout the text.
