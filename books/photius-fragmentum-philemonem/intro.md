Photius of Constantinople (c. 810–c. 893) wrote this brief comment on Philemon 1:1.

Photius observes that in many letters Paul places an unusual word at the opening to prepare for the argument he is about to introduce. Here Paul intends to make a request on behalf of Onesimus, so he grounds that request in his own standing, which consists in being in chains.

A reader will meet Paul as the one making the appeal and Onesimus as the person on whose behalf Paul makes it. To follow the comment, a reader should know that the unusual word is preparatory, that Paul's standing is his imprisonment, and that the appeal is his request for Onesimus. The passage under discussion is Philemon 1:1.
