Severianus of Gabala (fl. c. 400) wrote these comments on selected verses of Paul's Letter to the Philippians. The fragments survive in chain commentaries that gathered excerpts from various interpreters.

Severianus addresses the question of Paul's audience and purpose. He argues that Paul wrote Philippians to clergy, not laity, and that his main aim was to instill humility in them, since pride especially afflicts those who live well. He explains Paul's appeals to the Philippians' faith and steadfastness as proof that his sufferings and defense of the gospel are not fruitless.

A reader will meet Paul, Timothy, the patriarch Abraham, and Euodia and Syntyche, the two people Paul urges to agree in Philippians 4:2. Severianus quotes Paul's words and argues against alternative readings. He interprets Paul's counting his former gains as loss and rubbish through an extended agricultural image: Abraham's faith is like the seed producing the root of the law, the stalk of the prophets, and the ear of grain that is Christ, . He also clarifies that Paul's forgetting what lies behind does not mean his past labors were evil, and he notes a tradition identifying Euodia as the jailer's wife and Syntyche as the man, likely the jailer himself. Readers should know that Bible books are cited by their traditional English names.
