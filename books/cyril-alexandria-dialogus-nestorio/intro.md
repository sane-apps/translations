Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444) wrote this dialogue with Nestorius. Two exchanges survive in this file. It is not the Dialogues on the Holy Trinity, and it is not the Thesaurus.

Nestorius says that Scripture speaks of the birth from the virgin as the birth of Christ, the Lord Jesus, and not as the birth of God the Word. He will say Christotokos, and he will not call the holy virgin Theotokos, that is, God-bearer. Cyril answers with Isaiah: the virgin shall conceive and bear a son.

The other exchange is on the sacrifice and the meal. Christ was offered once to bear the sins of many, and he offered himself for the people, not for himself. The question is whether the one who eats eats the divinity or the humanity. Cyril says he receives by faith the flesh of the life-giving Word, and that those who eat this bread will not taste death.
