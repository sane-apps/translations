This short dialogue is printed among the works of Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444), in PG 76, cols. 249-253. Cyril and Nestorius answer each other with verses of Scripture.

Nestorius says that when Scripture speaks of Christ's birth from the Virgin, it never uses the name "God", only "Christ", "Lord" or "Jesus". So he will call the holy Virgin Christ-bearer (Christotokos), but not God-bearer (Theotokos). Cyril answers with Isaiah: "Look, the virgin will conceive and bear a son, and they will call his name Emmanuel," which is translated "God with us." The two then argue whether the Son is of one substance with his mother, and whether the natures of Christ may be divided.

The last exchange is on Christ's sacrifice and the Eucharist. Nestorius asks Cyril what he eats, the divinity or the humanity. Cyril answers that he receives by faith the life-giving flesh of the life-giving Word, and that those who eat of this bread will never taste death. The text breaks off here, in the middle of Cyril's reply.
