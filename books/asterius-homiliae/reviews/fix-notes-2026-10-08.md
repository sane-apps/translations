## Finding M-1 — ἀκτήμων ("non-possessor") → "steward" (MAJOR)

**Was:** "…and that also is wealth, which he holds as a steward."

**Finding:** ἀκτήμων = "one without possessions/property." The point is that the poor man's little *is* wealth precisely because he does not possess; "steward" (one entrusted with another's property) is the 180° opposite.

**Now:** "…and that too is wealth, which he holds as one who owns nothing."

**Why (Greek):** ὃ ἔχει ἀκτήμων — "which he, owning nothing, has." ἀκτήμων (α-privative + κτῆμα, "possession") = the property-less man.

## Finding M-2 — κανθήλιος ("pack-ass") → "basket" (MAJOR)

**Was:** "For just as a chariot is to the rich man, so a humble basket is to the poor man."

**Finding:** κανθήλιος = a pack-ass/donkey. The rich man's chariot is paralleled by the poor man's donkey; "basket" destroys the vehicle/animal parallel.

**Now:** "For just as a chariot is to the rich man, so a cheap pack-ass is to the poor man."

**Why (Greek):** ὁ εὐτελὴς κανθήλιος — "the cheap pack-ass." εὐτελής = cheap, of small worth; κανθήλιος = donkey/pack-ass.
