Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–c. 254) wrote this commentary on Romans. It is the commentary Rufinus translated, not the catena of fragments on Romans.

It begins with why Romans is the hardest letter, and with Paul as servant and son. It then takes up the Jew and the Greek, circumcision of the heart, Abraham justified by faith, and Christ as the propitiation in his blood.

The later books take up Adam and Christ, baptism into Christ's death, the other law in the members, life in the Spirit, Israel and mercy, and the word that is near you. The citations use the old names of the biblical books.
