Didymus the Blind (c. 313–c. 398) was a Christian teacher in Alexandria, Egypt. Blind since childhood and a layman all his life, he was appointed by Bishop Athanasius to head the catechetical school of Alexandria, where he taught for about half a century. He was a leading opponent of Arianism and a faithful follower of Origen, and many of his writings were later lost because his works, though not his person, were condemned by the Second Council of Constantinople in 553.

This work is a set of fragments from Didymus's comments on the Second Letter to the Corinthians. It is not a continuous commentary but a collection of explanations tied to passages in the letter. The fragments address questions raised by Paul's text and offer doctrinal and moral interpretation of the apostle's words.

A reader will meet Paul's words quoted and discussed, along with Didymus's explanations of such topics as divine blessings and afflictions, the sincerity of ministry and divine knowledge, the dual nature of Christ's presence, the meaning of 'many' and the living letter, the glory of the ministry of the Spirit, perishing and the light of truth, the two dwellings, the new creation and the dwelling of Christ, purification and textual variants of 2 Corinthians 7, the joy of obedience and the equality of giving, spiritual warfare against sophisticated falsehood, election and deception, the deception of Manichaean light and Paul's visions, the limits of speech and the nature of visions, and the certainty of witness and apostolic blessing. To follow the fragments, a reader should know that the Second Letter to the Corinthians is referred to as 2 Corinthians.
