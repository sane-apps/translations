Eustathius of Antioch (c. 320–337) wrote this treatise on the inscriptions of the Psalms of Ascents.

The surviving fragments explain the titles of the Psalms of Ascents. Eustathius argues that the Father does not receive acquired glory, since he is perfect, infinite, incomprehensible, and without need of beauty and every seemliness. Nor does the Word, who is God begotten from the Father and through whom all created things were made.

A reader will meet the Father, the Word, and the man of Christ. The man of Christ is raised from the dead, exalted, and glorified, while his enemies openly took up shame. The work comments on the Psalms of Ascents in the Septuagint numbering. Key terms include acquired glory, the man of Christ, the Word, temple, raised, and his enemies.
