Didymus the Blind (c. 313–c. 398) wrote this dialogue. He was a Christian teacher in Alexandria, blind from childhood, and a follower of Origen. He wrote it to defend the church's teaching against Montanist claims that Montanus, Priscilla, and Maximilla were inspired prophets and that the Paraclete had come in Montanus.

The dialogue stages a debate between an unnamed Montanist and an unnamed Orthodox Christian. The Montanist argues that Paul's promise of 'the perfect' was fulfilled in Montanus as the Paraclete. The Orthodox speaker answers that 'the perfect' refers to the future vision of God face to face, when faith and partial knowledge will cease. He then shows from Scripture that the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are distinct persons sharing one nature, against Montanus's claim to be all three. He argues that the Spirit dwelt in the apostles and all believers as the pledge of promised goods, not that the apostles lacked the Spirit. Finally he argues that while women may prophesy, they may not teach or write books in their own name, which is why Priscilla and Maximilla are rejected as false prophetesses.

A reader should know that 'the first of Kingdoms' is the Greek Septuagint title for 1 Samuel. Priscilla and Maximilla were Montanist prophetesses whose writings the Orthodox speaker rejects. The Orthodox speaker calls Montanus a false prophet and former priest of Apollo, meaning a pagan priest. The phrase 'a vessel of election' comes from Acts 9:15 and refers to Paul as God's chosen instrument.
