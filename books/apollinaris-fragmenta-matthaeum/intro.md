Apollinaris of Laodicea (c. 310–c. 390) was bishop of Laodicea in Syria and a prominent theologian of the fourth century. He wrote these comments on selected passages of Matthew's Gospel for readers who needed help with difficult verses and with rival interpretations. His aim was to defend the reality of Christ's incarnation, passion, and resurrection, and to show how the Law and the prophets are fulfilled in Christ.

The fragments survive because later writers quoted them, often to argue against Apollinaris's distinctive teaching about Christ. In these comments he answers exegetical questions and corrects opponents, including Valentinians, Marcionites, and Manichaeans, who spiritualized or denied Christ's bodily life. He also cites the apostle Paul, the evangelists Mark, Luke, and John, and the early writer Papias, whose account of Judas's death he records.

A reader will meet Joseph, Mary, John the Baptist, Herod, Peter, Judas, and many Old Testament figures such as Moses, Elijah, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, David, and Abraham. Apollinaris argues that Joseph is rightly called Christ's father by grace and by flesh, that the Magi prefigure the Gentile church, and that the Law's types are fulfilled in Christ. Key terms include flesh, Spirit, type, grace, Law, righteousness, salvation, resurrection, faith, church, mystery, Son of Man, and coming. Old Testament books are cited by their Greek names, and Psalms follow the numbering of the Septuagint.
