## Finding 1 — ch 2 [33]: inverted clause (MAJOR)

**Was:** "So do you not refute its lack of perception?"

**Finding:** The context argues idols are dead matter: a human with perception would not endure such punishment; the stone endures it *because it has no perception*. The rhetorical question means "do you not thereby refute [the claim of] its perception" — the endurance test exposes that it has none. Inserting "lack of" inverts the meaning, asking the reader to prove the stone *does* perceive.

**Now:** "So do you not thereby refute its [claim to] perception?"

**Why (Greek):** «οὐκ οὖν τὴν αἴσθησιν αὐτοῦ ἐλέγχετε;» — ἐλέγχειν τὴν αἴσθησιν = "to refute its perception" (i.e., refute the claim that it perceives); no "lack of" (ἀναισθησία) in the Greek.
